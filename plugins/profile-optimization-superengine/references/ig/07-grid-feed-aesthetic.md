# 07 ... Grid / Feed Aesthetic

> Scope: The Instagram grid as a first-impression landing page - specifically the first 9 posts, the current grid preview crop, layout styles, and the cohesion rules that make a coach's profile look like a system instead of a scrapbook. This is IG-specific; Facebook has no grid equivalent.

---

## Why This Matters

When cold traffic clicks through from a Reel, they land on your profile and the first thing they see is your grid, not a single post. Treating it like a random mood board, or clinging to the old 2010s hyper-perfect square aesthetic, actively repels potential coaching clients. Modern B2C consumers run on "beauty skepticism" and want authenticity - but that authenticity still needs to sit inside a structured, high-converting digital storefront. Random and messy reads as unprofessional. Perfectly curated and robotic reads as fake. The grid has to hit both: intentional and human.

## The First 9 Posts - Your Landing Page

The first 9 posts a visitor sees determine whether 73% of those visitors choose to follow you. Treat these 9 slots as a mini sales funnel that answers the prospect's core objections in order, top to bottom:

| Row | Slot | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **Top (Awareness)** | Post 1 | State your core promise/result |
| | Post 2 | Identify your exact target audience |
| | Post 3 | Establish your unique positioning |
| **Middle (Authority & Proof)** | Post 4 | Best educational carousel |
| | Post 5 | Undeniable social proof - client transformation or video testimonial |
| | Post 6 | Clearly outline your coaching offer |
| **Bottom (Conversion)** | Post 7 | Behind-the-scenes/values |
| | Post 8 | Common FAQ or objection handled |
| | Post 9 | Hard CTA - DM a keyword or book a call |

**What this means for you:** don't just post in chronological order and hope. Before you post something new, ask which of these 9 jobs it's doing. If your last 9 posts are all "educational carousel," you have no proof, no positioning, and no CTA doing work on your landing page.

## The Grid Preview Crop: 3:4 vs. 4:5

This is the part coaches get wrong most often, so read both numbers carefully - they're not the same thing and both are correct for different jobs.

| Where | Ratio | Pixels | What it's for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Profile grid preview** | 3:4 vertical | 1012 x 1350 | How Instagram *displays* your post thumbnail on the grid - not a size you design at |
| **Feed post / carousel design** | 4:5 vertical | 1080 x 1350 | The ratio you actually *design and upload* your posts and carousels in |
| **Reels** | 9:16 full vertical | 1080 x 1920 | The ratio you shoot and upload Reels in |

The grid no longer shows posts as 1:1 squares. It renders a 3:4 vertical crop. That means both your 4:5 feed posts and your 9:16 Reels get cropped down to 3:4 when someone is browsing your grid. Vertical posts get 28% more engagement than square ones by taking up more screen real estate, so stop designing squares entirely.

**The Safe Zone Rule:** because your 4:5 posts and 9:16 Reels both get chopped to 3:4 on the grid, keep every essential element (headline text, faces, logos) centered within the middle 80% width of the canvas. Anything near the left/right edges risks getting cropped off in the grid view. For Reels specifically, leave about 250px of padding top and bottom so the platform's UI (captions, buttons) doesn't cover your text.

## Grid Layout Styles

| Style | How it works | When to use it |
|---|---|---|
| **Column Pillars** | Each vertical column = one content theme (e.g. left = educational carousels, middle = social proof/transformations, right = offers/CTAs) | Structured coaches selling high-ticket expertise who want a visibly balanced feed |
| **Row-by-Row (Sandwich Method)** | Each horizontal row of 3 tells a mini-story, alternating Educational (carousel) → Connection/Lifestyle (photo) → Promotional (offer) | Program launches, or coaches who want the grid to guide a visitor from learning → liking → buying |
| **Checkerboard** | Alternating post formats/visual styles - e.g. a text-heavy graphic next to a human-focused photo or Reel cover | Personal brands that want visual rhythm and variety without the rigidity of strict columns |

Pick one and stick with it. Switching layout logic every few posts undoes the "intentional system" signal you're going for.

## Cohesion Rules

The 2026 wellness visual trend is "Raw Craftsmanship" - unfiltered, human imagery paired with minimalist design elements. Authentic, not sterile. Consistent, not templated to death.

- **Color palette:** lock in 3 to 5 colors total - 2 primary brand colors, 2 accent colors, neutral backgrounds - and don't deviate. Soft pastels and earth tones with natural lighting are currently high-performing for wellness brands.
- **Typography:** restrict graphics to exactly 1 headline font and 1 body font (or one font family across different weights). Text must be high-contrast and readable on a tiny mobile screen.
- **Editing style:** apply the same editing preset, filter, color temperature, and contrast to every photo and video cover you post. That sameness is what reads as "brand" to a scrolling visitor.
- **Cover system:** use branded template covers for educational carousels and Reels. This lets you post messy, real, behind-the-scenes coaching footage while the grid preview still looks organized and intentional.
- **Skip the watermark habit:** don't slap your logo on every single post. A strong, cohesive design system is recognizable without a logo stamped on it.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It Fails |
|---|---|
| Designing posts as 1:1 squares | Outdated crop, loses the 28% engagement bump vertical posts get, gets cropped oddly on the grid |
| Treating the grid as chronological, not strategic | First 9 posts miss the awareness → proof → conversion funnel, so cold traffic has no reason to follow |
| No CTA in the first 9 | Visitor sees good content but is never told what to do next |
| Important text/faces near the canvas edges | Gets chopped off when the 4:5 post or 9:16 Reel crops down to 3:4 on the grid |
| Mixing 4+ colors with no locked palette | Grid looks like a mood board instead of a brand |
| Switching layout style (columns vs. rows vs. checkerboard) every few posts | Undoes the "intentional system" signal the grid is supposed to send |
| Different filter/editing look on every post | Kills visual cohesion, grid reads as random instead of curated |
| Logo watermark on every single post | Reads as insecure branding, not needed if the design system is already cohesive |
