# 03 ... Profile Photo

> Scope: Instagram profile photo (the circular avatar) - the single highest-converting trust signal on a coach's profile. Instagram has no cover photo, so this is the whole visual first impression.

---

## Why This Matters

Your profile photo has to work as a high-converting trust signal, not decoration. If you're building a personal coaching brand, this is a clear photo of you... not a business logo. Cold traffic decides whether you're a real person worth trusting in about a second. This image is doing that job.

## Dimensions & Format

| Attribute | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Upload size** | 320 x 320 px |
| **Crop shape** | 1:1 circle - Instagram auto-crops and renders your upload as a circle |
| **Face safe zone** | Center your face within the inner 60-70% radius so the circular crop doesn't cut it off |
| **Mobile render** | Renders very small on mobile screens (general industry practice puts it around ~110px - this specific number isn't sourced, but the takeaway holds: optimize like it'll be tiny) |

**What this means for you:** design for a thumbnail, not a portrait. If a detail isn't obvious at postage-stamp size, it's wasted effort.

## Framing & Proportion

- **Face first.** Use a close-up, high-quality headshot or selfie where your face is entirely clear and visible. This is not the place for a full-body shot.
- **Avoid "distance pics."** Full-body or far-away shots lose your features entirely once the image shrinks down - you become an unrecognizable blob in the feed.
- **Center the face** inside that inner 60-70% radius so nothing important lands in the part the circle crop trims off.

## Contrast, Background, and Lighting

- **Background:** Solid color background. Eliminate extra props or distracting background elements - anything that competes with your face is a liability at this size.
- **Contrast:** Use highly contrasting colors so the small image pops in the feed. Example: white or teal clothing against a bright orange wall creates strong visual contrast.
- **Lighting:** Use as much natural light as possible so the image reads bright and professional.

## Brand Alignment - What to Show

- **Approachable, not corporate.** Look professional, but avoid the stiff, suit-and-tie LinkedIn headshot look. That's the wrong register for B2C fitness/health/wellness coaching.
- **Let your niche show.** Your personality and coaching niche should be visible in the photo. If your daily uniform as a fitness coach is a hoodie, wear the hoodie. If you're a health or nutrition coach, a photo of you happily sipping a smoothie can work, as long as it matches your brand identity and doesn't clutter the frame.

## Filters and What to Avoid

Authenticity is non-negotiable for converting cold B2C traffic. If they sense fake, trust is gone before the DM even starts.

- **No artificial filters.** Skip Snapchat filters or heavy Instagram filters that alter your facial features. The photo needs to look natural.
- **No digital fakes.** Never use an NFT or an AI-generated image as your profile photo.
- **Don't hide your face.** No side-profile shots, no sunglasses that obscure your eyes.
- **Don't clutter with extra people.** Skip multiple people in the photo unless it's strategic (e.g., you and your spouse if you specifically run a couples' coaching business).
- **No text overlays.** Never place text or tiny logos over the image - they become unreadable once the photo scales down in the app.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It Fails |
|---|---|
| Full-body or "distance" shot | Face is unrecognizable at circle-crop thumbnail size |
| Business logo instead of your face | Kills the 1:1 personal trust signal cold B2C traffic needs |
| Busy or cluttered background | Competes with your face, hurts the "pop" at small size |
| Heavy filters or AI-generated image | Reads as inauthentic, erodes trust before contact |
| Sunglasses or side-profile | Hides your eyes, the strongest trust cue in the photo |
| Text or logo overlay | Unreadable at render size, looks unprofessional |
| Stiff, corporate suit-and-tie look | Wrong register for approachable B2C coaching brand |
| Multiple people (no strategic reason) | Dilutes focus, confuses who the coach actually is |
