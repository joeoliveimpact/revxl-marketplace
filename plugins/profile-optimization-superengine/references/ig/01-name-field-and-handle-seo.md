# 01 ... Name Field + @Handle SEO

> **Scope:** Instagram's Name field (the bold, searchable line above your bio) and your @username/handle ... the two fields Instagram's search algorithm reads to decide who shows up for a cold search.

---

## Character Limit & Field Rules

| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| **Name field hard cap** | 64 characters (Instagram raised this from the old 30 ... plenty of sources still cite 30, they are stale) |
| **Name field uniqueness** | Not required ... you can use the exact same keywords as thousands of other coaches |
| **Name field font rule** | Plain text only. Cursive/bold fonts from third-party generator apps cannot be read or indexed by IG search |
| **Username uniqueness** | Required ... one-of-a-kind across all of Instagram |
| **Search weighting** | Instagram search prioritizes the Name field over the username for topic-based, cold discovery |

In 2026 Instagram search behaves like a real search engine. It treats your Name field as word-for-word searchable text. Your username, by contrast, functions as your unique brand identifier, not a keyword net.

---

## The Keyword Formula (Name Field)

```
[Your First Name] | [Your Specific Niche / Target Audience / Location]
```

Every one of the 64 characters is premium SEO real estate. Use your real first name, then a separator, then the exact niche or audience a prospect would type into the IG search bar. You do not have to fill all 64 ... clarity beats cramming ... but you now have room for a fuller keyword phrase than the old 30-char cap allowed.

### Worked Examples (well under the 64-char cap)

| # | Name Field | Chars | What It Targets |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `Sarah \| Fat Loss for Moms` | 25 | Specific demographic + specific outcome |
| 2 | `David • Men's Gut Health` | 24 | Specific niche problem |
| 3 | `Elena \| Miami Yoga Coach` | 24 | Localized B2C market (city + modality) |

Any separator works (`|`, `•`, `-`) as long as the text on both sides stays plain, readable, and keyword-true to what your prospect would search.

### Outdated Tactic to Flag

Using the Name field to just restate your full name (`John Smith`) or a cute, meaningless phrase wastes the single highest-leverage SEO slot on your profile. If your niche or profession isn't in the Name field, you are actively hiding your business from Instagram's search results.

---

## The @Handle: Rules for 2026

Your username's job is brand recall and trust, not keyword stuffing ... because it must be unique, broad keyword handles are usually already claimed (if someone owns `@FatLossCoach`, no one else can).

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| **Keep it clean and readable** | Must be simple to spell and instantly understandable |
| **Ban underscores, hyphens, numbers** | These signal a spam or casual personal account, not a professional coaching business |
| **Match your other channels** | Same handle across website, Facebook, YouTube, TikTok builds one recognizable brand ecosystem |
| **Choose brand vs. name deliberately** | Personal brand → your real name (`@JeremyMoser`). Niche-hinting brand → short variation (`@NickTrades`, `@CoachSarah`) |

### Handle Examples

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Optimal** | `@CoachDavid`, `@SarahWellness`, `@ElenaPilates` |
| **Poor / Outdated** | `@sarah_smash_fitness_123` ... too long, underscores + numbers, impossible to say out loud on a podcast or in passing |

---

## How Instagram Search Uses Both Fields

Instagram routes two different kinds of traffic through two different fields:

- **Warm/known search** (someone who already knows you and types your name or brand) → pulls up your **@username**.
- **Cold/discovery search** (a problem-aware stranger typing "how to fix my gut" or "online fitness coach") → Instagram scans the **Name field** to match those exact phrases.

Net effect: your username earns trust from people who already know you exist. Your Name field is what gets you found by people who don't ... yet.

---

## Step-by-Step Framework

### Step 1 ... Lock the Name Field Formula
Write `[First Name] | [Niche/Audience/Location]` and count characters. Stay at or under 64. Use plain text ... no font generators.

### Step 2 ... Stress-Test the Keyword
Ask: "Would my ideal client literally type this phrase into the IG search bar?" If not, swap it for the phrase they would type (outcome, problem, or demographic ... not a job title).

### Step 3 ... Set the Handle for Trust, Not Keywords
Pick your real name or a short brand-hinting variation. Strip any underscores, hyphens, or numbers before claiming it.

### Step 4 ... Check Omnichannel Consistency
Confirm the same (or closest available) handle is used on your website and other platforms so the brand reads as one entity everywhere.

---

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It Fails |
|---|---|
| Name field = full name only | Wastes the field's SEO value; hides your niche from cold search |
| Cute/meaningless phrase in Name field | No keyword match, so it's invisible to problem-aware search |
| Cursive/bold fonts in Name field | IG search cannot read or index stylized Unicode fonts |
| Underscores, hyphens, or numbers in handle | Reads as spam/casual account, not a professional coaching business |
| Handle inconsistent across platforms | Fragments brand recall and makes you harder to find/verify off-platform |
| Treating username as the SEO field | Search weights the Name field far more heavily for cold discovery |
