## What a lead magnet is (and its job)

A lead magnet is a **complete solution to a narrow problem**, offered free or low-cost to identify who is genuinely interested in your services (Hormozi, *$100M Leads*).

Its job is **not** to collect random emails. It is to **attract and pre-qualify future paying clients** by delivering a "quick win" that builds trust before any money changes hands. Two reasons this matters:
- **Only ~5% of your market is ready to buy right now.** The lead magnet captures the other 95% who need time, so you can nurture them.
- **A prospect who pays with their time is more likely to pay with their money later.** Consumption is a commitment signal.

Every lead magnet solves one narrow problem AND reveals the next problem — the one your core offer solves. That bridge is the whole point.

---

## The three types

Pick the type by how your core offer works.

**1. Reveal Their Problem (diagnosis).** Make the prospect aware of a problem they didn't know they had, or quantify one they underestimate.
- *Best when:* the problem gets worse the longer they wait.
- *Examples:* website speed audit, posture screening, financial/tax audit, "what's my house worth."

**2. Samples & Trials.** Give full-but-brief access to the core offer — limit duration, uses, or quantity.
- *Best when:* the core offer is a recurring solution to a recurring problem.
- *Examples:* one free adjustment for immediate relief, a 7-day trial, Costco-style samples.

**3. One Step of a Multi-Step Process.** Give away the first step of a complex solution for free.
- *Best when:* the core offer solves a genuinely complex challenge.
- *Examples:* the first coat of sealant free (reveals two more are needed), a spreadsheet/template that organizes data (reveals the effort of executing the full plan).

Each type solves the narrow problem AND exposes the next one. If solving your free problem doesn't naturally reveal a problem your core offer solves, you picked the wrong problem.

## The 7-step creation process

1. **Pick the problem + who it's for.** Narrow and meaningful. Governed by the Problem-Solution cycle: every solved problem reveals a new one. Choose a problem whose *next* problem is what your core offer solves.
2. **Choose how to solve it.** Which of the three types is the right vehicle.
3. **Choose how to deliver it.** Four formats: **Software** (calculators, assessments, spreadsheets), **Information** (mini-course, guide, interview), **Services** (done-for-you audit/work), **Physical** (book, chart). (3 types × 4 formats = up to 12 magnet shapes.)
4. **Test what to name it.** Naming/headline/packaging drives engagement more than the content itself — can 2×/3×/10× opt-ins. Test headline first, then image, then subheadline. (See `hooks-and-titles.md`.)
5. **Make it easy to consume.** People default to least effort. Deliver in multiple formats (audio/video/text/interactive) so they consume it whichever way is easiest. (The principle is *low-effort / multi-format*, not a literal "<10 minute" rule.)
6. **Make it darn good.** The market judges your paid offer by your free material. "Give away the secrets, sell the implementation" (below). Make it good enough that you *could* charge for it.
7. **Make it easy to say they want more.** One clear, direct CTA telling them exactly what to do next. Pair with a real reason to act now — ethical scarcity (calendar/cohort limits) or urgency (limited-time bonus), never fake countdowns.

## Give away the secrets, sell the implementation

The cornerstone rule. Owners who publish shallow, low-value content signal that their paid offer is equally shallow. Instead: **give away your highest-value secrets free.** If someone can consume your free material and get a real transformation on their own, the odds they hire you to go further are *high*.

You teach the **what** and the **why** for free. Your core offer sells the **how**, the **who**, and the **software/labor/support** — the time, effort, and execution the secrets require.

## The economics (why this works mathematically)

**Perceived value of the lead magnet must exceed the price of your core offer** before they buy. That erases risk and makes the purchase feel obvious.

A high-quality free magnet also lowers **Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC = total spend ÷ new paying clients):**
- Advertise a $10k service straight to cold traffic → high skepticism → maybe $1,000 ad spend per booked call → close 1-in-3 → **CAC $3,000**.
- Advertise an irresistible free magnet (hard cost ~$25) → low friction → ad-spend-per-call drops to ~$75 → +$25 fulfillment = **~$100 to get a warm, qualified prospect on a call.** Same close rate → dramatically lower CAC, higher margin.

The lead magnet isn't a cost center — it's what makes paid acquisition profitable.

## Naming & headline (snap-decision layer)

~5× more people read the headline than the body — the opt-in decision is made almost entirely on the title. Promise a clear, specific, desirable outcome.

Proven formulas:
- **Number + Outcome + Timeframe** — "3 Emails That Turn Cold Leads Into Clients in 24 Hours"
- **How to [outcome] without [obstacle]** — "How to Build a Funnel Without Hiring a Copywriter"
- **The [adjective] [thing] That [result]** — "The Lazy Funnel Template That Converts Like Crazy"
- **X Mistakes Keeping You From [outcome]** — "4 Mistakes Keeping Your Business Under $1M"

Lead with the value prop in the **first ~50 characters** (58%+ of traffic is mobile; longer titles truncate).
