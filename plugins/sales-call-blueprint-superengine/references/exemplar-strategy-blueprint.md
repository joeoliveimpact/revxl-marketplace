## 🎯 CALL STRUCTURE (full strategy call — RFPDP)

### RAPPORT (< 30 sec)
**Their Energy:** Direct, competitive, a little guarded. Match it — be warm but get to the point fast. Stiff or hyper-friendly both lose him.
**Opening Line:** "Marcus — good to finally talk. I watched you book yourself off that video, so I'll assume you're not here to waste either of our time. Before we dig in, what made you actually pull the trigger and book?"
**⚠️ AVOID:** Small talk that runs long, or anything that sounds like a scripted "sales call" open. He'll smell it.

### FRAME (< 30 sec)
"Here's how I run these so it's straight: I'm going to ask you real questions about where your business actually is — not a pitch in disguise — and if I think what we do can get you off this plateau, I'll show you exactly how and what it costs. If it's not a fit, I'll tell you that too and point you somewhere better. Fair?"
**Confidence Anchor:** He booked himself and liked your no-pitch DMs — he already expects directness. Reward it.

### PAIN (10 sec)
**Opening Question:** "You said you've hit a ceiling you can't break through — walk me through what a normal day actually looks like right now."
**Expected Response:** The manual grind — hours in DMs, posting daily, onboarding/coaching clients himself, no system, mentally fried, revenue flat.
**Follow-Ups:** (1) "What part of that drains you the most?" (2) "How long's it been flat — and what have you tried to change it?"

### DISCOVERY (12–25 min) — RANKED FOR THIS PROSPECT
Ranked by Marcus's situation. Deepen every one; do not skip. Items he already answered in the DMs, confirm and go deeper rather than re-ask.

**1. CURRENT SITUATION — do this first**
- *Why first:* You need the real numbers before anything else means anything, and his pain lives here.
- **Questions:** "Where's revenue at on a normal month?" · "How many clients, and how are you getting them right now?" · "How many hours a week are you actually working *in* the business?"
- **Listening for:** A repeatable lead source (there won't be one — it's all manual DMs) and the hours-to-revenue ratio that proves he's the bottleneck. *Red flag:* if he can't name his numbers, he's less ready than he looks.
- **Landmine:** Don't pile on about how broken the manual approach is — he knows. Acknowledge the effort ("doing 2–3 hours of DMs a day takes discipline") before naming the gap.

**2. FINANCES / RESOURCEFULNESS — address proactively, don't wait for the close**
- *Why #2:* The partner variable and the burned-on-a-course history both live near money. Surface capacity early so the price drop isn't the first time money comes up.
- **Questions:** "If we found the right system to break this plateau, are you in a position to invest in it — or is that something you'd need to figure out?" · "When you invested in that course last year, how'd you decide to do it?"
- **Listening for:** Savings/runway (he mentioned ~$15k) AND *how* he frames spending — investment vs. risk. *Red flag:* "I'd have to really stretch" = either desperation or a soft no; dig.
- **Landmine:** Don't get specific on price here — just confirm capacity and the decision process. Specifics come at the price drop.
- **Sub-scenario — savings present but he hesitates:** "It's not whether the money exists, it's whether you believe it'll come back. That's the right question — let's make sure by the end of this you can actually see the return, not just hope for it."

**3. PAST ATTEMPTS — critical for trust**
- *Why #3:* The $2k course wound is the single biggest objection pre-loaded. Get it on the table on YOUR terms before it becomes "how is this different?"
- **Questions:** "Tell me about that course — what'd it actually deliver vs. what you hoped?" · "What was missing?"
- **Listening for:** "Advice not implementation," "recycled," "left me to do it myself." That's your exact differentiation. *Red flag:* if he blames only himself, he may repeat the pattern — make sure the system removes that risk.
- **Landmine:** Don't trash the other coach by name — agree with his *diagnosis* ("advice without a system is why most of these don't work") and position yourself as the opposite.

**4. GUARANTEE / TRUST — address the elephant**
- *Why #4:* He's testing you for hype. Name the trust question before he has to.
- **Questions:** "After that course, why would you believe this is different?" (ask it FOR him) · "What would you need to see today to feel sure?"
- **Listening for:** Whether proof (case study, the actual system on screen) moves him, or whether he needs a risk-reversal. *Red flag:* if nothing would convince him today, he's not closing today — find out now.
- **Landmine:** Don't over-promise to win the moment. One inflated claim and you're coach #2.

**5. GOALS — paint the future state**
- *Why #5:* His $30k/mo + "propose to Sara" goal is the emotional close anchor. Get him to say it out loud and feel it.
- **Questions:** "If this works, what does your business — and your life — look like 12 months from now?" · "Why that number? What does it unlock?"
- **Listening for:** The identity goal under the revenue (being the best again, providing for Sara, freedom from the grind). *Red flag:* vague goals = weak urgency; sharpen them.
- **Landmine:** Don't rush past the emotional answer to get to logistics — this is the fuel for the close.

**6. PAIN — deeper dive**
- *Why #6:* You opened with pain; now twist gently to raise the cost of staying stuck.
- **Questions:** "What's it costing you to stay on this plateau another year — not just money?" · "How does the grind affect things outside work?"
- **Listening for:** The emotional + relational cost (time with Sara, burnout, the embarrassment of being stuck). *Red flag:* if he minimizes it, urgency is low.
- **Landmine:** Don't manufacture pain he didn't express — reflect his own words back, amplified.

**7. TIMING — why now**
- *Why #7:* He set his own deadline (propose next year). Make the timeline real.
- **Questions:** "Why fix this now and not six months ago — or six months from now?" · "What changes for you if you're still here next year?"
- **Listening for:** The self-set deadline + the cost of delay. *Red flag:* "no rush" undercuts the close — re-anchor to his own goal.
- **Landmine:** Use HIS deadline, not a fake "spots are limited."

**8. PARTNER — surface and neutralize early**
- *Why #8 (but flagged critical):* Sara is the most likely stall. Surface here so it's handled before the close, never at it.
- **Questions:** "You mentioned Sara — is a decision like this something you two make together?" · "What would she want to know?"
- **Listening for:** Whether it's a true joint decision (then get her involved / pre-empt her questions) or him deferring out of habit. *Red flag:* "I'd have to run it by her" said softly = the stall forming.
- **Landmine:** Don't dismiss her ("it's your business, your call") — that backfires. Equip him to bring her the decision with confidence: "What would make Sara feel good about this, not just you?"

**9. DOUBT — challenge him**
- *Why #9:* A competitive ex-athlete responds to a respectful challenge.
- **Questions:** "Real talk — what's the part of you that thinks this won't work for you specifically?" · "What's stopped you from breaking this plateau on your own?"
- **Listening for:** The self-doubt under the bravado, so you can address it directly in the pitch. *Red flag:* if the doubt is about the model itself, handle it before pitching.
- **Landmine:** Challenge the doubt, not the man. "You've clearly got the coaching skill — so the only thing missing is the system. Agree?"

**10. COMPARISON — surface the competition**
- *Why #10 (last):* By now he trusts you; find out who else he's weighing so you can position cleanly.
- **Questions:** "Are you looking at any other way to solve this — another program, hiring, doing it yourself?" · "How are you thinking about the trade-offs?"
- **Listening for:** Whether he's shopping or sold. *Red flag:* heavy comparison shopping = not ready; light = close-ready.
- **Landmine:** Don't trash alternatives — position {{PROGRAM_NAME}} as the only one that *installs the system* instead of handing him more to do.

### PITCH (10–15 min)
**TEMPERATURE CHECK FIRST:** "So if I'm hearing you right — you've got the coaching skill, you're stuck because everything runs through you manually, and you want to get to $30k without working more hours so you can build the life with Sara. On a scale of 1–10, how do you feel about putting a system in place that does that?" **Wait for a 9–10 before pitching.** If lower, ask what's missing and address it.

**CUSTOM PITCH PILLARS** (each: his pain → how it's solved → why he can't do it alone)

**Pillar 1 — Kill the manual DM grind with a lead system that runs without you.** He's spending 2–3 hours a day in the DMs with no repeatable source. {{PROGRAM_NAME}} installs the lead-gen + qualification engine so conversations come to him pre-warmed. *Why not alone:* he's tried for 3.5 years and plateaued — the bottleneck is that he IS the system; you remove him from it.

**Pillar 2 — A sales + delivery system that scales past his own hours.** Right now every client runs through Marcus personally, which caps him. The system handles onboarding, check-ins, and the repeatable parts so he can double clients without doubling hours. *Why not alone:* building this himself is exactly the work that's already keeping him stuck — he'd never get out of the grind to build the thing that gets him out of the grind.

**Pillar 3 — Done-WITH-you infrastructure he owns, not advice he has to implement.** Directly answers the course wound: this isn't recycled videos, it's the actual system built with him and left running. *Why not alone:* the last guy sold him advice and left; the whole point here is he ends with an asset, not a to-do list.

**DELIVERY OVERVIEW (brief — no logistics seeding):** Day 1 → find the single highest-leverage revenue mover. Week 2 → lead engine live. Month 3 → sales/delivery system running. Month {{PROGRAM_LENGTH}} → off the plateau, working less. Keep it outcome-focused; don't drown him in mechanics.

**PRICE DROP:** State the investment plainly — you supply the figure live; the skill stores no pricing — then **stop talking.** Let the silence do the work. (If {{BRAND_NAME}} offers tiers or a PIF, frame the recommended tier to his situation, then stop.)

## 🚨 OBJECTION HANDLING PLAYBOOK
Pre-loaded from the DMs + anticipated. For each: the real issue, then the isolate→reframe.

**1. "How is this different from that course I bought?"** (his #1 — pre-load it) → *Real issue: fear of being burned again.* "Fair question — and honestly if it sounded like that course I'd tell you to pass. The difference is they sold you advice and left you to do it. We build the system with you and it runs after. You end with infrastructure, not a folder of videos. You've already seen [the case study / the system] — that's not a promise, it's on the screen."

**2. "I need to talk to Sara."** (should be neutralized in discovery) → *Real issue: joint decision or soft no.* "Totally get it — what specifically would Sara want to know? Let's make sure you can answer it with confidence, not just relay it." Isolate her real question (money? risk? time?) and handle it live so he leaves equipped, not deferring.

**3. "It's a lot of money right now."** → *Real issue: ROI belief, not affordability.* "It is real money. So let's be clear-eyed: you've been flat at ~$10k for 14 months. What's another year on this plateau cost you — not just the lost revenue, but the time with Sara and the grind? The risk isn't the investment, it's staying exactly where you are." Offer payment plan / entry tier as a bridge if {{BRAND_NAME}} has one — never as a discount on belief.

**4. "I need to think about it."** → *Real issue: an unspoken doubt.* "Of course — but let's not let 'think about it' be code for a question I didn't answer. What's the part you're actually unsure about?" Surface it and handle it now.

**5. "Can I start with something smaller?"** → *Real issue: risk-hedging.* "You can start lighter, and I'll show you what that looks like. But your problem is the *system* — a half-system leaves you in the grind. The full build is what actually gets you off the plateau; the lighter option just takes longer to feel it."

**6. "What if it doesn't work for my niche specifically?"** → *Real issue: 'I'm the exception.'* "Busy-professional-men coaching is one of the cleaner fits for this, because your buyers have money and no time — exactly who a system serves best. And the build is customized to your offer, not a template. If anything yours is easier, not harder."

**7. "I've got other calls scheduled / I'm shopping around."** → *Real issue: not yet convinced you're the one.* "Smart to look. Here's all I'd ask: judge them on whether they hand you advice or build you the system. That's the line that matters for your plateau. Want me to show you exactly what we'd build first so you've got a real comparison?"
