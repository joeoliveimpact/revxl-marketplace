# Framework: Objection Handling

<purpose>
The full objection-handling method — not a list of canned replies. Use this to build the customized Objection Playbook in a strategy blueprint, and to pre-empt objections during discovery.
</purpose>

## Core Concepts

### What Objections Actually Are
Reframe before you respond — objections are a natural part of the conversation:
- Unanswered questions / missed discovery questions
- An opportunity to walk to the prospect's side of the table
- Natural uncertainty/fear — their brain is wired to keep them safe, not happy
- **Buying signs**

### The 4-Step Handling Sequence
1. **Isolate** — "Is that the only thing standing between you and getting started?" Pin down the *real* objection before responding.
2. **Triple tie-down certainty** — get them re-committed to the outcome and the strategy they already agreed to.
3. **Get creative** — Time, Payment Plan, Extra 1:1 Calls. Anchor any concession to TODAY / a specific timeframe, and make them feel you're doing them a favor.
4. **Hold them accountable** — tie back to what they told you earlier in the call.

### The Real Objection Behind the Words
Diagnose the root, don't answer the surface:
| They say | Real issue(s) |
|----------|---------------|
| "I need to think about it" | Lack of trust · clarity · or urgency |
| "I can't afford it" vs "That's a lot of money" | Financially incapable **vs** lack of VALUE |
| "Timing to start" vs "I don't have time" | Lack of urgency vs lack of value |
| "I need to talk to my partner/spouse" | Lack of confidence in themselves or the program |
| "I have other calls with competitors" | Not confident this is the *best* solution |
| "Send me an email with details" | Lack of urgency · very analytical · or over-shared → confused |
| "I'll stick with what I'm doing" | Lack of desire · don't-get-burned-twice |
| "Send me the contract first" / "Can I speak to a reference?" | Analytical · lack of trust (in you or themselves) |
| "Are you sure this works? What if it doesn't?" | Lack of confidence in themselves or in you |

### One-Liners (use sparingly, go slowly)
- "Highest version of you vs lowest version of you"
- "Your brain is designed to keep you safe, not happy"
- "The only way to make a decision is access to information — and I have the information"
- "Compared to what?"
- "Why don't we just call it a no?" (takeaway)

## Behavioral Rules
- **DROP THE TEMPERATURE in the room** — stay calm, compose your emotions, never get frustrated.
- **Go slowly** — don't be quick to fire off a one-liner. Come to their side of the table.
- Use **ROI best-case / worst-case** framing.
- Best objection handling happens in **discovery** — surface and defuse concerns before the pitch so they never harden into a "no."

## Examples
- **Isolate then reframe (money):** *"I hear you — what's it actually costing you to stay where you are? How many months of runway before this is a crisis instead of an inconvenience?"*
- **Partner (defused live, not at close):** *"When you talk to them tonight, what's their biggest concern going to be — the money, the program, or something else?"* → address it now → *"Would it make sense to get them on a quick call so I can walk you both through it?"*

## Anti-Patterns
- ❌ Answering the surface objection without isolating the real one
- ❌ Firing one-liners rapid-fire → reads as combative, raises the temperature
- ❌ Meeting "I need to talk to my partner" at the close (it should've been handled in discovery)
- ❌ Open-ended concessions with no TODAY anchor → kills urgency
