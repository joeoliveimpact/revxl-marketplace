# Framework: High-Impact Questions & Tactics

<purpose>
The question design, power-opener, active-listening, and closing tactics that enrich the RFPDP structure. Distilled from Engine For Impact's High Impact Sales Call Blueprint. Use these to sharpen the specific questions and scripts inside any strategy blueprint.
</purpose>

## Core Concepts

### The Power Opener (Intent Statement)
A ~30-second opener that sets tone and creates instant rapport. Build it from: introduce with impact (who + benefit) → build empathy (acknowledge their struggle) → confirm commitment (they set aside the time) → create exclusivity (not for everyone) → ask permission to explore → set expectations (what happens if it's a fit) → get the green light.

### The 5 High-Impact Questions
Layer these into discovery — they uncover emotional motivators, not surface goals:
1. **Goal Excavator** — *"If we were talking a year from now and you were thrilled with your progress, what would have changed?"*
2. **Obstacle Identifier** — *"What's the biggest challenge that's held you back from achieving this on your own?"*
3. **Pain Amplifier** — *"If nothing changes, how does that impact your life 6 months / a year from now?"* (creates urgency)
4. **Commitment Gauge** — *"On a scale of 1–10, how committed are you to making this change, no matter what?"*
5. **Solution Seeker** — *"If you had a magic wand and could instantly solve one aspect of this, what would it be?"* (reveals what they value most)

### Active Listening (turn answers into insight)
Reflective listening (paraphrase back) · dig deeper with follow-ups · read emotional cues (tone, pauses, word choice) · connect the dots back to your solution · take notes · use silence to make them elaborate · validate feelings. **The goal: make them feel truly heard.** Mirroring their exact words is the single highest-leverage tactic.

### Closing Techniques (match to personality)
- **Assumptive** — proceed as if they're in: *"Which option works better for you to get started?"*
- **Summary** — recap the value, then ask for the sale
- **Urgency** — limited availability / time-sensitive
- **Emotion-driven** — tie the decision to their emotional motivator
- **Silent** — ask for the sale, then go quiet
- **Objection-to-close** — turn the objection into the reason to buy

## Examples
- **Pain Amplifier in action:** prospect spending savings with no income → *"If the lead-gen problem is still here in 6 months, where does that leave the runway you're burning?"*
- **Mirroring:** prospect said *"I'm ready to start coaching more"* (a big statement for a reserved communicator) → blueprint flags it as the emotional close anchor and quotes it back verbatim.

## Anti-Patterns
- ❌ Asking the 5 questions like a checklist without listening to the answers
- ❌ A generic opener ("so how's business?") — the prospect already told you the problem in DMs
- ❌ Picking a close that fights their personality (hard urgency on an analytical, guarded buyer)
