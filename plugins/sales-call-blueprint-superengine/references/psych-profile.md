# Framework: Psychological Profile Extraction

<purpose>
Teaches how to read a pre-call DM thread (and any triage notes) and extract a structured psychological profile of the prospect. This profile is the foundation of every blueprint — both triage and strategy. The goal: walk into the call knowing the prospect better than they know themselves, using THEIR exact words.
</purpose>

## Core Concepts

### The Five Extraction Dimensions
Read the DM thread and document each:

1. **Pain Points & Urgency**
   - What specific problems did they articulate? Their *exact* words/phrases.
   - Timeline pressure — why now vs. 6 months ago?
   - Emotional intensity — frustration, desperation, hope, skepticism?

2. **Commitment Signals**
   - Investment-readiness tells (mentioned budget, asked about pricing/cost directly)
   - Decision authority — solo, or is a partner/spouse involved? (watch for "we")
   - Action orientation — moving now vs. "just exploring"
   - Past failures they referenced

3. **Objection Previews**
   - Concerns, hesitations, qualifiers raised in the DMs
   - Questions about time, money, effectiveness, guarantees
   - Comparison-shopping behavior

4. **Language & Communication Style**
   - Formal vs. casual · verbose vs. direct · emotional vs. analytical
   - Specific phrases to mirror back on the call
   - Channel tells (voice notes = comfort/investment; one-word replies = guarded)

5. **Relationship Context**
   - How they found you · how long following · what content resonated
   - Familiarity with your methodology / the closer / the brand

### Reading Decision-Making Pattern
How fast did they move once they decided? Days between replies? Analytical questions or emotional ones? This *predicts how they behave at the decision point* on the call. A prospect who made a big life decision recently (moved, quit a job) doesn't dabble — the risk is usually a partner variable, not their own commitment.

### Trust Calibration
Score trust Low / Medium / High and say WHY. Long-time follower who consumes content ≠ someone who just answered a cold DM. Don't oversell a relationship that isn't there — it reads as fake on the call.

## Handling Thin or Missing DMs  *(do not fabricate intel)*
The DM thread is sometimes short, missing, or just a booking confirmation. When intel is thin:
- **Flag the gap explicitly** in the profile: write `[NOT IN DMs — confirm live]` rather than inventing a pain point, number, or emotion.
- Downgrade Closing Probability reasoning to reflect the uncertainty.
- Convert each missing dimension into a **priority discovery question** for the call — the gap becomes the agenda.
- If triage notes exist, treat them as *confirmed* intel and lean on them to fill gaps (see ${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/references/rfpdp-method.md on integrating triage notes).
- Never present a guess as a fact. A blueprint built on fabricated psychology is worse than one that honestly says "we don't know yet — find out in discovery."

## Examples
- **Exact-phrase mirroring:** Prospect wrote *"Getting leads, or trying to, is taking up 90% of my bandwidth."* → Profile logs it verbatim and the blueprint mirrors it back in the Pain step and the close. That phrase IS the gap.
- **Decision-pattern read:** Prospect moved countries, left a career, asked "can we discuss your program and cost?" → "Decisive; makes big moves when ready. Risk is the partner variable, not her commitment."
- **Thin-DM case:** Only message is "booked for Tuesday 👍" → Profile reads `Primary Pain: [NOT IN DMs — confirm live]`, Closing Probability: `Unknown — no discovery data`, and the blueprint front-loads pain discovery.

## Anti-Patterns
- ❌ Inventing a pain point or emotional state to fill the template → fabrication erodes the closer's trust in the doc
- ❌ Generic profiles ("motivated buyer, wants results") that could describe anyone
- ❌ Treating relative language as fact ("seems to have money") instead of flagging it to confirm
- ❌ Ignoring a "we" / partner mention — it's the most common close-killer
