# Teach Mode — teach to fish while we fish

`teach_mode` = on (default) / off. State lives in the persona pack meta (`meta.teach_mode`).
Toggle anytime: the user says "teach mode on" / "teach mode off" → honor it for the rest of the session.

The point: the user LEARNS how audience-testing works while we run it — so over time they read their
own market better and trust the tool more. Teach-to-fish + learn-to-fish at once.

## When ON
Drop a short plain-English "why" next to each move — then do the move.
- 8th-grade test: would a sharp 13-year-old get it on the first read? Use no word the coach wouldn't
  say out loud. If a real term is unavoidable, gloss it in half a line.
- Tie the lesson to the move you JUST made — not abstract theory.
- Name the principle once so they can reuse it.
- One or two lines per note. Never a lecture. Don't slow the run.
- Render in the MAIN chat only — wrapped around the verdict. NEVER inside the swarm subagents.

## When OFF
Build and report exactly as the skill does today. No teaching notes.
(Normal jargon-translation still applies; teaching overhead does not.)

## Good vs bad note (calibrate to this)
- BAD (jargon, no why): "Monadic isolation reduces anchoring bias across the panel."
- GOOD (plain, tied to the move, named once): "Each fake panelist reacts on their own, without seeing
  the others — so nobody just copies the loudest voice. That's why the split is honest.
  (Fancy name for it: 'judging alone.')"

## What to teach in THIS skill (Focus Group concepts) — pick the 1–2 that fit the moment, don't dump all
- Why a PANEL of strangers, not one opinion: one person is a coin-flip; a spread of fake buyers shows the pattern.
- Why each reacts ALONE: so they don't copy each other — the result is honest.
- The TWO scores, never blended: attention = "did it make me stop?" · convert = "did I get what you do,
  and do I trust it?" A thing can grab attention and still fail convert.
- Confidence-weighting: a few people who feel STRONGLY can outweigh a bored majority — when that flips
  the winner, that's a real signal, not noise.
- Tap-through is the bio's real score, not the follow: the bio's only job is to earn the tap to your
  profile; the follow gets earned later by your face + content.
- What an objection cluster MEANS for you: if the same worry shows up a lot, that's your next fix —
  change that one thing and re-test.
- Cold vs warm panel: cold = strangers (acquisition); warm = people who already follow you (retention).
  Same artifact scores differently to each.
- Directional, not a crystal ball: this points you the right way (A beats B); it does not predict exact
  numbers. Confirm a big call with one real-world check (a Story poll, real comments).

## The line to remember
We're not just handing you the verdict. We're teaching you to read the room yourself.
