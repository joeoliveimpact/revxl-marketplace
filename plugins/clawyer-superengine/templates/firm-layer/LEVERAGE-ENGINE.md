# Hunt every layer of law for something the client can use. Turn the other side’s mistakes into motions.

> Fires automatically on any adverse filing, any court order, and any time the client asks “what can I use here.”
> Every authority it surfaces is run through the Verification Partner before it’s relied on. Nothing unconfirmed gets used.

-----

## WHAT IT DOES

The Leverage Engine reads a situation or a document and asks one question at every level of law: **what rule, deadline, case, or requirement here can be turned to the client’s advantage?** Not just “what does the law say” — “what can I do with it to put the other side on the back foot.”

It scans five layers, top to bottom, every time.

-----

## THE FIVE LAYERS

**1. LOCAL — your court's district local rules and the chancellor’s practices.** Hearing-setting and trial-setting procedure (return dates come from the Court Administrator), judgment-form requirements, the ex parte calendar, the district’s sanctions provision, and how the district handles unrepresented parties. Where a local rule conflicts with a higher authority, the higher one controls — that itself is leverage when the other side leans on a local practice that the UCCR or a statute overrides.

**2. UNIFORM CHANCERY COURT RULES (UCCR).** The high-yield pressure points in family litigation live here:

- **Rule 8.05 financial disclosure** — mandatory in every case with economic issues. A missing, late, or false 8.05 is a contempt and fraud-on-the-court angle. This is the single most-used piece of leverage in a contested chancery divorce.
- **The earwigging / ex parte ban** — no communicating with the chancellor on the merits outside the orderly progress of the case; copying opposing counsel does not cure it.
- **The findings requirement** — the chancellor must make findings on the record; a missing-findings order is attackable.
- **The approval-of-orders-as-to-form rule** — a proposed order must go to the other side before it goes to the chancellor.
- **Note the 2022 renumbering** — the firm confirms the current UCCR number against the official source before citing it. Old numbers no longer map.

**3. MISSISSIPPI RULES OF CIVIL PROCEDURE.** Service (Rule 4 for the divorce complaint vs. the special Rule 81 summons for temporary, contempt, and modification matters — served on the party, not the attorney; a defect here is jurisdictional leverage); time computation (Rule 6, including the extra days for service by mail and the non-extendable post-trial clocks); discovery and its sanctions (Rule 37); findings (Rule 52(b)); new trial / alter-or-amend (Rule 59); relief from judgment (Rule 60).

**4. MISSISSIPPI RULES OF EVIDENCE.** The admissibility layer — what comes in and what gets kept out: hearsay and its exceptions, authentication, prejudice, the limits on lay vs. expert opinion and the reliability standard for experts. This is where the firm finds the openings to keep the other side’s weak evidence out and to get the client’s evidence in.

**5. MISSISSIPPI CODE (TITLE 93) + CASE LAW + FEDERAL OVERLAYS.** The substantive law — custody, support, equitable distribution, alimony — and the controlling cases (Albright, Ferguson, Armstrong, and the rest). Plus the federal hooks that touch this case: federal retirement division (the TSP uses a Retirement Benefits Court Order and FERS/CSRS uses a Court Order Acceptable for Processing — a QDRO is the wrong instrument for federal plans, and getting it wrong is costly), the dependency-claim release for taxes, and the UCCJEA for custody jurisdiction.

## TURNING THE OTHER SIDE’S MISTAKES INTO MOTIONS

When the Engine finds a defect in the other side’s conduct or filing, it pairs it with the response:

- Missed deadline or late filing → motion to strike; oppose any enlargement absent excusable neglect.
- Defective Rule 81 service → object to jurisdiction / move to quash; insist on a proper special summons for any contempt or modification.
- Missing or incomplete Rule 8.05 → motion to compel plus contempt; ask the court to disregard the non-compliant statement.
- Discovery failure → motion to compel, then sanctions under Rule 37.
- Ex parte / earwigging contact → object on the record; weigh a recusal motion.
- Proposed order that exceeds what the court ruled → object to form; tender a competing proposed order; if it’s already entered, move to alter or amend.
- Missing findings → motion for findings on the contested Albright/Ferguson/Armstrong factors.

Each pairing comes to the client as: here is the defect, here is the rule it violates, here is the motion or objection that answers it, here is the citation (confirmed).

## THE REPEATABLE SCAN (run on any document or posture)

1. Classify the document and whose it is.
1. **Local layer** — any your court's district local rule or chancellor practice triggered?
1. **UCCR layer** — 8.05? earwigging? findings? approval-of-form? (current rule numbers, confirmed)
1. **MRCP layer** — deadline/time computation, service, discovery, post-trial motions.
1. **MRE layer** — admissibility problems to exploit or defend.
1. **Substantive layer** — which Title 93 statute and which factor test governs; are required findings present?
1. **Federal layer** — retirement (RBCO/COAP/TSP), tax dependency release, UCCJEA.
1. **Output** — a ranked list: *rule / deadline / case / requirement → the leverage → the responsive motion or objection*, each with a confirmed citation.

-----

## THE VERIFICATION RULE

Every statute, rule, and case the Engine surfaces is pulled from a real source this session and confirmed good law before the firm relies on it. The Engine is aggressive about finding leverage and absolute about not inventing it. A made-up rule is not leverage — it is a loss waiting to happen. If the Engine cannot confirm an authority, it says so and does not use it.
