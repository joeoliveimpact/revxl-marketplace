# Deep Custody Intake — get everything, calmly

> **Jurisdiction note (Clawyer):** this file was written for Mississippi family court. Outside Mississippi, or outside a family case, treat every rule number, case name, deadline, fee and form in it as an example of what to look up, not as your law. Family-law steps in it do not apply to other kinds of case. Confirm your own state's version first and tag anything unconfirmed `CONFIRM-BEFORE-CITING`.

The firm's full custody questioning. Goal: **pull out ALL the information** that bears on the child's best interest, so
every specialist can build the strongest, most realistic case. The person usually doesn't know what matters — so the
firm **asks everything and decides what's relevant; it never asks the person "can I use this?"** Each group ties to the
forum state's custody factors (MS example: the **Albright** factors, `references/albright-factors.md`, *Albright v. Albright, 437 So. 2d 1003 (Miss. 1983)*; for a non-MS case, tie to the resolved state-law profile's factors)
`[VERIFIED — MS Supreme Court / Justia & courts.ms.gov]`.

## Say this first — the candor compact (before any questions)
Open every intake with a plain, firm honesty agreement, in the person's own language:
> "Before we start — I need you to be **honest with me about everything, including the parts that make you look bad.**
> This is your case and your kids. The other side and the judge will dig, and gaps or surprises hurt you far worse than
> the truth. If you hide things from me, the firm is working blind and it can all be for nothing. There's nothing you
> can tell me that we can't work with — but only if I know it. Deal?"
Then reassure: this is private working prep, we go one step at a time, and "I don't know" is a fine answer.

## How to ask
- **A few questions at a time**, conversationally — never dump the whole list. Calm, plain, child-first.
- **Extract everything**: if an answer opens a door, follow it. Capture names, dates, specifics.
- **Write answers to `case-profile.md` and the Child Snapshot** (`references/child-snapshot.md`) as you go — organize, don't just chat.
- **Frame to steady the person**: each question is concrete and answerable, which calms panic and gets them thinking clearly.
- For each involvement claim, ask **"what proves it?"** (texts, calendar, receipts, photos, records).

## The question bank (grouped; ask in batches)

### A. The parent — schedule, availability, home  *(employment responsibilities; capacity for primary care; stability)*
1. Exact work schedule — days, start/end times, rotating or on-call shifts? 2. Do hours change week to week; how far ahead do you know? 3. Employer flexibility for a sick child or appointment (leave/work-from-home)? 4. Commute each way? 5. Who can help when you're working/unavailable (family, partner, sitter) — how close, how reliable? 6. Your home: own/rent, bedrooms, does the child have their own bed/room/space? 7. How long there; staying or moving? 8. Is the home set up for the child (safe sleep, supplies, school distance)? 9. Walk me through a typical weekday and weekend with the child, hour by hour. 10. Your own wake/sleep schedule — does it fit the child's needs?

### B. Caregiving history & division of labor  *(continuity of care — often decisive)*
11. Before separation, who handled mornings (wake, dress, breakfast, out the door)? 12. Meals/feeding? 13. Bath and bedtime? 14. Homework and school projects? 15. To/from school, daycare, activities? 16. Scheduling + attending doctor/dentist/therapy? 17. Staying home when the child was sick? 18. Talking to teachers/coaches/other parents? 19. Buying clothes/shoes/supplies and tracking needs? 20. Birthdays/holidays/social calendar? 21. Since separation, how has each shifted — who does what now? 22. For each — what proves it (texts, calendar, receipts, app logs, photos, sign-in sheets)?

### C. The child — health  *(age/health of child; home/school/community record)*
23. Chronic conditions, allergies, special needs? 24. Medications — name, dose, schedule, who administers? 25. Pediatrician + specialists — names, locations, last visits? 26. Therapy/counseling (speech, OT, behavioral, mental health) — provider, frequency? 27. Dental/vision + recent visits? 28. Upcoming procedures/treatments/follow-ups? 29. Immunization status + where records are? 30. Dietary needs/restrictions?

### D. The child — education  *(home/school/community record; parenting skills)*
31. School, grade, teacher(s)? 32. Academic performance, recent grades/report cards? 33. IEP, 504, or special-ed service — what it provides? 34. Learning differences, accommodations, tutoring? 35. Attendance/tardiness — who gets the child there? 36. School behavior — notes, conferences, interventions? 37. School distance from each home + transport? 38. Who attends conferences and events?

### E. The child — activities, routine, social world  *(emotional ties; stability)*
39. Activities/sports/clubs/lessons/religious — schedule + locations? 40. Who signs up, pays, transports? 41. Close friends + where they live relative to each parent? 42. Daily routine — meals, screen time, homework, bedtime? 43. What comforts/matters to the child (people, toys, foods, traditions, pets)? 44. Recent changes in mood/sleep/appetite/behavior you've observed? 45. Special needs requiring specific care or stability?

### F. The other parent — role, schedule, communication  *(continuity; emotional ties — neutral facts)*
46. Their work schedule/availability, as you understand it? 47. What caregiving they've historically handled? 48. Current parenting-time arrangement (formal/informal) and how it's working? 49. How you two communicate now (call/text/app/third party)? 50. Where communication breaks down — examples you can show? 51. Agreements being / not being followed — documented? 52. What the child's relationship with the other parent looks like, from what you observe?

### G. Safety / red flags — DOCUMENTED CONDUCT ONLY  *(record facts, never labels or diagnoses)*
> Every answer = observed, dated, provable events; never conclusions, motive, or name-calling.
53. Specific safety incidents you witnessed or have records of — what, when, what proof (photos, messages, reports, witnesses)? 54. Documented substance concerns affecting caregiving — dated observations/records? 55. Violence or threats reported/recorded — police reports, medical records, protective orders? 56. Missed exchanges, unsafe transport, unsupervised risk, unmet medical/school needs — with dates? 57. Third parties who witnessed/documented (family, school, doctors)? 58. Any existing order, CPS contact, or safety plan?

### H. Co-parenting & communication  *(parenting skills; willingness to foster the other relationship)*
59. How you share info (medical, school, schedule)? 60. How decisions are made now (joint/one/contested)? 61. How you handle disagreements? 62. Any tools used/proposed (shared calendar, co-parenting app) — open to one? 63. Can you show you keep the other parent informed and support that relationship?

### I. Finances — ONLY as relevant to custody  *(capacity to provide; stability — KEEP SEPARATE from support)*
64. Childcare you use/can arrange (provider, hours, cost, reliability)? 65. Can you meet day-to-day needs (housing, food, clothing, supplies) in your home? 66. Health insurance for the child, or access to it? 67. Child expenses you cover now — documented?
> This is *ability to care*, not income division/alimony — those go in the **support** file, filed separately (one issue per filing).

### J. The parent's OWN vulnerabilities — defensive prep  *(prepare answers before the other side raises them)*
68. Anything in your past/present the other side could raise (incidents, records, gaps in involvement, work demands, housing, health, past conflict)? 69. For each — the honest context + proof it's resolved/improving/managed? 70. Periods you were less involved — why, and what's changed? 71. Messages you've sent that could read badly out of context? 72. Your plan to address each weakness proactively (stability, routine, documentation started)?

## After intake
Hand the answers to the Custody Strategist (Albright matrix), the Parenting-Plan Drafter (a specific schedule), the
Evidence Specialist (proof to gather), and the Hearing-Prep Coach — and present the **routes & outcomes**
(`references/custody-routes-outcomes.md`). End with a NEXT ACTION. The child's best interest is the polestar, always.
