# Grandparent Visitation & Third-Party / Non-Parent Custody (Mississippi)

> **Jurisdiction note (Clawyer):** this file was written for Mississippi family court. Outside Mississippi, or outside a family case, treat every rule number, case name, deadline, fee and form in it as an example of what to look up, not as your law. Family-law steps in it do not apply to other kinds of case. Confirm your own state's version first and tag anything unconfirmed `CONFIRM-BEFORE-CITING`.

> **When this fires (auto).** "I'm the grandparent and they won't let me see my grandkids," "I've raised this child, can
> I get custody," "the parents are unfit / absent," a non-parent (grandparent, relative, step-parent, caregiver) seeking
> **visitation** or **custody.** These are **separate actions** from a divorce, with their own tests — and the law starts
> heavily in the **parents' favor.** Research/drafting support — **not legal advice.**

## Two different things — don't confuse them
**Visitation** (time with the child) and **custody** (legal/physical control) are governed by **different, much higher**
standards for a non-parent. Name which one the person actually wants first.

## A) Grandparent visitation — § 93-16-3
A grandparent may petition for visitation in **two situations**:
1. **§ 93-16-3(1):** when a court has **awarded custody to one parent** or **terminated one parent's rights**, or when
   **one parent has died** — then *that parent's* parent (the grandparent) may petition the court that entered the order
   (or, on a parent's death, the chancery court where the child resides). `[VERIFIED — FindLaw published Miss. Code
   § 93-16-3(1), read verbatim 2026-05-24]`
2. **§ 93-16-3(2):** any other grandparent may petition **only if the court finds (a)** the grandparent **had a viable
   relationship** with the child **and** the parent/custodian **unreasonably denied** visitation, **and (b)** visitation
   is in the **child's best interest.** `[VERIFIED — FindLaw published Miss. Code § 93-16-3(2), read verbatim 2026-05-24]`
- **"Viable relationship"** and the weight given to a fit parent's decision are defined further by statute/case law —
  the bar is real; a fit parent's choice gets deference. `[CONFIRM BEFORE CITING — verify § 93-16-3(3) viable-relationship
  definition + the controlling MS case on parental deference before filing]`

## B) Third-party / non-parent CUSTODY — the natural-parent presumption
A non-parent seeking **custody** against a parent faces the **natural-parent presumption**: a fit natural parent is
entitled to custody over a third party **unless** the third party proves the parent has (i) **abandoned** the child,
(ii) engaged in conduct **clearly detrimental/immoral** to the child, (iii) is **unfit**, or (iv) (a recognized basis
such as) the **natural-parent presumption has otherwise been overcome.** Only **after** the presumption is overcome does
the court reach **Albright** best-interest. `[CONFIRM BEFORE CITING — the natural-parent presumption and its specific
rebuttal grounds are MS case law; pull the controlling MS Supreme Court / Court of Appeals case and verify the elements
before filing]` Albright itself may be cited without a fresh search once the presumption is overcome.
- **In loco parentis / de facto custody** alone does **not** defeat a fit parent — it's a factor, not a trump.
  `[CONFIRM BEFORE CITING]`

## Filing sequence
1. Name it: **visitation (§ 93-16)** or **custody (natural-parent presumption + Albright)** — separate petitions.
2. **Petition** in chancery (Rule 81 special summons) — Drafting Counsel (`references/filing-templates.md`).
3. Build the proof: for visitation, the **viable relationship** + **unreasonable denial** (Evidence Evaluator, Records);
   for custody, evidence going to a **rebuttal ground** (abandonment/unfitness/detriment) — high bar, document heavily.
4. Hearing prep (`references/courtroom-scripts.md`); proposed order → **Order Integrity Reviewer**.

## Honest limits & cautions
The parental-deference and natural-parent-presumption case law is the heart of these cases and is **not** something to
wing — **verify the controlling authority** (`scripts/fetch_law.py` for the statute; the case law needs a real read) and
strongly consider **free human legal help** (`references/tools-and-integrations.md`); custody-from-a-parent is among the
hardest asks in family court.
