# Custody Premortem — red-team the MOVE before it leaves the firm

> **Jurisdiction note (Clawyer):** this file was written for Mississippi family court. Outside Mississippi, or outside a family case, treat every rule number, case name, deadline, fee and form in it as an example of what to look up, not as your law. Family-law steps in it do not apply to other kinds of case. Confirm your own state's version first and tag anything unconfirmed `CONFIRM-BEFORE-CITING`.

> 🧭 METHOD IS UNIVERSAL — authorities named below are the Mississippi worked
> example (this plugin's convention); your forum's law comes from the State-Law
> Resolver profile, which controls. **What this adds over the ADRC
> Outbound Mode:** the council red-teams a DOCUMENT; this red-teams a DECISION —
> should we file at all, should we send at all, is this the right vehicle, what
> does it provoke. Run it BEFORE drafting, then ADRC Outbound on the draft.

**Auto-fire triggers:** before filing or responding to any custody/visitation
petition · before any message to the opposing parent, opposing counsel, or the
GAL that goes beyond pure logistics · before any enforcement or contempt move ·
before any report to any agency · before offering evidence about the other
parent · whenever the client asks "should I file this," "should I send this,"
"how would they attack this," "premortem this."

Assume it is the day after the hearing and **we lost ground** — the petition
survived, the judge read us as the high-conflict parent, our own message
became their best exhibit, the GAL's next report cites our conduct. Work
backwards and find why, while it is still free to change.

**Give a straight answer.** Commit to a verdict, state the reasoning, name the
confidence level, and say what would change your mind. Where the outcome turns
on the judge's discretion, say which way it usually breaks and what moves
the odds. "Consult an attorney" in place of a finding is a non-answer.

## The standard everything is measured against

**The child's best interests, through your forum's factor framework.**
(📍 MS example: the Albright factors, Albright v. Albright, 437 So. 2d 1003
(Miss. 1983).) Not fairness to a
parent, not who is right, not who has suffered more.

Two consequences that drive most findings:

1. **Winning behavior and sympathetic behavior are the same behavior.** The
   calm, documenting, relationship-fostering parent wins on the merits AND looks
   like the better parent. There is rarely a tradeoff.
2. **A parent who looks like they are litigating against the other parent rather
   than for the child loses altitude fast** — even with good facts.

## Safety overrides tactics

Genuine present danger to the child is not a strategic question. Say so plainly
and act. Everything below assumes we are past that.

Conversely: **a report made because it is tactically useful is the single most
destructive thing available.** If a CPS call, emergency application, or safety
allegation is on the table and the honest answer to "would we do this if it
gained us nothing?" is no — that is a P0, full stop. (This firm's forbidden-
outputs rule already bars premature contempt and criminal allegations; this
extends the same discipline to agency reports and emergency vehicles.)

## Ground rules

- **Real facts only.** A manufactured or inflated narrative about the other
  parent is the fastest way to lose the custody position we hold — judges
  see it constantly and punish it. Mark anything unproven as unproven.
- **Our conduct gets the same scrutiny as theirs — ours FIRST.** Whatever we
  find, they will find.
- **Tag every legal proposition** per Citation Discipline. Untagged does not
  ship.
- **Never counsel waiving a right** — including parenting time — to reduce
  conflict. Flag it; the client decides. (Standing rule:
  "recommended is not required" — never satisfy a condition the other side
  invented, as a tactic.)
- **The child is never an instrument.** No coaching, no interviewing, no using
  the child as messenger, courier, or witness. Any finding that depends on it is
  dead on arrival.

## The hunt list

### A. Threshold — is this move even available?
- **Modification requires a material change in circumstances adversely affecting
  the child, then a best-interests analysis.** (📍 MS example: material change adversely affecting the child, then best
  interests — Ellis v. Ellis, 952 So. 2d 982 (Miss. Ct. App. 2006).) Your
  forum's threshold comes from the resolver profile. What specifically changed, when, and can we prove the date?
- **Are they meeting that threshold, or re-litigating?** A rerun of facts
  already decided is a threshold defense — much cheaper than a full
  best-interests fight.
- **Right vehicle.** Modification vs. enforcement vs. contempt vs. emergency —
  different filings, different standards, different service rules. Contempt and modification often carry special-summons/service
  procedure (📍 MS example: MRCP 4(i), formerly Rule 81(d)); wrong vehicle
  wastes the move. `[CHECK YOUR FORUM'S RULE]`
- **Forum and posture.** Which court holds the operative order, and is a motion
  already pending that this duplicates? (Check the docket FIRST — a duplicate
  petition on top of an unheard one is wasted paper.)
- **Deadlines.** Response windows, return dates, service walls already running.

### B. The status quo — strongest asset, biggest blind spot
- **Whatever custody time we currently hold is the thing to protect.** Does this
  filing open a door we want shut? Does it invite re-examination of an
  arrangement that is working?
- **Are we the moving party without needing to be?** Defending an order is
  stronger posture than seeking to change one. Going on offense needs a reason.
- **Delay analysis.** Time entrenches the status quo. Does moving fast help us
  or them? (Watch the reverse trap: a "stable arrangement"
  the other side built by self-help becomes THEIR status-quo argument — interference caselaw is the counter, and delay feeds it.
  📍 MS example: Ellis v. Ellis, 952 So. 2d 982 (Miss. Ct. App. 2006).)

### C. Willingness to foster the other parent's relationship — the Albright
factor that quietly decides cases
- **Gatekeeping.** Every denied visit, late exchange, unanswered request,
  unilateral schedule change on OUR side. Each is an exhibit against us.
  (📍 MS example: Masino v. Masino, 829 So. 2d 1267 (Miss. Ct. App. 2002) —
  interference alone decided custody. Most states weigh this factor heavily;
  resolve your forum's articulation.)
- **Communication record.** Read our last 20 co-parenting messages as the judge
  will. Terse is fine. Cold is fine. Sarcastic, accusatory, or point-scoring is
  not.
- **Disparagement.** Anything said about the other parent within the child's
  hearing, posted anywhere, or repeated by the child.
- **Are we doing the thing we are accusing them of?** Run it honestly. Most
  common own-goal in custody.

### D. Substance / safety allegations — proof, staleness, proportion
- **What is provable, and how recent?** Old conduct weighs less than current
  capacity. The chancellor asks "what is true now."
- **Admissibility.** Hearsay, unauthenticated screenshots, third-party accounts,
  records needing a release or subpoena. What survives an objection? (Authentication rules and your state's
  recording-consent law both gate this — resolve them before relying on any
  recording. 📍 MS example: MRE 901 + § 41-29-531(e) one-party consent.)
- **Recovery cuts against us.** Documented treatment or sustained sobriety is
  credited. Framing that ignores it reads as punishing recovery.
- **Proportion.** Overreaching — total cutoff where conditioned time fits the
  facts — reads as vindictive and costs credibility on the good facts.
- **Our own exposure.** Anything on our side a symmetrical inquiry surfaces.

### E. The GAL and neutrals — Mississippi's version
- **Know which model your forum uses — they are NOT interchangeable.** A
  best-interests GAL (📍 MS example: investigator whose recommendation is not
  binding, Lorenz v. Strait, 987 So. 2d 427 (Miss. 2008)) is a different animal
  from a child's-position advocate (e.g., New York's Attorney for the Child).
  Resolve your forum's model before assuming how the neutral weighs a move.
- **How does this move look in the GAL's next report?** Everything said to a
  neutral goes in a report, including its tone.
- **Would this move trigger an evaluation or investigation we don't want?**
  Slow, expensive, intrusive, unpredictable.
- **GAL-impeachment discipline:** attacks on the GAL's process belong in cross
  and briefing, on the record already built — never in correspondence, never in
  tone.

### F. Our own record, read hostile
- **OFW, texts, email, filings.** Assume every one is an exhibit. Anything
  written in anger?
- **Recordings.** Lawfulness first (your state's consent law controls; some
  states are all-party. 📍 MS example: § 41-29-531(e) one-party consent), then
  wisdom — recording the child, or a conversation with the child in it, reads
  badly regardless of legality — gate any child-voice audio behind
  a separate offer-or-withhold decision.
- **Household composition, moves, schooling, medical decisions** — anything
  frameable as instability.
- **Missed exchanges, appointments, school events** on our side.
- **Prior inconsistent statements.** Check the chronology and contradiction log
  before asserting anything. Never assert a schedule fact without reading the
  operative order's actual paragraphs — projected rotations and remembered
  weekends are how parties impeach themselves.

### G. Blowback and economics
- **What does this provoke?** Cross-petition, counter-allegations, an emergency
  filing (if the case history shows manufactured-emergency
  patterns, assume the response arrives in that shape), a GAL position
  against us, a referral.
- **Fee exposure.** Can they seek fees, and does anything here support a
  bad-faith finding against us? (resolve your forum's fee-shifting standard
  before asserting it.)
- **Cost vs. gain.** Model the realistic best outcome, subtract what reaching it
  costs — money, months, the child's stability, the client's capacity.
- **Escalation ratchet.** Does this close a settlement path still wanted?
- **The trial-proximity question:** inside ~30 days of trial,
  most emergency vehicles will not be heard before the trial that decides the
  same issue. The premortem asks: does this land as relief, or as trial
  evidence? Build accordingly.

### H. How the judge reads it
- Write the two-sentence version the judge forms after reading our filing.
  If it is "two parents who cannot stop fighting," the filing failed regardless
  of contents.
- **The precise claim beats the broad one.** One overstatement discredits the
  accurate allegations beside it. ("The allegation is false" is provable; "she
  made the false report" is not — assert exactly what the record supports.)
- Would we want this read aloud with the child old enough to hear it?

## Steps

1. **Restate the move in one sentence** and name the irreversible thing it
   causes.
2. **Load ground truth** — the operative order (read the actual paragraphs),
   the case profile, the chronology, the specific evidence the position rests on,
   and the actual message thread if a message is in scope. Never premortem from
   memory.
3. **Run our own conduct through C, D, F first.** Ours before theirs, always.
4. **Run the remaining lenses**, verifying as you go.
5. **Write their best response** — how competent opposing counsel attacks this,
   plus the GAL's likely read.
6. **Report** (ADRC P0–P3 format), **fix P0/P1 before presenting**, say what
   changed.

## Severity and the gate

| Severity | Meaning | Gate |
|---|---|---|
| **P0** | Fatal — risks custody we hold, or is a move a judge punishes | **Blocks send** |
| **P1** | Serious — materially weakens us or hands them the better story | **Blocks send** |
| **P2** | Should fix — a careful opponent exploits it | Fix if time |
| **P3** | Note | Log it |

Close every run with: **Verdict** (SEND / SEND AFTER FIXES / DO NOT SEND — no
fence-sitting) · **How this reads to the judge** (two sentences) · **How
this reads to the GAL** (one sentence) · **What we are knowingly accepting** ·
**NEXT ACTION.**

**Nothing goes to the other parent, counsel, the GAL, the court, or any agency
with an open P0 or P1.** The client can override explicitly, with the risk
stated back; the override is logged. A clean run still reports what it checked —
**silence is not a pass.**
