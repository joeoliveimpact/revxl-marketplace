## Armstrong — alimony / rehabilitative support

**Authority:** *Armstrong v. Armstrong*, 618 So. 2d 1278 (Miss. 1993) `[CONFIRM BEFORE CITING — confirm reporter cite
and year before filing]`. (The charter references a 1996 date; the commonly cited Armstrong factors decision is 1993 —
**resolve this discrepancy by verifying the official cite before any filing.**)

**Statutory authority:** Miss. Code **§ 93-5-23** — on divorce the court "may... make all orders touching... the
maintenance and alimony of the **wife or the husband**" (gender-neutral). `[VERIFIED — FindLaw §93-5-23, 2026-05-23]`

**Receiving vs. resisting — pick the theory that fits the actual person.** The firm fights *both* directions. If the user
is the **lower-earning / dependent / broke spouse, alimony is something to CLAIM, not just defend against** — and a
genuine ongoing **need is a legitimate basis** (don't talk a needy client out of the support they're entitled to). Choose
the alimony **type** (below) and theory that matches their reality:
- **Genuine ongoing need / long marriage / permanent earning disparity** → pursue **periodic** (and/or **lump-sum**) on a
  need + standard-of-living basis. *(This is where the old "never need-based" framing was too narrow — for a dependent
  spouse, need IS the case.)*
- **Can realistically retrain to self-sufficiency** → **rehabilitative** (temporary, milestone-based) — the
  reasonable-looking ask when the user has earning potential to rebuild.
- **Funded the other spouse's degree/career** → **reimbursement.**
- **Resisting** an alimony demand → attack the unproven Armstrong factors and use the property-first rule below.

**No gender-unfairness framing in either direction** — build the case on the statutory factors, economic need,
contributions, and the standard of living, never on "as the husband/wife I deserve."

**Apply to the facts in the user's case profile (`case-profile.md`).**

**Armstrong factors to address:**
1. Income and expenses of the parties.
2. Health and earning capacity of the parties.
3. Needs of each party.
4. Obligations and assets of each party.
5. Length of the marriage.
6. Presence/absence of minor children in the home.
7. Age of the parties.
8. Standard of living during the marriage and at the time of the support determination.
9. Tax consequences of the spousal-support award.
10. Fault or misconduct (only as MS law permits; **no crime allegations, no revenge framing**).
11. Wasteful dissipation of assets.
12. Any other just and equitable factor.

**Map to the user's facts:** contributions to the other spouse's education/career; each party's earning
capacity/employability; marital standard of living → the right alimony type and a concrete number/term.

### The four MS alimony types — pick the right ask
`[CONFIRM BEFORE CITING — confirm the controlling MS case for each type before filing; framework per reputable MS sources, 2026-05-23]`
| Type | What it is | Claim it when (offense) |
|---|---|---|
| **Periodic** | Ongoing payments at intervals; can last **indefinitely** until a terminating event; ends on the recipient's remarriage / a party's death; modifiable | Long marriage + a real, lasting income gap; the dependent spouse can't reach the marital standard of living alone |
| **Lump-sum** | A **fixed** total (one payment or installments); **not modifiable**, does **not** end on remarriage/death | You want certainty / a clean break / to offset a property imbalance; risk the payor won't keep paying |
| **Rehabilitative** | Time-limited (often ~2–5 yrs) to retrain/educate to self-sufficiency | The user can realistically become self-supporting with a defined plan |
| **Reimbursement** | Repays a spouse who financed the other's degree/career (*Guy v. Guy*, 736 So. 2d 1042 (Miss. 1999) `[CONFIRM pinpoint]`) | The user worked/paid so the other could get the education or build the career |

You can plead **more than one in the alternative** (e.g., periodic, or lump-sum in lieu). One issue per filing still
applies — alimony stays out of a custody or property motion.

> **Stayed home / gave up a career / put the other spouse through school?** That sacrifice has its own focused playbook —
> `references/career-sacrifice-claim.md` — which stacks reimbursement alimony (*Guy v. Guy*) + periodic/rehabilitative
> alimony + a larger Ferguson share, and shows how to **quantify** the foregone career and the education you funded.

### Property-first sequencing — fight for the bigger property share BEFORE alimony
`[CONFIRM BEFORE CITING — verify the MS sequencing rule/case before filing; consistent across reputable MS sources, 2026-05-23]`
In Mississippi the chancellor **divides the marital estate first (Ferguson), then awards alimony only if that division
leaves a deficit.** Strategy for a lower-earner: **maximize the equitable-distribution award first** (see Ferguson
offense below) — every dollar of a larger, secure property share strengthens the case *and* a thin property award is the
very thing that **justifies alimony on top.** Build both together: the property need and the alimony need are one story.

## Ferguson — equitable distribution of marital property

**Authority:** *Ferguson v. Ferguson*, 639 So. 2d 921 (Miss. 1994) `[VERIFIED — widely cited MS equitable-distribution
standard; confirm pinpoint before filing]`.

MS is an **equitable-distribution** state (not community property). The chancellor classifies property as marital or
separate, then divides marital property equitably under the Ferguson factors.

> **Always state the order of operations when you explain property division** (not just when alimony is the question):
> the chancellor **(1) classifies** each asset/debt as marital or separate, **(2) values** the marital estate, **(3)
> divides** it equitably under Ferguson — and **only then (4) considers alimony**, and only if the equitable division
> still leaves one spouse with a **deficit.** "Equitable" means *fair*, **not automatically 50/50.** A person asking only
> "how is the house/401k/business split?" should still be told where alimony sits in the sequence, because the size of
> the property award is what determines whether alimony is even reached. `[CONFIRM BEFORE CITING — classify→value→divide
> then-alimony sequence is the standard MS chancery order of operations; verify the controlling case before filing]`

**Ferguson factors:**
1. **Substantial contribution** to accumulation of the property (direct economic, domestic, and indirect contributions).
2. **Dissipation or disposal** of assets by either party.
3. Market value and emotional value of the assets.
4. Value of assets not subject to distribution (separate/non-marital property).
5. Tax and other economic consequences of the distribution.
6. Extent to which property division can eliminate the need for alimony or reduce future friction.
7. Needs of the parties for financial security.
8. Any other equitable factor.

**Offense — arguing for a LARGER (not just equal) share.** "Equitable" does **not** mean 50/50 — it means *fair*, and the
factors can justify a bigger share for the lower-earner. Build it:
- **Domestic / homemaker / indirect contributions count as substantial contribution** (factor 1) — raising the child,
  running the household, and supporting the other's career are weighed alongside the paycheck. A spouse who earned less
  cash often contributed *more* in kind; make that record concrete (years, roles, what it freed the other to do).
- **The other side's dissipation** (factor 2) — money spent on an affair, gambling, hiding, or wasting assets comes off
  *their* side of the ledger. Trace it (Forensic Accountant + discovery) and claw it back into the estate.
- **Your separate property stays yours; trace it** (factor 4) — inheritance/pre-marital assets kept separate aren't
  divided, so they don't reduce your share of the *marital* pot.
- **Need + financial security** (factors 6–7) — a larger property share that **reduces or eliminates the need for
  alimony** is a factor *in favor of* awarding it; tie the bigger share to your post-divorce stability and the child's.
- **Vehicle:** plead equitable distribution affirmatively (complaint/counterclaim), build the **Marital Estate Inventory**
  (`marital-estate-inventory.md`), and bring proper valuations — not the other side's lowball.

**Common scrutiny points (do not let these pass unchallenged):**
- An **online automated estimate** (e.g., a Zillow "Zestimate") on a marital home is **not** a court-standard valuation
  — flag the need for a formal appraisal before any equity-based trade.
- A **"net-difference"** retirement split is a proposal, not a standard — retirement earned during the marriage is
  generally marital; address via proper valuation and, where applicable, a **QDRO** `[CHECK — confirm QDRO requirement
  and process for the specific plans]`.
- **Inheritance/separate-property tracing** matters — separate property kept separate is generally not divided; trace it.
- Joint debt (e.g., mortgage and joint credit cards) is allocated as part of equitable distribution — address debt, not
  just assets.
