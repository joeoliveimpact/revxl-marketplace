# Divorce Roadmap — start to finish (Mississippi)

> **Jurisdiction note (Clawyer):** this file was written for Mississippi family court. Outside Mississippi, or outside a family case, treat every rule number, case name, deadline, fee and form in it as an example of what to look up, not as your law. Family-law steps in it do not apply to other kinds of case. Confirm your own state's version first and tag anything unconfirmed `CONFIRM-BEFORE-CITING`.

> **The firm's spine for "the whole case."** This is the map of *every* filing a Mississippi divorce needs, in order,
> so a self-represented person is never left wondering "what's next." The **Action-Plan Navigator** turns the current
> stage into the next concrete step; **Drafting Counsel** produces each document using `references/filing-templates.md`.
>
> **Mississippi is the built-in spine.** For a non-MS case, keep the *structure* (these stages exist in every state) but
> research that state's statutes/forms and re-tag. Research/drafting support — **not legal advice.**

## How to use this file
1. **Find where the case is now** (Stage 0 below) — ask the person, or read it from `case-profile.md`.
2. **Name the track** — (A) irreconcilable differences (uncontested/agreed) or (B) contested / fault. With children adds a support + custody + financial-disclosure layer to either.
3. **Give the person the *one* next filing** — not the whole list at once (plain-language, one step at a time).
4. **Draft it** from `references/filing-templates.md`, then hand it off with the courthouse walk-through (Action-Plan Navigator).

## Track A — Irreconcilable-differences (agreed / uncontested) divorce
Governing statute: **Miss. Code § 93-5-2.** The simplest, fastest path **when the spouses agree** (or agree to let the
court decide what they can't).

| # | Filing / step | What it is & what triggers it | Template | Authority · tag |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | **Joint Complaint for Divorce** (ID) — *or* a Complaint where the other spouse is **personally served** or signs a **written waiver of process** | Opens the case. ## Track B — Contested / fault-ground divorce
Governing statute: **Miss. Code § 93-5-1** (twelve fault grounds). Runs on the **MS Rules of Civil Procedure.** Slower,
evidence-heavy; the injured party must **prove** the ground. ID is commonly pleaded **in the alternative** (§ 93-5-2(6)).

**The twelve § 93-5-1 grounds**, in statutory order (decreed to the **injured party**): (1) natural impotency · (2)
adultery (barred by collusion, or by cohabitation after the complainant knew) · (3) being sentenced to the penitentiary
and not pardoned before being sent · (4) willful, continued and obstinate **desertion for one (1) year** · (5) habitual
drunkenness · (6) habitual and excessive use of opium, morphine or other like drug · (7) **habitual cruel and inhuman
treatment, including spousal domestic abuse** (provable by a single credible witness, who may be the injured party) ·
(8) **mental illness or intellectual disability at the time of marriage**, if the complaining party did not know of it ·
(9) **bigamy** (marriage to another person at the time of the pretended marriage) · (10) **pregnancy of the wife by
another** at the time of marriage, if the husband did not know · (11) **prohibited kinship/incest** · (12) **incurable
mental illness** (strict statutory proof: ≥3 years' institutional confinement + two qualified physicians' affidavits).

> **Post-judgment / reserved issues.** A court may grant the divorce but **reserve** financial and custody issues for a
> later trial (Rule 54(b)). Those reserved issues then run their own track to a **final adjudication** — this is where a
> case like the workspace's own sits (divorce granted, custody/support/property reserved for a final trial). For that
> phase the relevant tools are **Drafting Counsel** (single-issue motions), the **Order Integrity Reviewer** (the entered
> order must match the ruling), discovery, and hearing prep — not the opening complaint.
>
> **After the final judgment, the case still isn't over.** Custody/support/visitation can be modified and enforced until
> the child is **emancipated** (MS: generally age 21), property/alimony issues can resurface, and a parent may try to
> relocate. That entire after-decree arc — modification, contempt, enforcement/collection, relocation, emancipation,
> college support, interstate, appeal — has its own spine in **`references/post-judgment-roadmap.md`.** When the case is
> past the decree, route there, not here.

## With-children layer (adds to either track)

### Custody — **Albright** best-interest factors
Decided on the Albright factors (see `references/albright-factors.md`). Custody types (joint/sole) and the
family-violence presumption are statutory (§ 93-5-24). **One-issue rule:** custody never shares a filing with support or
property. *(A 2026 joint-custody legislative change surfaced in research from a non-official source —
`[CONFIRM against the enrolled bill / amended § 93-5-24 before relying on it]`.)*

### Child support — **Miss. Code § 43-19-101** guidelines
Rebuttable-presumption percentages of the paying parent's **adjusted gross income (AGI)**:

| Children due support | % of AGI |
|---|---|
| 1 | **14%** |
| 2 | **20%** |
| 3 | **22%** |
| 4 | **24%** |
| 5+ | **26%** |

`[VERIFIED — FindLaw published MS Code §43-19-101, read verbatim 2026-05-23: the percentage table is exact]`
The guidelines **apply unless** the court makes a **written/specific finding** that applying them would be **unjust or
inappropriate** under the §43-19-103 criteria (§43-19-101(2)). AGI = gross income from all sources **minus** statutory
deductions, then ÷12 for the monthly figure (§43-19-101(3)). If AGI is **more than $100,000 or less than $10,000/yr**,
the court **shall make a written finding** as to whether applying the guidelines is reasonable (§43-19-101(4) — verbatim).
Use `filing-templates.md` → *Child-Support Guideline Worksheet*.

### Financial disclosure — **UCCR 8.05**
Mandatory in domestic cases with economic issues. Each party files a sworn statement of **income, monthly expenses,
assets, liabilities**, with the **most recent tax return** attached. `[VERIFIED — official chancery 8.05 form]`

### Parenting class / extra cooling-off
**No statewide statutory parenting-class mandate** and no cooling-off beyond the § 93-5-2(4) 60-day ID wait —
**but these are commonly set by local rule.** `[CHECK LOCAL RULE — your court's district]`
