# Post-Judgment Roadmap — after the decree, until the child is grown (Mississippi)

> **Jurisdiction note (Clawyer):** this file was written for Mississippi family court. Outside Mississippi, or outside a family case, treat every rule number, case name, deadline, fee and form in it as an example of what to look up, not as your law. Family-law steps in it do not apply to other kinds of case. Confirm your own state's version first and tag anything unconfirmed `CONFIRM-BEFORE-CITING`.

> **The case does not end at the final judgment.** A family-law matter with a child runs until the child is
> **emancipated** (in MS, generally age 21 — see below), and money/property issues can resurface for years. This file is
> the **after-decree spine**: every kind of proceeding that can happen *after* the divorce/custody judgment, in plain
> order, so a self-represented person is never told "your case is over" when it isn't.
>
> Pairs with `references/divorce-roadmap.md` (the *before-decree* spine). Same rules: **one issue per filing**, plain
> language to the person, correct chancery language in the filing, a **NEXT ACTION** every time. Research/drafting
> support — **not legal advice.**

## The court keeps power over the case
A chancery court that entered the decree keeps **continuing jurisdiction**: *"The court may afterwards, on petition,
change the decree, and make from time to time such new decrees as the case may require."*
`[VERIFIED — FindLaw published Miss. Code § 93-5-23, current to Jan 1 2025, read verbatim 2026-05-24]` Custody orders are
made under **§ 93-5-24**. So custody, visitation, and support are **never permanently fixed** while the child is a minor —
they can be modified when the law's test is met.

## How to use this file
1. **Name what happened** since the decree — someone broke the order, circumstances changed, a parent wants to move, the
   child is aging out, or money is owed. Match it to a track below.
2. **Check the legal test for that track** *before* drafting — modification and contempt each have a specific standard;
   a petition that doesn't meet the test gets dismissed.
3. **One issue per petition.** A modification of custody does **not** share a filing with a contempt for unpaid support.
4. **Watch the special summons.** Post-judgment matters are **Rule 81(d)** actions with their own summons and short
   appearance clock (see the Rule 81 box) — a defective special summons is a real, fixable defect (and a real defense).

## Track P1 — Modify custody / visitation
**Test (case law):** the moving parent must show (1) a **material change in circumstances** in the custodial home that
(2) **adversely affects the child**, and that (3) modification is in the child's **best interest** (re-applying the
**Albright** factors). A change that doesn't harm the child is not enough. `[CONFIRM BEFORE CITING — material-change /
adverse-effect standard is MS case law; pull the controlling case (e.g., a current MS Supreme Court / Court of Appeals
modification opinion) and verify the pinpoint before filing]` Albright itself may be cited without a fresh search.
- **Visitation** is modified on a lighter standard than custody (best-interest of the child; a material change is not
  always required to adjust a visitation *schedule*). `[CONFIRM BEFORE CITING]`
- **Restriction / supervised visitation** needs evidence the current arrangement endangers the child — not anger at the
  other parent. (See `references/response-arsenal.md` and `references/abuse-coercive-control.md`.)
- **Filing:** Petition to Modify Custody (Rule 81 special summons) → discovery if needed → hearing (Albright proof) →
  proposed modified order (run it through the **Order Integrity Reviewer**).

## Track P2 — Modify child support
**Test:** a **material change in circumstances** since the last order (e.g., a substantial, lasting change in either
parent's income, the child's needs, custody time). `[CONFIRM BEFORE CITING — material-change standard for support is MS
case law]` If met, the number is recomputed off the **§ 43-19-101** guideline percentages (1=14%, 2=20%, 3=22%, 4=24%,
5+=26% of adjusted gross income) `[VERIFIED — FindLaw published Miss. Code § 43-19-101, read verbatim 2026-05-23]`.
- Modification is **not retroactive** before the date the petition is filed — *file promptly* when income drops; an
  informal handshake to pay less does **not** change the order and arrears still accrue. `[CONFIRM BEFORE CITING]`
- **Filing:** Petition to Modify Support (Rule 81) + updated **UCCR 8.05** financial statement from each side.

## Track P3 — Modify alimony
- **Periodic (open-ended) alimony** is **modifiable** on a material change in circumstances. **Lump-sum / alimony in
  gross** (a fixed total) is generally a vested property right and **not modifiable**; **rehabilitative** alimony is
  modifiable in some circumstances. `[CONFIRM BEFORE CITING — MS alimony-modification distinctions are case law; verify
  the type and the controlling case before filing]` Armstrong factors frame the analysis (`references/armstrong-ferguson.md`).
- Cohabitation / remarriage of the recipient can terminate or reduce periodic alimony. `[CONFIRM BEFORE CITING]`

## Track P4 — Contempt (make them obey the order)
When the other party **willfully** disobeys a clear provision of the decree (withholds the child, won't pay support,
won't turn over property), the remedy is a **petition for contempt**.
- **Civil contempt** is coercive — the goal is compliance; the contemnor "carries the keys to the jail" by complying.
  The movant shows a clear order + non-compliance; the burden then shifts to the other party to show **inability** to
  comply (not mere unwillingness). `[CONFIRM BEFORE CITING — civil-contempt burden-shifting is MS case law]`
- Attorney's fees and the arrears can be awarded. **One issue per petition** — don't bundle contempt with a modification.
- **Filing:** Petition for Citation of Contempt (Rule 81 special summons) → hearing → order adjudicating contempt + purge
  terms.

## Track P5 — Enforce / collect support
A support arrearage has powerful collection tools — the person owed is rarely without options:
- **Judgment by operation of law:** any periodic support payment unpaid **≥ 30 days** *automatically* becomes a
  **judgment** for the amount due, with the **same effect as any other judgment**, a **lien on all the obligor's
  property**, and **full faith and credit** in other states. *No separate suit is required to fix the amount.*
  `[VERIFIED — FindLaw published Miss. Code § 93-11-71(1), read verbatim 2026-05-24]`
- **Income withholding** (wage garnishment) for support is available through the court / Department of Human Services.
  `[CONFIRM BEFORE CITING — withholding statute, e.g. § 93-11-103 et seq.; verify section + procedure]`
- **Retirement / pension** division from the decree is enforced through a **Qualified Domestic Relations Order (QDRO)** —
  if a QDRO was ordered but never entered, that is itself enforceable. `[CONFIRM BEFORE CITING]`
- **DHS Child Support (Title IV-D):** the Department of Human Services can enforce (and the court has **continuing
  jurisdiction** in any IV-D case). `[VERIFIED — § 93-11-65(6), read verbatim 2026-05-24]` License suspension and tax
  intercept are common IV-D tools. `[CONFIRM BEFORE CITING]`

## Track P6 — Relocation (a parent wants to move with the child)
Mississippi has **no single "relocation statute"** that flips custody automatically; a proposed move is evaluated as a
potential **material change** and on the child's **best interest (Albright)**, and may run up against any
notice/consent term already in the decree. **Read the decree first** for a relocation/notice clause. `[CONFIRM BEFORE
CITING — relocation is governed by MS case law + the parties' decree, not a dedicated statute; verify before filing]`
The other parent's tool against a bad-faith move is a **Petition to Modify** (Track P1) + (if the order was violated) a
**contempt** (Track P4) — separate filings.

## Track P7 — Emancipation / termination of support (the case's natural endpoint)
The duty to support a child **terminates upon emancipation.** Unless the support judgment says otherwise, emancipation
occurs when the child: **(i) turns 21**, (ii) marries, (iii) joins the military full-time, or (iv) is convicted of a
felony and sentenced to 2+ years. The court **may** also find emancipation when the child, having turned 18,
**discontinues full-time school** (unless disabled) or moves out, lives independently, works full-time, and stops
schooling. `[VERIFIED — FindLaw published Miss. Code § 93-11-65(8)(a)-(b), read verbatim 2026-05-24]`
- **Practical:** support does **not** stop by itself the day the child turns 21 if there's an arrearage or if the order
  set conditions — confirm against the decree, and file a **Petition to Determine Emancipation / Terminate Support** if
  the obligor needs the order formally ended. `[CONFIRM BEFORE CITING]`

## Track P8 — College / post-majority support
A MS court can address **college support** as part of the child-support obligation, but the duty is tied to the support
period and the child's relationship with the parent; it is **not** an automatic open-ended entitlement past 21.
`[CONFIRM BEFORE CITING — college/post-majority support is MS case law; verify the controlling rule and any conditions
before filing or relying on it]`

## Track P9 — Interstate (a party or the order is in another state)
Use **UIFSA** (Uniform Interstate Family Support Act) to **register** a foreign support order for enforcement or
modification, and the **UCCJEA** for interstate **custody** jurisdiction. Only one state has continuing exclusive
jurisdiction over support at a time. `[CONFIRM BEFORE CITING — verify MS's UIFSA/UCCJEA code sections + registration
procedure before filing]`

## Track P10 — Reconsider / appeal a ruling
- **Motion to alter/amend or for new trial — MRCP 59** (strict short deadline after entry of the judgment).
  `[CONFIRM BEFORE CITING — verify the Rule 59 day-count]`
- **Relief from judgment — MRCP 60** (clerical errors under 60(a); limited grounds like fraud/mistake under 60(b)).
  `[CONFIRM BEFORE CITING]`
- **Appeal to the Mississippi Supreme Court** (assignable to the Court of Appeals) — a notice of appeal with a short
  deadline after entry. `[CONFIRM BEFORE CITING — verify the appeal deadline (MRAP) before relying on it]`
- Before appealing, ask: is this better fixed by a **Rule 60(a)** correction or a **modification** going forward? Appeals
  are slow and review is deferential to the chancellor.

## Rule 81(d) — the special-summons clock for post-judgment matters
Custody, support, alimony, **modification, and contempt** are **Rule 81(d)** actions: they use a **special Rule 81
summons** (not the ordinary Rule 4 answer track), which sets a **specific date to appear** rather than a 30-day answer
window. Modification and contempt are triable on **7 days'** notice (81(d)(2)); custody/support *actions* use 30 days
(81(d)(1)). A defective or missing special summons is a genuine procedural defect — to raise, or to cure.
`[VERIFIED — MRCP 81(d) read s4 2026-05-24; re-confirm the exact day-counts against the current rule before filing]`

## One-issue rule (carries over from the divorce)
Never blend post-judgment issues in one filing: **modification ≠ contempt ≠ enforcement.** Separate petitions, each on
its own ground, each with its own Rule 81 summons. A blended petition gives the other side an easy reason to get part of
it dismissed (see `references/operating-doctrine.md`).
