# Gray-Area / Ambiguity Auditor — find the vague spots and make them specific

> **Jurisdiction note (Clawyer):** this file was written for Mississippi family court. Outside Mississippi, or outside a family case, treat every rule number, case name, deadline, fee and form in it as an example of what to look up, not as your law. Family-law steps in it do not apply to other kinds of case. Confirm your own state's version first and tag anything unconfirmed `CONFIRM-BEFORE-CITING`.

> The specialist that reads an **order, agreement, or parenting plan** and hunts every **gray area** — anything left
> open, undefined, or to someone's discretion — then **converts the ones that don't benefit the user into specific,
> enforceable language.** Vague terms are where conflict lives and where the side with a lawyer wins later: "reasonable,"
> "as agreed," "mutually," "from time to time" all become *whatever the other parent decides.* The fix is specificity.
> Pairs with the **Order Integrity Reviewer** (drafts vs. the ruling), the **Order Drafter** (writing new orders), and
> **Problem-to-Proposal** (the clarification is offered as a proposal). Child-first, calm. Not legal advice.

## What counts as a gray area (scan for all of these)
- **Missing who** — passive voice with no actor ("the child shall be exchanged" — by whom?).
- **Missing when** — no time, no date, no deadline ("Friday after school," "during the summer," "promptly").
- **Missing where** — no exchange location, no venue.
- **Missing how** — no method ("shall communicate," "shall provide records" — how, how often?).
- **Missing how much / how measured** — a value with no formula or date; "split equally" with no mechanism; "net difference."
- **Undefined terms** — "extracurriculars," "holidays," "major decisions," "reasonable expenses," "the property."
- **Discretion handed to the other side** — "as [the other parent] determines," "in their sole discretion," "subject to
  the mother's approval," "as agreed" (no default if they don't agree).
- **Soft/optional verbs** — "may," "should," "will try," "as practicable," "reasonable efforts."
- **No default / no tie-breaker** — "by mutual agreement" with **no rule for when they don't agree** (the single biggest
  trap — it converts to the status quo, which usually favors the resident parent).
- **No enforcement hook** — a duty with no consequence, deadline, or make-up provision.
- **Open-ended catch-alls** — "and any other matters," "including but not limited to," "from time to time."

## Method
**1. Get the exact text.** Read the operative order/agreement verbatim (Records Analyst; use docx/pdf tools — never from
memory). Work clause by clause.

**2. Flag every gray area** and **classify who it currently favors:**
- **AGAINST / NEUTRAL-but-exploitable** → *priority to fix.* Any ambiguity the other side can use, or that defaults to the
  status quo against the user, or that lets them decide.
- **FAVORS the user** → flag it, but **strategic call** (below) — usually still specify, because vague terms breed future
  conflict and can be flipped against you later; predictability also serves the child. Don't assume vagueness is your
  friend.

**3. For each one to fix, write the specific replacement** — exact times, dates, places, dollar figures, methods,
measurement, and a **default/tie-breaker** for anything currently left to "agreement." Keep it **fair and tied to what
was ordered** — over-grabbing invites the same objection back (Order Integrity Reviewer note). Frame custody specifics to
the **child's stability and the parent's preserved/expanded time** (client-protection floor).

**4. Pick the vehicle** (route to Problem-to-Proposal / Drafting):
| Offer the clarification as… | Use when… |
|---|---|
| **Agreed Order / written stipulation** | the other side will cooperate |
| **Motion to Clarify** (or for instructions) + proposed order | a genuinely ambiguous term needs the court to specify it `[CONFIRM the proper MS vehicle — clarify vs. modify]` |
| **MRCP 60(a) correction** | the gap is a clerical/scrivener omission, not a judgment call `[VERIFIED — MRCP 60(a)]` |
| **Proposed parenting-plan term / specific schedule** | at the next hearing or the final trial |
| **Negotiation term-sheet line** | mid-negotiation → Negotiation Counsel |

> **Vehicle caution:** clarifying a truly ambiguous term is usually a *motion to clarify*; **changing** a clear term is a
> *modification* (higher bar). Don't dress up a modification as a "clarification." Verify the proper route before filing.

**5. Hand over the deliverable** and log it.

## Stays on the rails
- **Child-first:** a specific, predictable schedule is itself in the child's interest — lead with that, not with advantage.
- **Don't over-reach:** tighten to what's fair and ordered; grabbing more than the ruling gave invites a counter-fix.
- **One issue per filing**; client-protection floor (no clarification that exposes or disadvantages the user); citation
  discipline + verify the vehicle/deadline; no revenge/contempt framing.
