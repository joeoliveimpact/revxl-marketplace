# TRIAL-CRAFT DOCTRINE — bench-trial method for the self-represented

> 🧭 METHOD IS UNIVERSAL — distilled from a research campaign that read the
> classical cross-examination schools (Younger's Ten Commandments, Pozner & Dodd's
> chapter method, MacCarthy, Mauet) and converted them for one specific situation:
> a self-represented parent trying a family-law bench trial. Verify any rule of
> procedure against YOUR forum's rules before relying on it — rule numbers below
> are deliberately omitted because they vary by state.

---

## 1. THE BENCH-TRIAL ADJUSTMENT (everything else flows from this)

There is no jury. The audience is one professional fact-finder who has read the
file, has seen ten thousand hearings, and decides both the facts and the law.
Consequences:

- **Drama loses. Paper wins.** The theatrical cross that works on TV reads as
  aggression in a bench trial, and aggression from the higher-conflict party is
  evidence against them.
- **The judge's questions are the map.** When the judge interrupts to ask
  something, that is the court telling you what it cares about. Answer THAT,
  fully, before returning to your outline.
- **Openings and closings may not exist.** Many benches skip them or cut them
  short. Ask once ("may I give a brief opening — no more than a few minutes?"),
  accept the answer, argue never. Build the case so the PAPER carries the closing:
  a proposed findings-of-fact document beats a speech.
- **Findings of fact are the real closing argument.** If your forum permits
  requesting written findings, file that request BEFORE the court begins its
  opinion. A per-factor proposed findings document walks the judge through your
  case in the format the judge must ultimately write.

## 2. CROSS-EXAMINATION — the distilled method

- **One fact per question.** Not one topic — one FACT. The question contains the
  fact; the witness's only job is yes or no.
- **Leading always.** Every question ends "Correct?" / "Right?" / "Is that
  accurate?" Never "why," never "what happened next."
- **Topic bands, not chronology.** Group questions by theme, foundation questions
  first, payoff question last. Each band locks one point.
- **Stop after the kill.** When the admission lands, STOP. The silence after a
  winning answer is a tool. The one-question-too-many un-wins the point.
- **Trap on truth.** Every question traps a documented truth or exposes a
  documented lie. If you cannot pin the answer to an exhibit or prior sworn
  statement, the question does not get asked.
- **Never argue the inference on cross.** State the fact, stop. The argument
  belongs in closing (or the findings document). The judge draws the inference —
  and an inference the judge draws is worth double one you hand over.
- **Impeachment by their own words:** prior sworn statements are gold (in many
  forums a prior inconsistent SWORN statement is substantive evidence, not just
  impeachment). Written party statements (co-parenting app messages, texts,
  emails) are usually admissible against the party who wrote them. Build the
  cross so every impeachment is exhibit-anchored: quote → "that's your message?"
  → next.

## 3. DIRECT EXAMINATION AND WITNESS PREP

- **Treat every witness as hostile until proven friendly.** Friendly witnesses
  turn under pressure. Build the full adverse cross pack for everyone; soften to
  direct only for the ones who hold up.
- **A witness whose posture you do not know is a decision, not a hope.** If the
  packet builds someone as friendly in one section and hostile in another, that
  contradiction must be resolved BEFORE the subpoena deadline, not at the podium.
- Direct is short questions, no leading, exhibit-anchored: who/what/when/where,
  hand the exhibit, "what is this?", "is it accurate?", move on.

## 4. THE SELF-REPRESENTED PARTY AS WITNESS

The strangest moment of a pro se trial: putting yourself on. Prepare all three
formats because the court chooses, not you:
- **Narrative** — you tell your story in sections; have a written outline of the
  facts each section must land.
- **Self-directed Q&A** — you ask yourself questions and answer them; drill it so
  it doesn't sound absurd.
- **Judge-led** — the court just asks you questions; your job is short, direct,
  factual answers.

Script the sentence that puts you on the stand ("Your Honor, I call myself as a
witness and ask the Court's guidance on the format it prefers"). Prepare the
exhibit-from-the-stand mechanics: how you mark, show opposing counsel, hand up,
and move admission while you are the one testifying — ask the court reporter or
clerk in advance what the local mechanics are.

## 5. THE TIME BUDGET

Count the minutes honestly. A one-day trial is ~180 usable minutes a side after
recesses and the other side's case. Build a minute map: every witness gets a
budget; every cross band gets a priority rank so when time compresses (it always
compresses) you cut from the bottom, never the top. The single most probable
trial-day surprise is not an ambush — it is the clock.

## 6. OBJECTIONS — make and meet

Carry a one-card list of the six you will actually use: hearsay, foundation,
speculation, relevance, asked-and-answered, mischaracterizes-the-evidence. For
each: the two-second form ("Objection — hearsay") and the two-sentence response
when it's aimed at you. In a bench trial, object sparingly — the judge knows what
is inadmissible — but object EVERY time on the points you must preserve for
appeal, and get a ruling on the record.

## 7. THE SCENARIO MATRIX (contingency doctrine)

Before trial, build one table of the fifteen-to-twenty things that can go wrong —
pre-trial (a continuance motion, an emergency filing, a witness unserved),
trial-day (a surprise exhibit, a witness turning, the day not finishing, getting
baited into anger), and self-inflicted (your own exhibit misfiled, your key
question gated on an unsigned foundation document). For each: the warning sign,
the verbatim first sentence you say, and the artifact you grab. Anchor every
likelihood to a dated instance from the record, never a guess. Build one-page
playbooks for the top five only.

## 8. EVIDENCE FOUNDATIONS THE PRO SE FORGETS

- **Recordings:** a recording needs (1) lawfulness under your state's consent
  law, (2) speaker identification — a participant to the call can identify voices
  from personal familiarity; get that identification into a signed declaration
  BEFORE trial, never at the podium, and (3) accuracy — the recording is the
  evidence; a machine transcript is a finding aid, and any quoted language must
  be confirmed by ear first.
- **Business records** (school portals, bank records, phone logs): they need a
  custodian or a certification. If your forum allows certification in lieu of
  live testimony, the certification usually carries an advance-notice
  requirement — calendar it backward from trial.
- **Screenshots:** capture same-day, preserve the original file, hash it if you
  can. Portal records (attendance codes, timestamps) are editable after the fact
  — the screenshot freezes what the record said on the day.

## 9. CONDUCT DOCTRINE UNDER FIRE

The other side may escalate as trial approaches: withheld exchanges, manufactured
emergencies, new allegations timed to hearings. The doctrine:

- **Route everything through the court, never self-help.** The contrast between
  the parent who files and the parent who withholds IS the custody argument.
- **Document, don't confront.** One neutral written question ("What does the
  court order state?") outperforms ten paragraphs of argument — especially when
  it goes unanswered.
- **Show, don't say.** Lay the dates side by side and stop. No adjectives, no
  motive words ("weaponizing," "manufactured," "retaliation") in anything filed
  or said — every one of them moves the judge's attention from their conduct to
  your tone.
- **The precise claim beats the broad one.** "The allegation is false" (provable)
  is a different claim from "she made the false report" (usually unprovable).
  Assert exactly what the record supports, decline the rest, and the part you
  decline makes the part you assert more credible.
