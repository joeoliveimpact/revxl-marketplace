# Evidence Evaluator & Trial Prep Binder (Bompiani)

> **Jurisdiction note (Clawyer):** this file was written for Mississippi family court. Outside Mississippi, or outside a family case, treat every rule number, case name, deadline, fee and form in it as an example of what to look up, not as your law. Family-law steps in it do not apply to other kinds of case. Confirm your own state's version first and tag anything unconfirmed `CONFIRM-BEFORE-CITING`.

> 🧭 **METHOD IS UNIVERSAL — the named authorities are the Mississippi example.** The evaluation method, exhibit index, and binder here apply in every state. Where this file says tie an exhibit to an "Albright / Armstrong / Ferguson factor" or check the "Mississippi Rules of Evidence," that is the **Mississippi worked example** — for a non-MS case, tie to the **resolved state-law profile**'s factors and check that state's rules of evidence and recording-consent law (see `state-law-resolver.md`).

For the Evidence Specialist and the Hearing-Prep Coach. Principle: **evidence beats argument; if it cannot be proven,
it does not get filed.**

## Bompiani 5-Step Evaluator — run every exhibit through this before using it

For each piece of evidence, answer all five. If any answer is weak, the exhibit isn't ready — say what would fix it.

1. **What does it prove?** State the single fact it establishes. If it proves nothing specific, it's clutter — cut it.
2. **Is it authentic and admissible?** Can its origin be shown (who created it, when, how it was kept)? OFW logs,
   screenshots with timestamps, certified records authenticate more easily than hearsay or unsourced notes.
3. **Which factor does it support?** Tie it to a specific factor in the resolved state-law profile (Mississippi example:
   an Albright / Armstrong / Ferguson factor) or a specific contested fact. Evidence that maps to nothing in the filing
   doesn't go in.
4. **What's the counter-read?** How will opposing counsel characterize it? If it cuts both ways or invites
   a damaging response, weigh whether to use it.
5. **Does it stay calm and child-centered?** Reject anything that reads as revenge, accusation, or emotion. The judge
   sees the exhibit, not the feeling behind it.

## Exhibit index format

Build a numbered table — never loose attachments:

| Ex. | Description | Source / how authenticated | What it proves | Factor it supports |
|-----|-------------|----------------------------|----------------|--------------------|

Commonly strong categories: co-parenting-app call logs & message history (e.g., OurFamilyWizard); parenting-time
denial patterns (ordered schedule vs. actual enforcement — present as a calendar); financial records & asset tracing;
contradiction documentation (a statement vs. a document that disproves it). **Apply to the facts in the user's case
profile (`case-profile.md`)** to identify which categories are strongest here. **Document daily** — gaps in the record
are gaps in proof.

## Types of evidence, hearsay & objections (Bompiani E-D-O-P)

Bompiani's evidence workflow is **E-D-O-P: Evaluate → Document → Organize → Prepare** (the 5-Step Evaluator above is the
"Evaluate" stage). Quality evidence is **admissible, relevant, reliable, and persuasive.**

- **Know the types:** *direct* vs *circumstantial*; *physical* (documents, photos, records); *documentary*;
  *testimonial* (what a witness can say). Match each fact to the strongest type available.
- **Hearsay awareness:** an out-of-court statement offered to prove the matter asserted may be excluded. Prefer the
  **original record** (the OFW message itself, the certified record) over someone *describing* it. Flag likely-hearsay
  exhibits and find the admissible source `[CHECK LOCAL RULE on exceptions]`.
- **Anticipate objections:** for each exhibit, ask how the other side will object (authenticity, hearsay, relevance) and
  have the answer ready before the hearing.
- **Recordings — never bless their legality without a verify tag.** Whenever the person has an **audio/phone/video
  recording**, the *first* thing to flag is whether it was **lawfully made** — this controls one-party vs. all-party
  consent and whether the recording is usable or itself exposes the maker to liability. **Always state this as something
  to confirm, tagged `[CONFIRM BEFORE CITING — verify MS recording-consent law (federal + Miss. wiretap/eavesdropping
  statute) and any all-party-consent state on the other end of the call before using or relying on it]` — never assert
  "it's legal / you can use it."** Then handle authentication separately (who recorded it, when, unedited, foundation
  witness). A recording of a *child*, or made by recording an extension/other party's call, raises extra red flags —
  surface them rather than encouraging use.

Verify any specific evidentiary rule against the forum state's rules of evidence (Mississippi example: the Mississippi
Rules of Evidence) before relying on it — never state an evidence rule as certain without checking.

## Trial Prep Binder — organize before every hearing

Assemble in this order so the user can find anything in seconds and present calmly:

1. **One-page case summary** — what this hearing decides (one issue), the relief sought, the date.
2. **The filing(s) at issue** — the motion and any response, tabbed.
3. **Exhibit index** (above) + the exhibits in order, tabbed and numbered.
4. **Anticipated questions + answer scripts** — see below.
5. **Exhibit-to-question map** — for each likely question, the exhibit number that answers it.
6. **Proposed order** — what you want the judge to sign.
7. **Day-of checklist** — what to bring, when to arrive, where to file/check in, copies for the court and opposing party.

## Answer-script discipline (Hearing-Prep Coach, Tier 1)

- **Short answers win.** Answer the question asked, then stop. Silence after a winning answer is a tool.
- Anticipate the chancellor's likely questions (the judge needs to see the child's stability and a workable
  schedule) and opposing counsel's (expect questions designed to provoke; tailor to opposing counsel's known
  tactics per the case profile).
- Prepare the calm response to provocation: acknowledge, redirect to the schedule/the evidence, do not argue.
- **Calm parents win. Reactive parents lose.** Never coach the child. This is preparation, not therapy.
