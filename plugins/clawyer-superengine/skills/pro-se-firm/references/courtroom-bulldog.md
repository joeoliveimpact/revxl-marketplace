# Courtroom Bulldog — hold the line, never get walked over

> **Jurisdiction note (Clawyer):** this file was written for Mississippi family court. Outside Mississippi, or outside a family case, treat every rule number, case name, deadline, fee and form in it as an example of what to look up, not as your law. Family-law steps in it do not apply to other kinds of case. Confirm your own state's version first and tag anything unconfirmed `CONFIRM-BEFORE-CITING`.

> **Bulldog in substance, calm in tone.** The aggression is in the *relentlessness* — on the burden of proof, the rules,
> the record, and the other side's own words — **never in the voice.** The reactive, loud party gets dinged; the calm,
> prepared party who **refuses to let anything slide** wins. This file is the always-on reflex layer: it makes sure the
> self-represented person never **silently absorbs** an overreach, a misstatement, or an unproven claim.
>
> Pairs with `references/adversarial-combat.md` (overall fight posture) and `references/courtroom-scripts.md` (the
> read-aloud cross/direct/objection packet). Everything here stays inside the firm's rules and the **client-protection
> floor**. This is procedure/skills coaching — not legal advice.

## The assertiveness floor (the core rule)
**Every overreach gets a calm, specific, on-the-record response — none passes silently.** When the other side misstates a
fact, asserts something not in evidence, hands over a document and pushes for an instant answer, claims a deadline that
isn't in a rule or order, or slips scope into a proposed order, the firm's default is **not** "let it go to keep the
peace." It is: name it, cite the source, and hold the line — in a level voice. Silence reads as agreement. Civility is
kept (it *is* leverage with the judge); the spine is not.

## In the courtroom — real-time hold-the-line reflexes
1. **Make them carry their burden.** Don't concede an element because it's asserted confidently. "Your Honor, that hasn't
   been established." They prove it, or it isn't a fact.
2. **Object on substance — the few that matter, used sparingly.** Plain forms (confirm against the MS Rules of Evidence
   before relying — `[CHECK MS RULES OF EVIDENCE]`):
   - *"Objection, hearsay."* (out-of-court statement offered for its truth)
   - *"Objection, foundation / authentication."* (no proof the document/voice is what they claim)
   - *"Objection, relevance."*
   - *"Objection, leading."* (only on **their** direct of a friendly witness)
   - *"Objection — misstates the evidence / facts not in evidence."*
   - *"Objection, speculation / calls for a legal conclusion."* · *"Asked and answered."* · *"Argumentative."*
   A pro se who over-objects loses credibility — object when it **matters**, not reflexively.
3. **Correct the record, immediately and with the cite.** When opposing counsel tells the judge something untrue:
   *"Your Honor, the record / Exhibit ___ actually shows ___."* Calm, specific, sourced. Never let a misstatement become
   the judge's working assumption.
4. **Never get rushed.** If a document is handed up: *"May I have a moment to review that, Your Honor?"* **Never respond
   to, agree to, or sign anything you have not read.** Pressure to answer fast is itself the tactic.
5. **Don't stipulate away advantage.** Do not agree to facts, exhibits, or schedule changes on the fly. *"I don't
   stipulate to that,"* or *"I'd like to address that in writing."* Off-the-cuff concessions are how ground is lost.
6. **Get YOUR evidence in.** Move each exhibit into evidence. If an objection is sustained, lay more foundation — or make
   an **offer of proof** (*"Your Honor, if permitted, the exhibit would show ___"*) so the point is **preserved for
   appeal** even if excluded. Facts the judge never hears can't help you.
7. **Hold them to their own words.** Deemed/actual admissions (MRCP 36 — silence = admission in 30 days), prior sworn
   statements, the GAL's adopted recommendation, and the **exact wording of the order** are anchors. When testimony
   drifts from them, pin it (impeachment move in `courtroom-scripts.md`).
8. **Ask the court for findings.** Request specific findings on each governing factor (e.g., each Albright factor; the
   reasons for any departure from a mandatory GAL recommendation — *S.N.C. v. J.R.D.* `[CONFIRM pinpoint]`). Findings box
   the other side in and preserve the record for appeal.
9. **Preserve, don't argue.** State the objection clearly *for the record*; if overruled, you've preserved it — **move
   on.** Arguing with the chancellor costs credibility; a clean record costs them nothing later.
10. **On their cross of you:** answer only what's asked; *"That's not what I said,"* if mischaracterized; expand the
    truth on your own narrative/redirect — not by fighting the lawyer.

## Handling the other side OUTSIDE court (where most pro ses actually get steamrolled)
- **Proposed orders — never sign on trust.** Run the **Order Integrity Reviewer** three-way check every round; respond
  with redlines and the **source-of-truth quote**; refuse scope creep. (This case's documented pattern is exactly this.)
- **Deadlines — verify before you comply.** Don't accept a deadline the other side asserts; confirm it against the rule
  or order (verify-real-standards). A made-up deadline is a pressure tactic.
- **Correspondence — concede nothing.** Answer only the documented issue; don't argue, apologize into liability, or
  agree in writing without deciding deliberately. Every message is a future exhibit (Communication Command).
- **Settlement — never under pressure.** Measure every term against the goal **and** the child; counter overreach with a
  specific alternative; get it **in writing** — no verbal deals.
- **Discovery — enforce it.** If they stonewall, move to compel (within the rules); use RFA deemed-admitted; don't let
  them run out the clock on you.
- **Procedure — comply AND weaponize.** Hold them to every rule they'd hold you to (service, form, notice). Your
  compliance is armor; their non-compliance is your motion.

## "Walked-over" red flags → the calm bulldog response (auto-detect)
| When this happens | Do NOT | Calm bulldog response |
|---|---|---|
| OC states a "fact" not in evidence | nod / stay silent | "Your Honor, that's not in the record." |
| A document is handed to you for an answer | answer on the spot | "May I take a moment to read it, Your Honor?" |
| A proposed order arrives | sign / approve to be agreeable | Run the integrity check; redline; quote the ruling. |
| They assert a deadline | scramble to meet it | Verify the rule/order first; then respond. |
| They mischaracterize the order | argue from memory | Read the order's exact words aloud. |
| They bury you in paper | panic-respond to all of it | Triage; answer the documented issue; calendar each deadline. |
| OC is rude / provoking | counterpunch in kind | Stay level; answer the issue; let it sit in the record. |
| Judge seems to lean against you | argue/emotional | Make the record calmly; request findings; preserve for appeal. |

## Stays on the rails (non-negotiable)
- **Calm voice always** — bulldog is substance, not volume (Bompiani: calm parent wins, reactive parent loses).
- **Civil to opposing counsel, firm on the merits** — civility protects credibility, which is leverage; firmness is not
  rudeness.
- All firm guardrails apply: no contempt theatrics, no crime allegations, no gender framing, no improper ex parte
  contact, citation discipline, **one issue per filing**, and the **client-protection floor** (don't let "winning a
  point" expose the person or risk the child or the parenting time).
