# Evidence Organization — the firm organizes it FOR the person (baby steps, every state)

Universal method, no state's law. The firm **does this work** — the person hands over their pile and the firm sorts,
labels, and orders it. Crawl before you walk (`operating-doctrine.md`): one small step at a time, never a wall.
Auto-fires whenever the person uploads documents, mentions evidence, or a hearing/trial is coming. Pairs with
`evidence-evaluator.md` (judge each piece) and `courtroom-notes.md` (use it at the hearing).

## The promise (say this first, plainly)
"You don't have to sort anything. Send me everything you've got — court papers, texts, the co-parenting-app logs,
photos, receipts, all of it, in any order. Sorting it is my job. I'll turn your pile into a labeled, numbered set you can
hand a judge."

## Step 1 — get it all in one place (the person's only job)
Tell the person, one line at a time:
- "Drop everything into a single folder, or just upload it here. Don't rename or sort it."
- "Don't leave anything out because you think it's small or bad. I decide what matters, and I need the bad facts to
  protect you from them."
Convert everything to readable text first (`markdown-intake.md`; OCR scanned images with `scripts/ocr_pdf.py`).

## Step 2 — the firm builds the MASTER evidence set (once, then kept current)
The firm makes one running table — the single home for all evidence in the case:

| # | Date | What it is | Source / how we'd authenticate | What it proves | Issue it supports | Status |
|---|------|-----------|--------------------------------|----------------|-------------------|--------|

- **Number every item once** and keep that number for the life of the case (so "Exhibit 14" always means the same thing).
- Run each item through the **Bompiani 5-step** (`evidence-evaluator.md`): if it's weak, mark what would fix it.
- Sort into plain buckets the person understands: **Kids / Money / Communication / Court papers / Other.**
- Keep a **running evidence log** and add to it as new things happen. "Document daily — gaps in the record are gaps in
  proof." The firm updates the master set every time the person sends something new.

## Step 3 — per-HEARING set (a small subset, one issue at a time)
A single hearing usually decides ONE issue. So for each hearing the firm pulls a **small subset** from the master set:
- Only the exhibits that prove THIS hearing's one issue (keep it tight; a judge has minutes, not hours).
- Re-numbered for that hearing as Exhibit 1, 2, 3... in the order you'll use them, with a one-line "what it proves" each.
- Built into the **Trial Prep Binder** for that date (`evidence-evaluator.md`) + the one-page hearing sheet
  (`courtroom-notes.md`).
- The firm tells the person exactly which printed exhibits to bring and how many copies (one for the judge, one for the
  other side, one for you) — `ASK THE CLERK` for the court's copy rule.

## Step 3.5 — the Exhibit Quick-Reference ("the lineup" / grab sheet) — the FIRST page of the binder

This is the page that keeps the person from freezing at the podium. Real-world problem it solves: even with everything
neatly tabbed and separated, under stress people **forget the lineup** and waste time hunting for "which tab was that?"
The grab sheet is a one-page, large-type table of contents you glance at to know **instantly which tab to grab.**

| Tab # | Exhibit (plain name) | Proves (3–4 words) | Grab it when... |
|-------|----------------------|--------------------|-----------------|
| 1 | Missed-pickup texts | her unreliability | she denies missing pickups |
| 2 | School pickup log | I do the daily care | your testimony on daily care |
| 3 | Drinking-during-her-time photo | safety concern | the safety part of cross |

- **Sort by tab number** (the physical order in the binder) so finding any exhibit is instant — never by type or topic.
- **The "Grab it when..." column is the whole point:** it tells the person the exact moment to reach for each tab, so
  they never hunt. It also doubles as their running order for the hearing.
- **One page, large type.** If it spills past one page, that is the signal there are too many exhibits for this hearing —
  trim to the strongest. (For the final trial, make one grab sheet **per issue-tab**.)
- Put it as the **very first page**, and keep a second copy loose on the podium. Tab it so you can always flip back to it.

## Step 4 — the FINAL-trial master set (everything, tabbed by issue)
The final trial decides all the reserved issues at once, so the set is bigger and organized **issue-by-issue in tabbed
sections** (never blended in argument — same one-issue discipline, just under one cover):
- A tab per issue (custody, parenting time, support, property, etc.), each with its own ordered, numbered exhibits.
- A master exhibit index up front (every exhibit, its number, what it proves, which issue/factor it ties to in the
  resolved state-law profile).
- An exhibit-to-question map and exhibit-to-witness map so the person can find the right paper in seconds at the podium.
- The firm assembles the whole binder and gives a print-and-tab checklist, one step at a time.

## Step 5 — keep it safe and current
- Keep the digital master set backed up (it's the person's case in one place).
- Every new event: add to the log, re-evaluate, slot it into the master set, and note which upcoming hearing it serves.
- Before any hearing, the firm re-checks the per-hearing subset against the latest master set so nothing new is missed.

## Crawl reassurance (the firm says this)
"Send me the pile. I'll number it, sort it, tell you which pieces win and which need shoring up, and build the exact set
you carry into each hearing and the bigger one for the final. You never have to organize it yourself. One step at a time."
