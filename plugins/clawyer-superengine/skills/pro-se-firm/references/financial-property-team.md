# Financial & Property Team — Role Playbooks

> **Jurisdiction note (Clawyer):** this file was written for Mississippi family court. Outside Mississippi, or outside a family case, treat every rule number, case name, deadline, fee and form in it as an example of what to look up, not as your law. Family-law steps in it do not apply to other kinds of case. Confirm your own state's version first and tag anything unconfirmed `CONFIRM-BEFORE-CITING`.

How the firm-in-a-box works the money and the property in a marital case. Each role below is a **method playbook** —
how that specialist works, what they produce, and how far a pro-se litigant can take it alone. These are not
legal-authority documents; they assert no holdings. The only case names used are Albright (custody), Armstrong
(rehabilitative support), Ferguson (equitable distribution). Every other authority, deadline, fee, or guideline
percentage is left as a placeholder for the user to supply, tagged `[CONFIRM BEFORE CITING]` or `[CHECK LOCAL RULE]`.

> **READ FIRST — two reflexes that must show up in every money/property answer:**
> 1. **Order of operations (always state it when you explain property division):** the chancellor **(1) classifies**
>    each asset/debt marital vs. separate → **(2) values** the marital estate → **(3) divides** it equitably under
>    **Ferguson** ("equitable" = *fair*, **not automatically 50/50**) → and **only then (4) considers alimony**, and only
>    if the division still leaves a spouse with a **deficit**. Even a pure "how is the house/401k/business split?"
>    question gets told where alimony sits in the sequence — the property award size is what decides whether alimony is
>    reached. `[CONFIRM BEFORE CITING — standard MS chancery order of operations; verify the controlling case before filing]`
> 2. **Proof is silent-running:** when the goal is to *prove* hidden income or undisclosed assets, the records come
>    through **discovery run on the don't-telegraph protocol** (`references/discovery-strategy.md`) — ask for small,
>    neutral, documentable facts without revealing the theory, prove each number 2–3 ways, and **never** frame it as a
>    crime/fraud accusation. Always pair a tracing plan with "…and here's how we get it without tipping them off."

## Forensic Accountant / Financial Analyst
> **Deep playbook:** for the full hidden-money hunt — the four reconstruction methods, the concealment-scheme checklist,
> the document set, and the findings outputs — use **`references/forensic-financial.md`** (the Forensic Financial Analyst
> owns this). The summary below is the bench overview.

**Job:** Reconstruct the marital cash picture from primary records — find unreported income, undisclosed accounts, and lifestyle spending that the sworn financial disclosures do not explain.
**Method / workflow:**
1. Gather the source set: tax returns, W-2s/1099s, all bank and credit-card statements, pay stubs, the opposing party's sworn financial statement.
2. Build a month-by-month inflow/outflow ledger from the statements — never from a summary someone wrote.
3. Run a lifestyle-vs-income test: does reported income support observed spending? Flag every gap.
4. Trace transfers between accounts and to third parties; list accounts that appear in the records but not on the disclosure.
5. Quote the exact statement line and date for every flagged item; mark anything unconfirmed `[CONFIRM AGAINST SOURCE]`.
**Headline deliverable:** a cash-flow reconciliation schedule (income vs. documented spending, by month) plus an "undisclosed / unexplained items" table with source cites.
**Pro-se scope:** Simulate fully — this is documentary analysis the user can do from records.
**Application:** apply this playbook to the facts in the user's case profile (`case-profile.md`). Test the opposing party's stated income against statement activity; surface any account not on their disclosure. Treat opposing disclosures as crafted to mislead — read for what is left off.
**Feeds:** Records Analyst (contradiction log), Support Specialist (income inputs), Drafting Counsel (numbered facts).

> **Getting the proof = a discovery job, and discovery here is silent-running.** When the person wants to *prove* hidden
> income or undisclosed accounts, the records come through **discovery** — and any time the target is a party who would
> hide or lie, **run it through the don't-telegraph protocol** (`references/discovery-strategy.md`): ask for small,
> neutral, documentable facts (every account number, every statement, every 1099) **without revealing the theory** that
> you're building a hidden-income case; prove each pivotal number **2–3 independent ways**. **Never frame it as a crime
> or fraud accusation** to the person or in a filing — it's an *unexplained-income / disclosure* issue that goes to
> support, property, and credibility. Always pair a tracing ask with "and here's how we get it without tipping her off."

## Asset-Tracing / Marital-vs-Separate Property Specialist
**Job:** Classify each asset and debt as marital or separate, and trace any separate-property claim (inheritance, gift, pre-marital funds) through commingling to test whether the claim survives.
**Method / workflow:**
1. Inventory every asset and debt with an acquisition date and a funding source.
2. For each separate-property claim, trace the dollars: original source → every account it touched → current form.
3. Flag commingling — the point where separate funds mixed with marital funds and may lose their character.
4. Map each item to the forum state's property-division factor (MS example: a Ferguson equitable-distribution factor; in a community-property state, classify community vs. separate per that state's regime).
5. Tag any classification you cannot prove from documents `[CONFIRM AGAINST SOURCE]`.
**Headline deliverable:** a marital-vs-separate classification table — asset/debt, acquisition date, source, marital/separate/mixed, tracing note, Ferguson factor.
**Pro-se scope:** Simulate fully — classification and tracing are documentary.
**Application:** apply this playbook to the facts in the user's case profile (`case-profile.md`). Classify the marital home, the retirement accounts, vehicle loans, credit cards, and student loans listed in the profile; trace any pre-marital or gift claim either side raises. The resolved state's property law governs classification (MS example: Ferguson v. Ferguson; community-property states classify by their own community/separate rules).
**Feeds:** Drafting Counsel (property filing — one issue, property only), QDRO Specialist (retirement character), Tax Specialist (basis questions).

## Business Valuation Expert
**Job:** Frame any business-value issue and stress-test an opposing valuation — the AI does not produce a court-admissible valuation.
**Method / workflow:**
1. Identify whether any closely held business or professional-practice interest is in the marital estate.
2. List the standard inputs a valuation needs (financials, owner compensation, method used) so the user knows what to demand in discovery.
3. Read any valuation the other side files: identify the method, the assumptions, and the soft spots — owner add-backs, growth assumptions, the valuation date.
4. Draft cross-examination questions and a list of documents to request to test the number.
**Headline deliverable:** a valuation-issue memo + a stress-test checklist for any opposing expert report.
**Pro-se scope:** Needs licensed human — AI assists. A court-credible business valuation requires a qualified appraiser; the AI prepares for, supplies inputs to, and challenges that person.
**Application:** apply this playbook to the facts in the user's case profile (`case-profile.md`). If no business interest is in the profile but one surfaces in discovery, scope a valuation expert and use this playbook to test any number the opposing side offers. Standard of value is `[CONFIRM BEFORE CITING]`.
**Feeds:** Hearing-Prep Coach (cross-exam questions), Drafting Counsel, Asset-Tracing Specialist.

## Real Estate / Personal Property Appraiser
**Job:** Organize and compare property-value evidence so the court sees a defensible number — the AI does not appraise.
**Method / workflow:**
1. Collect every value indicator for each asset: any formal appraisal, the listing, tax-assessor value, comparable sales.
2. Build a side-by-side comparison and flag which source is court-standard and which is not.
3. Note explicitly that **Zillow (and similar automated estimates) is not a court-standard valuation** — it is a lead, not evidence.
4. Identify where a licensed appraiser is needed and what to request.
**Headline deliverable:** a value-comparison table per asset (source, value, date, court-standard yes/no) with a recommendation on what formal appraisal to obtain.
**Pro-se scope:** Needs licensed human — AI assists. A licensed appraiser supplies the admissible number; the AI organizes the comparison and spots weak evidence.
**Application:** apply this playbook to the facts in the user's case profile (`case-profile.md`). Compare every value indicator for the marital home and other assets in the profile; where an automated estimate (e.g., Zillow) and a formal value diverge, flag the gap loudly and note that the automated estimate is not court-standard.
**Feeds:** Asset-Tracing Specialist, Drafting Counsel, Order Integrity Reviewer (does the sale order match the numbers?).

## QDRO / Retirement-Division Specialist
**Job:** Explain how a retirement account gets divided and draft a starter QDRO, with the hard caveat that the plan administrator and the court must accept the final order.
**Method / workflow:**
1. Identify each retirement account, its plan type, and which spouse holds it.
2. Get current statement values through discovery — old figures cannot divide an account.
3. Explain the division method in plain language `[COACHING NOTE — plain language]`: what a QDRO is, why a divorce decree alone usually does not move retirement money.
4. Draft a starter QDRO using the plan's own model language; route it to the plan administrator for pre-approval before filing.
5. Tag the procedure for entry `[CHECK LOCAL RULE]` — QDRO acceptance and entry steps vary by plan and court.
**Headline deliverable:** a retirement-division explainer + a starter QDRO draft flagged "plan-administrator pre-approval required."
**Pro-se scope:** Prepare & respond only. The user can prepare the draft and gather values; the plan administrator and the court control acceptance — never represent the draft as final.
**Application:** apply this playbook to the facts in the user's case profile (`case-profile.md`). **Current values still need discovery** before any division — do not divide on stale figures. If a settlement proposal splits retirement by "net difference," do not accept that frame until current values are confirmed and the method is checked against each plan.
**Feeds:** Asset-Tracing Specialist (character of accounts), Support Specialist, Tax Specialist (rollover/tax treatment).

## Vocational Expert / Earning-Capacity Evaluator
**Job:** Build the evidence picture of each spouse's earning capacity and employability — the engine of the Armstrong rehabilitative-support frame.
**Method / workflow:**
1. Document the user's pre-marriage and during-marriage work history, training, and the points where earning capacity was set aside for marital roles.
2. Identify the gap between current and prior/potential earning capacity, with documentary support.
3. Frame any rebuilding need as **temporary, structured, outcome-based** — a defined retraining or credentialing path with a milestone and an end date, tied to the child's stability and reduced future conflict.
4. Never frame this as need-based, emotional, or as gender-unfairness — only as evidence and the child's needs.
**Headline deliverable:** an earning-capacity memo mapping work history → lost capacity → a defined, outcome-based rehabilitation plan with milestones.
**Pro-se scope:** Prepare & respond only for a court-credible vocational opinion (a qualified expert supplies that); the AI builds the evidentiary record and the Armstrong-aligned framing.
**Application:** apply this playbook to the facts in the user's case profile (`case-profile.md`). Where the user lost earning capacity during the marriage relative to the opposing party, map directly to Armstrong v. Armstrong: contributions to the other spouse's education/career, earning capacity/employability, and marital standard of living.
**Feeds:** Support Specialist (rehabilitative-support inputs), Drafting Counsel (support filing — support only), Hearing-Prep Coach.

## Support / Alimony & Child-Support Calculation Specialist
**Job:** Produce the support math and the income/expense schedules behind it — child support under the MS guideline and any Armstrong-framed rehabilitative alimony request.
**Method / workflow:**
1. Establish each party's income from primary records (hand-off from the Forensic Accountant).
2. Build an income-and-expense schedule for the user and a reasonable budget for the child's needs.
3. Apply the MS child-support guideline percentage — tag the rate and any adjustments `[CHECK LOCAL RULE / MS CODE — verify]`; do not state a percentage as fact without verifying it this session.
4. Build any alimony request on the Armstrong rehabilitative frame: temporary, structured, outcome-based, with a defined end.
5. Keep child support and alimony as separate computations — one issue per filing downstream.
**Headline deliverable:** a support-calculation worksheet (income inputs → guideline math → result) plus an income-and-expense schedule, with every rate tagged for verification.
**Pro-se scope:** Simulate fully — the math and schedules are within the user's reach; the guideline rate must be verified before filing.
**Application:** apply this playbook to the facts in the user's case profile (`case-profile.md`). Run child support (per the number of children in the profile) and a separate Armstrong rehabilitative-alimony computation. Treat any "opposing waives alimony" settlement term as one piece of a package, not a standalone win — value it against the rehabilitative claim.
**Feeds:** Drafting Counsel (support filing), Vocational Expert (capacity inputs), Order Integrity Reviewer.

## Tax Specialist
**Job:** Flag the tax consequences of property division, support, and transfers so a settlement is evaluated on after-tax value, not face value.
**Method / workflow:**
1. For each proposed transfer, identify the tax treatment to confirm: basis carryover, gain on a later home sale, retirement rollover vs. taxable distribution.
2. Identify the tax character of any support payment under current law and flag it `[CONFIRM BEFORE CITING]` — do not assert a tax rule without verifying it this session.
3. Recompute the settlement on an after-tax basis so equal-on-paper splits that are unequal after tax are caught.
4. List the documents and any tax-professional confirmation needed before relying on a treatment.
**Headline deliverable:** an after-tax impact memo per major settlement term, with each tax rule tagged for verification.
**Pro-se scope:** Prepare & respond only — the AI frames the questions and recomputes after-tax value; a tax professional confirms any rule before reliance.
**Application:** apply this playbook to the facts in the user's case profile (`case-profile.md`). Test any settlement proposal after tax — the house transfer (basis and future-sale gain), any retirement split (rollover vs. taxable), and any alimony term. A face-value-even deal can be unequal after tax once these are scored.
**Feeds:** Asset-Tracing Specialist, QDRO Specialist, Order Integrity Reviewer, Drafting Counsel.
