# Order Integrity Reviewer — Proposed-Order & Redline Audit

> **Jurisdiction note (Clawyer):** this file was written for Mississippi family court. Outside Mississippi, or outside a family case, treat every rule number, case name, deadline, fee and form in it as an example of what to look up, not as your law. Family-law steps in it do not apply to other kinds of case. Confirm your own state's version first and tag anything unconfirmed `CONFIRM-BEFORE-CITING`.

The specialist that stops the opposing side from putting words in the judge's mouth. In MS chancery, the prevailing
side often **drafts the proposed order** — and a drafted order can quietly say more, less, or other than what the court
actually ruled. Then every redline exchange is another chance to slip something in. This reviewer's job: **the entered
order must match the ruling, word for word in scope, every round.**

**Example:** the bench adopts the GAL recommendation — *joint legal custody, one parent has final decision-making over
**educational and medical** decisions*. Opposing counsel's drafted order says *final decision-making over
**everything**.* That is a scope expansion, not a wording choice. Catch it before it is signed.

## Step 1 — establish the Source of Truth (SoT), and quote it exactly

You cannot audit a draft without the controlling text. The SoT is, in order of authority:
1. The court's **ruling on the record** — the transcript of the bench/oral ruling, or a written opinion/bench ruling.
2. The **GAL recommendation as adopted** — if the judge said "we'll go by the Guardian's recommendation," the SoT is
   that recommendation's exact terms, only to the extent adopted. Pull the recommendation and the part of the record
   adopting it.
3. The **parties' agreement on the record** — for agreed/consent orders, the read-in terms.

Quote the SoT verbatim for every operative term (custody type, decision-making scope, parenting schedule, support
amounts, property/debt allocations, deadlines, conditions), **with an exact locator for each quote** — transcript
page:line (`Tr. __:__`), or written opinion / GAL rec page + paragraph (`p.__ ¶__`). A quote without a locator is not
audit-ready: the locator is what lets the judge verify the slip in ten seconds instead of taking your word for it. If
the SoT isn't available, say so and **stop** — flag
`[NEED THE RULING / TRANSCRIPT / GAL REC BEFORE I CAN AUDIT THIS ORDER]`. Never audit a draft against memory of what
was said.

> **Pre-ruling negotiation (no court ruling yet)?** This reviewer audits a draft against the *court's ruling*. When a
> draft **agreement or temporary order is still being negotiated back and forth** — before any ruling exists — the
> baseline is the **last agreed version + the user's term sheet**, not a ruling. Use **`references/negotiation-counsel.md`**
> (scope-lock + full-document diff + change ledger) for that phase; bring this reviewer in once it becomes a proposed
> order tied to a ruling or a signed agreement.

## Step 2 — clause-by-clause comparison (SoT ↔ their draft)

Walk every operative clause of the draft against the SoT. For each, classify:

- **Expansion** — draft grants more than ruled (e.g., "educational and medical" → "all decisions" / "everything";
  "final decision-making" with no subject → unlimited; a specific schedule → "as the other parent determines").
- **Addition** — a term that was never ruled on at all (new restrictions, new obligations, attorney's fees not awarded,
  a right of first refusal not ordered).
- **Omission** — a term the court ordered that the draft drops (your awarded time, a make-up provision, a review date).
- **Alteration** — a changed number, date, condition, or qualifier (a joint schedule → primary; "shall" → "may"; a defined term
  redefined).
- **Catch-all creep** — open-ended phrases that expand authority by implication: "in their sole discretion," "as they
  deem appropriate," "and any other matters," "including but not limited to," "primary," "sole."

**Watch defined terms and qualifiers hardest** — the sneakiest changes hide in a definition, a "sole/joint," or a
single adjective, not in an obviously new paragraph.

## Step 3 — the discrepancy table (headline deliverable)

| # | Clause | Court ordered / adopted (verbatim quote + pin-cite locator) | Draft says (quote) | Type | Effect on the user | Proposed fix (exact language) |
|---|--------|----------------------------------------------|--------------------|------|-------------------|-------------------------------|

For every discrepancy, give the **exact replacement language** that conforms the clause to the SoT — so the redline you
send back is ready, not just a complaint. Keep proposed language neutral and tracking the ruling; do not over-correct
in your own favor beyond what was ordered (that invites the same objection back).

## Step 4 — redline round discipline (this is where slips reappear)

Every exchange is a fresh audit. Maintain a **change ledger** across rounds so nothing removed comes back and nothing
new sneaks in:

1. **Baseline** = the last version both sides agreed to (or the SoT for round one).
2. On each version received, diff **every clause** against (a) the last agreed version AND (b) the SoT.
3. **Every change must trace to an agreed edit.** Any change that doesn't trace to something both sides agreed to is a
   slip — flag it `[UNAGREED CHANGE]`, even if it looks minor.
4. **Re-check previously fixed clauses each round** — a clause corrected in round 1 can be quietly reverted in round 3.
   The ledger row stays open until the order is signed.
5. Watch for **moving the problem**: when one expansion is removed, scan for the same expansion reappearing in a
   different clause or a definition.

**Change ledger format:**

| Clause | SoT (quote + pin-cite) | R1 their draft | R1 our fix | R2 their draft | Status (conforms / open / re-slipped) |
|--------|-------------|----------------|------------|----------------|----------------------------------------|

## Tools

- Use the workspace **docx / pdf** skills to extract the full text of both the draft order and the SoT (transcript, GAL
  rec) before comparing — compare text, not memory.
- Log each round and each resolved discrepancy to the case record (the user's chronology / matter files) so the history
  of what was proposed, objected to, and agreed is preserved — that record itself is evidence if a signed order is later
  disputed.

## Discipline

- **Quote both sides.** A scope expansion is proven by the two quotes next to each other, not by characterization.
- **Scope, not style.** Distinguish a true substantive change from harmless rewording — but when a wording change
  shifts scope or discretion at all, treat it as substantive and flag it.
- **Never sign-off blind.** If you could not obtain the SoT or could not review every clause, say exactly what you
  reviewed and what you did not. A missed slip is a one-way door once the order is signed.
- End with NEXT ACTION — the redline to send back, or the SoT document still needed.

> **Different job from the Gray-Area Auditor:** this reviewer checks whether the draft **matches the ruling** (scope
> changes/slips). To hunt **vagueness** in an order — undefined terms, "as agreed" with no tie-breaker, missing
> who/when/where — and tighten the ones that don't benefit the user, use `references/ambiguity-auditor.md`. Run both on a
> proposed order: integrity (does it match the ruling?) **and** ambiguity (is any term exploitably vague?).
