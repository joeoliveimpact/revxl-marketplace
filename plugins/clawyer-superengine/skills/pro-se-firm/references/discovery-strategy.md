# Discovery Strategy — extract the truth without telegraphing your case

> **Jurisdiction note (Clawyer):** this file was written for Mississippi family court. Outside Mississippi, or outside a family case, treat every rule number, case name, deadline, fee and form in it as an example of what to look up, not as your law. Family-law steps in it do not apply to other kinds of case. Confirm your own state's version first and tag anything unconfirmed `CONFIRM-BEFORE-CITING`.

> **A party willing to abuse is willing to lie.** If a question reveals what you're trying to prove, a dishonest witness
> dodges, minimizes, or lies — and you've *warned* them and prepared them for trial. So the rule is: **never let the
> question reveal the theory.** Build the proof from small, neutral, documentable facts the witness can't safely deny,
> assemble the picture yourself, and reveal the conclusion only in **argument** — never in the question. This governs how
> Requests for Admission, interrogatories, deposition questions, and even messages are worded and sequenced. Used by the
> Abuse, Discovery, Adversarial Combat, and Hearing-Prep specialists. Research/drafting support — **not legal advice.**

## The eight principles
1. **Don't telegraph the theory.** Ask for **facts, not conclusions.** Never *"Admit you financially abused me."* Ask the
   small components: *"Admit you held the only login to account ending 1234."* The loaded word ("abuse," "control,"
   "hide," "sabotage") never appears — the witness can't see the picture forming.
2. **Box them with the undeniable.** Word each request around a fact your **documents already prove**, so a denial is
   *provably false*. The witness is trapped both ways: **admit** → you win the fact; **deny** → you catch the lie (and a
   false denial becomes impeachment, a credibility hit, and possible cost-shifting). Make lying the worse option.
3. **Granular — one small fact per request.** Tiny facts look harmless alone; **assembled, they are the pattern.** Spread
   them across topics so the through-line isn't obvious from any single question.
4. **Sequence: neutral → specific → locked.** Open broad and innocuous, then narrow to the pinning facts. In a
   **deposition**, get the commitment *before* you show the document — *commit, confront, contrast* (see
   `courtroom-scripts.md`); never hand them the exhibit first.
5. **Prove every key fact 2–3 independent ways (corroboration rule).** Document **+** admission **+** their own prior
   words **+** a witness/third-party record. If one channel fails (they lie, a record's missing), the others hold. **Never
   rest a critical fact on a single source.**
6. **Save the inference for argument.** The conclusion — *"a decade-long pattern of financial control"* — goes in your
   **filing and closing**, after the facts are locked. The assembled, admitted facts *force* the inference; you never
   asked for it.
7. **Anticipate the lie; pre-load the contradiction.** Assume a dishonest answer and have the document that disproves it
   ready. A liar who denies a documented fact hands you their credibility — often worth more than the fact itself.
8. **Mask intent by mixing instruments.** The same fact surfaces, worded differently, in an RFA, an interrogatory, and a
   depo question; **bury the sensitive requests among ordinary financial discovery** so the target can't spot the theme.

## Worked example — financial control, NOT telegraphed
**Bad (warns them, invites the lie):** *"Admit that you financially controlled and abused Plaintiff for ten years."*
**Good (neutral, documentable, boxes them):**
- *"Admit that from [date] to [date], the online login credentials for the account ending 1234 were known only to you."*
- *"Admit that Plaintiff was not issued a debit card for the account ending 1234 during [period]."*
- *"Admit that on [date] Plaintiff sent you the message attached as Exhibit __ requesting funds for [expense]."* (you hold it)
- *"Admit that the [creditor] account ending 5678, opened [date], lists Plaintiff as an account holder."* (you hold the statement)
- *"Admit that Plaintiff's [year] W-2 reflects wages of $___."* / *"Admit Plaintiff did not file a separate tax return in [years]."*
Each is small, factual, and hard to deny — together they establish sole financial control. **The word "abuse" never
appears.** You argue the pattern *after* they're admitted.

## Multiple proof channels — build all of them (don't rely on the deposition alone)
- **Documents** — statements, records, the paper trail (the backbone).
- **Requests for Admission** — deemed admitted in 30 days (MRCP 36). `[VERIFIED]`
- **Their own prior words** — messages, co-parenting-app logs, prior filings/testimony (Records Analyst).
- **Third-party records** — banks, employers, creditors via **subpoena** (they can't coach a bank).
- **Witnesses** — people who saw the control/the at-home years.
- **Forensic analysis** — the Forensic Accountant reconstructs cash flow and finds the hidden/controlled money.
A fact proven by **document + admission + their own message** is effectively unshakeable — that's the target for every
pivotal fact.

## Guardrails
- **Lawful, truthful discovery only.** Strategic ≠ deceptive-to-the-court: no entrapment, no illegal collection, no
  harassing volume, no misrepresenting the record. The goal is to surface the **truth** efficiently and stop a liar from
  hiding it — not to manufacture anything.
- **Protect your own side too** — don't telegraph *your* vulnerabilities; answer their discovery **minimally and
  truthfully** (client-protection floor).
- One issue per filing; citation discipline on any rule/cite.
