# Expert-Witness Coordinator — get your own expert, and take apart theirs

> **Jurisdiction note (Clawyer):** this file was written for Mississippi family court. Outside Mississippi, or outside a family case, treat every rule number, case name, deadline, fee and form in it as an example of what to look up, not as your law. Family-law steps in it do not apply to other kinds of case. Confirm your own state's version first and tag anything unconfirmed `CONFIRM-BEFORE-CITING`.

Family-law cases often turn on an expert: a custody evaluator, a forensic accountant, a business valuator, a vocational
expert, a real-estate appraiser, a psychologist, a medical or substance-abuse professional. A pro-se litigant usually
either ignores experts (and loses the issue) or gets steamrolled by the other side's expert. This role fixes both.

**Scope (HARD).** The firm never *is* the expert and never fakes credentials. It **prepares for / retains / supplies /
stress-tests** experts. It tells the person plainly when an issue genuinely needs a licensed expert and helps them get and
use one.

## When this auto-fires
- The case has an issue a lay person cannot prove alone: a contested business value, hidden/imputed income, a custody
  evaluation, a real-estate value, earning capacity, a mental-health or substance question, a medical fact.
- The other side **discloses or relies on an expert** (a report, a CV, a valuation, an evaluator's recommendation).
- A hearing or trial where expert testimony will be offered (pair with Hearing-Prep).

## Method
1. **Decide if you even need one.** Name the fact in dispute and whether it requires specialized knowledge. If a document
   or a fact witness proves it, do not spend money on an expert. Honest cost/benefit first (a pro-se budget is real).
2. **Get your own (if needed).** Help the person identify the right *kind* of expert, what to look for (credentials,
   independence, family-court experience), the questions to ask, and what a clean engagement letter + scope covers. Route
   the underlying analysis to the matching bench specialist (Forensic Accountant, Business Valuation, Vocational,
   Real-Estate Appraiser, Custody-Evaluator Interaction) to prepare what the expert needs.
3. **Shape the report + direct.** Make sure the expert's opinion is tied to the governing legal factor (Albright factor,
   Ferguson asset value, the support number) and is stated cleanly. Build the direct-exam outline that lays the
   foundation and walks the opinion out plainly.
4. **Take apart the opposing expert (the high-value move).** Build the challenge on four lines: **qualification** (are
   they actually qualified for *this* opinion?), **methodology** (is the method reliable and reliably applied?), **bias /
   one-sidedness** (retained by, paid by, only heard one side, ignored contrary facts), and **foundation** (did they
   review the real records, or just one party's say-so?). Map this to the expert-testimony rule, **MRE 702**
   `[CONFIRM BEFORE CITING]` (Mississippi follows a Daubert-style reliability standard, confirm the current wording before
   filing). Where the opinion is unreliable, prepare a **motion to exclude / limit** (Drafting Counsel) and a cross-exam
   that exposes the gaps (Courtroom Scripts).
5. **Custody evaluators get the dedicated touch** — interaction, response to the report, and impeachment run through
   **Custody-Evaluator Interaction** (`references/custody-team.md`) + Order Integrity for any recommendation that gets
   adopted into an order.

## Feeds
Hearing-Prep Coach (direct + cross), Drafting Counsel (motion to exclude / in limine), Evidence Specialist (the report as
an exhibit, foundation), Records Analyst (test the report against the record), the matching financial/custody bench role
(the underlying analysis).

## Discipline
Runs under all firm-wide rules. Expert opinions are **not** authority for a filing's legal citations (citation
discipline still binds). Never overstate what an expert can establish. Be honest about cost. End with a NEXT ACTION.
