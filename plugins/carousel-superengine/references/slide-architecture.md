# Slide Architecture — structure frameworks every build follows

Carousels win on narrative architecture, not aesthetics. They are the deep-engagement format:
multi-slide dwell time and reference-worthy value drive the signals that matter. As of 2026, DM
shares (sends) are the strongest distribution signal; saves still mark reference-worthy value, but
their ranking weight is contested in 2026 (some Instagram-sourced reporting says downweighted)...
treat shares as the reach lever, saves as the audience-value metric, and expect this weighting to
keep moving. Reels discover; carousels convert attention into authority and leads.

## Slide counts (platform benchmarks)

- **Instagram: 8-12 slides** optimal for educational content (best completion rates); up to 20 for
  ultra-deep guides. Design 1080×1350 (4:5 portrait).
- **LinkedIn: 5-10 pages** (PDF document post). Design 1080×1080 (1:1 square).
- Short punch formats (myth-bust) can run 5-6 slides; never pad to hit a count.

## The universal 5-pillar arc

    [1: Hook] → [2-3: Context bridge] → [4-8: Value meat] → [7-9: Proof] → [final: CTA]

1. **Scroll-stopper (slide 1)** — specific promise, 5-8 words, curiosity gap (see @hook-patterns.md).
2. **Empathy/context bridge (2-3)** — agitate the pain so precisely the avatar thinks "this coach is
   in my head." Doubles as standalone hooks 2 + 3 (Triple Hook rule).
3. **Value meat (middle)** — deliver the cover's promise. ONE idea per slide (flashcard rule);
   every slide earns the next swipe (see @swipe-retention.md).
4. **Proof anchor (before the CTA)** — ground the advice in something real: client result with
   numbers, testimonial screenshot, data chart. From `{{PROOF_ASSETS}}` only; never invented.
5. **CTA (final slide)** — ONE action (see @cta-slide-patterns.md). No mid-deck soft-save or second
   ask: one carousel, one ask, on the last slide.

## The 3 build blueprints (pick by goal)

**A. Educational / Listicle — optimized for SAVES.** Problem-Agitation-Solution over a numbered
list. Hook → credibility hook → roadmap hook → N steps/mistakes (one per slide, actionable, each
with the WHY in one line) → implementation slide ("do this instead") → proof → cheat-sheet summary
(save trigger) → CTA. The cheat-sheet slide is the save magnet: a complete, scannable recap table.

**B. Story-led "How I" — optimized for CONNECTION + dwell.** Hero's journey, diary pacing. Hook
(candid photo + how-I claim) → opinion hook (the industry take) → the low moment (specific, visceral,
timestamped) → the catalyst/change → the routine (real moments, real details) → the hidden win
(non-obvious benefit) → confrontation slide (common advice vs what actually worked) → results with
numbers → CTA (soft: newsletter/follow/DM). Voice edge matters most here — this is the coach at
their most personal.

**C. Case-study "Problem-Solution-Result" — optimized for LEADS.** A mini sales letter in the feed.
Hook (client + striking metric) → the pain state (what they were doing that wasn't working) → the
shift (why the old approach stalled) → intervention steps (2-3 slides, specific) → the breakthrough
(numbers over time) → client voice (real testimonial screenshot) → synthesis cheat sheet (save
trigger) → hard CTA (DM keyword). Requires a REAL client story from config/VoC... elicit, never
invent (offer to capture one if the bank is thin).

## 6 topic frameworks that consistently perform (idea engine)

When the coach has no topic, generate from these categories, filled with THEIR pillars + avatar
pains: (1) data/receipt teardown of a real result; (2) specific transformation case study;
(3) nutrition/method deep-dive on one narrow mechanism; (4) habit-stacking for busy professionals
(the after-[existing habit], I-[new habit] formula); (5) anti-guru myth-vs-fact (highest comment
volume); (6) "yes, but..." honesty post (curated win vs behind-the-scenes struggle... humanizes,
earns shares). Rotate categories; never let a feed become one note.

## Sequencing rules

- One idea per slide. If a slide needs two sentences of body copy to land, it's two slides.
- The promise made on slide 1 must be FULLY paid by the second-to-last slide. CTA never interrupts
  unpaid value.
- Slide order within value meat — **the claim/payoff split (resolves the old "strongest first vs
  last" conflict):** the strongest CLAIM is teased on the cover ("#4 is the one nobody does"), the
  strongest PAYOFF is held to its position near the end — they are different beats. Early value
  slides open strong (post-hook attention is high), but the item the cover teased NEVER pays out
  early; revealing the primary payoff mid-deck is the #1 retention killer (see retention-loops.md).
- Every carousel maps slide-by-slide before any copy is written: n, role, job-in-≤12-words. Show the
  map, then write.
