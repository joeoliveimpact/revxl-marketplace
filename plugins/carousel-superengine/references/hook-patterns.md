# Hook Patterns — the slide-1 (and 2, and 3) decision

The hook slide is a mini landing page. In 0.5 seconds it must answer: *Is this for me?* and *What do
I get if I swipe?* The 28-45 B2C wellness consumer carries a hook shield against loud, preachy,
templated marketing... win with specificity, story, and pattern interrupts, never volume.

**Every example below is illustrative craft, not content.** Generation pulls topics, pains, numbers,
and proof from the coach's config + VoC. Never ship an example's facts.

## The Triple Hook rule (algorithm mechanic — non-negotiable)

Instagram re-serves a scrolled-past carousel later the same day, opening on **slide 2 or 3** instead
of slide 1 (the "second chance" / double-exposure mechanic). Therefore:
- **Slides 1, 2, AND 3 must each work as standalone hooks** — complete sense to a cold viewer with
  zero context, own scroll-stopping headline, high-contrast visual.
- Slide 2 is not a continuation; it's an independent entry point (credibility or contrarian angle).
- Slide 3 doubles as the context bridge but still stands alone (pain empathy or roadmap).

**Why it works (2026 mechanics, evolving):** dwell time, swipe-through rate, and completion rate
are the primary carousel ranking signals; strong early signals trigger the 24-48hr re-serve pass.
Two plays this unlocks:
- **Hook-swap:** per-slide like insights (in IG Insights) show which slide actually stops people...
  if a later slide outperforms the cover, reorder to frontload the proven slide.
- **Upcycle cadence:** re-uploading evergreen posts is explicitly allowed (Mosseri-confirmed, no
  penalty; trial reels are the one exception). Re-up proven evergreen carousels every 60-90 days,
  ideally with a fresh or swapped hook slide.

## The 7 hook archetypes

**1. The "How I" relational hook (story arc).** How-to lectures are dead for this demographic;
how-I journeys convert. Formula: `How I [specific result] in [timeframe] + [unique angle/constraint]`.
Example shape: "How I fixed my afternoon energy crashes in 7 days (without quitting my morning
espresso)." Visual: warm, real, candid photo of the coach in a real setting... not a posed cutout.
Raw-over-polished is a named 2026 shift, not just taste: polished, scripted-looking openers
underperform candid ones.

**2. The specific transformation split-screen (pattern interrupt).** Before/after with a
metric-driven outcome creates a curiosity gap the brain wants closed... the cover feels incomplete
without the swipe. Formula: `visual split (before left, after right) + specific metric + gated
secret`. Works with progress data, app screenshots, real numbers the coach actually has.

**3. The anti-guru counterintuitive claim.** Challenge a practice the avatar currently follows.
Formula: `Stop [accepted practice]. Do [specific alternative] instead.` Creates cognitive dissonance
that breaks scroll autopilot. Pair with a bold, high-contrast text-only slide. This is also the
highest-comment-volume archetype (people argue).

**4. The data/receipt teardown.** Real tracker screenshots, client charts, raw numbers... instant
authority for the precision-wellness consumer. Formula: `raw data visual + high-stakes diagnostic
line`. Only with REAL data from the coach's proof assets; never fabricate a screenshot.

**5. The expectation-subversion couplet.** <!-- T1 shape; surfaced as the #1 hook by ~10x in a
430-beat niche study (07.17.26) — the evidence stays in the coach's own inspire data (T3). -->
Two short declaratives: state the assumed number/fact, then snap it. Formula:
`The average [status/outcome] is [expected value]. Not [assumed value].` The gap between what the
reader assumed and what's true IS the hook — no question, no preamble, just the correction.
Strongest as huge type on a bare ground. Works because it makes the reader instantly wrong about
something they care about — the swipe is them fixing it.

**6. The colon list-promise.** Formula: `[N] [specific things] that [outcome]:` — the colon does
the work; it points off the edge of the slide. Tease the best-numbered item on the cover ("#4 is
the one nobody does") and HOLD it to its position — the tease is the claim, the payoff stays
inside the deck.

**7. The objection-killed how-to.** A how-to promise with the avatar's #1 objection pre-deleted in
the same breath. Formula: `How to [result]... [objection killed]` ("...with zero tech skills",
"...without posting daily"). Picks up the reader who already talked themselves out of the plain
version of the promise.

Pick the archetype by the avatar's `{{AWARENESS_LEVEL}}`: problem-aware → archetype 2, 3, or 5
(name the pain / break the myth / break the assumption); solution-aware → 1, 4, 6, or 7 (show the
path / the proof / the list / the objection-free path); product-aware → 4 (receipts) with a
heavier CTA arc.

## Hook copy rules

- **5-8 word core promise**, specific over clever: numbers, timeframes, named constraints.
- State the claim IMMEDIATELY. No preamble ("Have you ever wondered...", "As a coach, I see...")
  — the millennial-pause opener kills pacing and gets scrolled.
- Specificity is the 2026 standard: "How to eat better" is dead; a concrete protocol + number lives.
- Write 3 hook options per carousel (different archetypes), recommend one, let the coach pick
  (suggest-then-approve).

## What kills slide 1

- ❌ Solid background + pointing-selfie cutout + generic headline ("3 Tips to Lose Weight") —
  reads templated-coach, instant scroll.
- ❌ 2021 Canva-infographic aesthetic: clip art, sterile minimalism, stock photos. Trust killer.
- ❌ Text over the deck's format budget (default ~20% of canvas; see design-rules.md). It's a
  flashcard, not a blog post.
- ❌ Vague promises with no number, timeframe, or constraint.
- ❌ A hook the coach can't cash — slide 2+ must deliver exactly what slide 1 promised; a bait gap
  destroys saves and follows.
