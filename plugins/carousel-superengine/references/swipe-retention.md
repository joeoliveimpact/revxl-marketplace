# Swipe Retention — mechanics that earn every next swipe

Completion rate is the carousel's private conversion funnel: early exits tell the algorithm the
cover over-promised. Every slide has one retention job: make NOT swiping feel like leaving a
question unanswered.

The stakes, anchored to Q1 2026 data: 5-7 slide carousels pull ~3.4x more saves and ~2.1x more
shares than static images, and high-dwell posts earn 40-60% more distribution than low-dwell ones.

## 1. Open loops + cliffhangers (copy devices)

- **The "but here's the catch" transition** — micro-teaser anchored bottom-right of a value slide:
  "But if you do this at 5pm, it backfires. Next slide explains why →". Use on 1-2 slides max;
  every-slide teasers read as manipulation.
- **The reverse-expectation tease** — number the list and flag one item on the cover: "#4 is the one
  most people get wrong." The brain swipes to close that specific loop.
- **The mid-carousel pattern break** — one slide late in the value meat that flips tone ("Wait...
  here's the mistake that ruins all of this") re-spikes attention right before proof + CTA.
- **The loop-back close (2026 dwell play)** — the final slide contextually answers or echoes
  slide 1, prompting a second swipe-through pass. Distinct from the CTA: the echo is in the payoff
  framing, not an extra ask.

## 2. The seamless panorama (visual device)

One continuous background/graphic spread across 2+ slides, sliced at the boundary. A visual element
cut mid-slide creates a need for closure... swipe-through boosts up to 2x. Execution: design one wide
canvas (e.g. 4320×1350 for a 4-slide IG run), place a cutout/chart directly over a slide boundary,
slice into 1080×1350 frames. Direction note for the coach: "slides 2-3 share one continuous
background; the [element] sits on the seam."

## 3. Text density (the flashcard principle)

Slides are flashcards, not blog posts. Walls of text are the #1 swipe-exit trigger.
- **20% rule:** text never covers more than ~20% of the canvas.
- **25-50 words max** body copy per slide; headline carries the idea.
- **Bold the skim path:** bold the exact phrase that carries the value... a skimmer reading only
  bold words gets 100% of the point.
- Line height 1.4-1.6×, 35-45 characters per line, thumb-size readable.
- Overflow goes to the CAPTION (see @caption-strategy.md), never onto the slide.

## 4. Visual continuity (rhythm without monotony)

- Consistent background system + margins (~60px) + watermark position on every slide; change only
  the focal element per slide. Rhythm = recognition; chaos = authority leak.
- Micro-cue arrows (small, bottom-right) on early slides pace the swipe.
- A small progress indicator ("3/8", same top corner every slide) reduces mid-carousel abandonment
  by telling the reader the investment is finite (2026 tactic, sits alongside the micro-arrows).
- On IG, one 5-second silent looping video mid-carousel (form demo, b-roll moment) measurably lifts
  dwell time + engagement (~29% over static-only). One, not three.

## 5. Weighted CTA sequencing (retention's endgame)

Drop-off accumulates every swipe... a single slide-10 CTA catches only survivors.
- **Soft CTA on the summary slide (8-9):** the save trigger, placed at the dopamine peak right after
  value delivery: "Save this checklist for [specific reuse moment]." Drives ~31% more saves than a
  final-slide-only ask.
- **Hard CTA on the final slide:** the one conversion action (see @cta-slide-patterns.md).
- Never more than these two asks per carousel.
