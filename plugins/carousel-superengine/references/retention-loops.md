# Retention Loops — the organizing system above the devices

`swipe-retention.md` holds the DEVICES (teasers, panorama, pattern break, weighted CTAs).
This file names the SYSTEM those devices serve: three nested loop tiers + a per-swipe seam test.
Every carousel build tags its slide map against these tiers; the quality gate audits them.

Sources: shortform-superengine's loop system (the sustain/close framework), re-anchored to IG's
re-serve mechanism, gated against a 430-beat niche study (07.17.26). Three independent methods
converged on the same first rule — that convergence is the confidence.

## The three loop tiers <!-- T1: timeless craft -->

- **PRIMARY loop** — opens on slide 1 (the cover's claim/tease), resolves ONLY on the payoff
  slide. Revealing the primary payoff mid-deck is the #1 retention killer: strongest item LAST,
  the hook teases THAT one. The close recontextualizes slide 1 — end where you began, now meaning
  more (prompts the second read-through pass).
- **SECONDARY loops** — one per numbered item / value beat: each slide answers the question the
  last slide planted AND plants the next. Devices that serve this tier: "here's the catch"
  teaser, reverse-expectation tease, mid-carousel pattern break (all in @swipe-retention.md).
- **MICRO loops** — inside a single slide: the bold skim path. A skimmer reading only the bold
  words gets 100% of the point and a reason to keep swiping.

## The seam test <!-- T1: run per swipe, enforced in @carousel-quality.md -->

Walk every slide boundary and ask: *what open question carries the reader across this seam?*
A seam with no open question is a scroll point — the reader's exit is built into the deck.
Dead seam found → add a secondary-loop device at that boundary or restructure the slide order.

## Triple Hook × loop system <!-- T2: platform mechanic — current as of 07.2026; re-verify if IG re-serve behavior changes -->

IG re-serves scrolled-past carousels opening on slide 2 or 3 (the "second chance" mechanic —
full craft rules in @hook-patterns.md §Triple Hook). Consequence for loops: **slides 1-3 each
open the primary loop INDEPENDENTLY** — a cold viewer entering on slide 2 or 3 must still catch
a hook. Slides 4+ run as connected story/steps: the reader who swiped past 3 is committed, so
the body flows as narrative — standalone-vs-connected is position-dependent, not either/or.

## Partial-payoff honesty <!-- T1 -->

Never stack teases: between any two teases there must be delivered value. Tease-on-tease reads
as manipulation and kills saves. Partial payoffs are fine — pay something real, then open the
next question.

## Repeaters <!-- T1: teach-mode native -->

Land the key point twice, in two registers: the tight term first, then the stupid-simple analogy
("effort is the dial... you don't send a missile after an ant"). One repeater per deck, on the
beat the coach most needs remembered. Doubles comprehension without adding a slide.

## Benchmarks to beat <!-- T2: creator-reported, NOT Meta-sourced — design priors, never promises -->

- Drop-off shape: ~24% exit on slide 1, stabilizing ~15% by slide 4 — the Triple Hook + early
  seams are where retention is won or lost.
- Optimal length 6-10 slides (cap 20); peak reach lands deep in longer decks only when every
  seam holds.
- Close on **saves + DM-shares** — the top-2 signals after dwell; DM-shares carry the most
  non-follower reach weight (this is why keyword→DM gating is a REACH play, not only leads).

## Evidence guardrails <!-- T3-shaped: exact numbers come from the coach's own inspire/beat data when present -->

When persisted beat data exists, grade the build against it (see @carousel-quality.md §Copy vs
beat data). Typical winner shape from niche studies: ≥2 real proof beats per deck, ≤1 context
beat, ~20-25 words/slide, numbered tease held to its position. The coach's own data overrides
these defaults — that's the point of pulling it.
