# Value Equation — Agent Execution Reference

> Source: $100M Offers, Ch. 6 — "The Value Equation"

## Core Formula

```
VALUE = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice)
```

### Variable Definitions

| Variable | Position | Direction to Increase Value |
|---|---|---|
| **Dream Outcome** | Numerator | ↑ Increase — make the outcome bigger, more vivid, more desirable |
| **Perceived Likelihood** | Numerator | ↑ Increase — social proof, guarantees, credentials, case studies |
| **Time Delay** | Denominator | ↓ Decrease — faster results = more valuable |
| **Effort & Sacrifice** | Denominator | ↓ Decrease — less work required from client = more valuable |

### Key Insight
> "The gap between what people currently have and what they want is VALUE. The larger the gap, the more value we can provide."

---

## Agent Execution Protocol

When analyzing or constructing a client offer, score each variable on a 1–10 scale:

### Step 1: Dream Outcome Audit
- What is the client's #1 desired end-state?
- Is it expressed in **tangible, measurable** terms? (e.g., "lose 20 lbs" not "get healthier")
- Can you make the outcome **bigger** without changing the service? (reframe)
- Score: How desirable is the stated outcome to the target avatar? (1-10)

### Step 2: Perceived Likelihood Audit
- What proof exists that this outcome is achievable? (testimonials, case studies, credentials)
- Does the prospect believe **they specifically** can achieve it? (not just that it's possible)
- Are there guarantees in place? (see `05-guarantees.md`)
- Score: How confident is the average prospect that they'll get the result? (1-10)

### Step 3: Time Delay Audit
- How long until the client sees their **first result**? (not final result)
- Can any part of the process be front-loaded to create an early win?
- Is the timeline communicated clearly?
- Score: How fast does the client perceive they'll get the result? (1-10, where 10 = instant)

### Step 4: Effort & Sacrifice Audit
- What does the client have to **do** to get the result?
- What do they have to **give up**?
- Can you do more of the work for them? (done-for-you vs. done-with-you vs. do-it-yourself)
- Tools, checklists, templates that reduce their cognitive load?
- Score: How little effort does the client perceive is required? (1-10, where 10 = zero effort)

### Step 5: Calculate & Diagnose
```
Composite = (Dream Outcome + Perceived Likelihood) - (Time Delay_inverse + Effort_inverse)
```
- If numerator variables are weak → the offer doesn't excite
- If denominator variables are high → the offer feels too hard/slow
- **Target**: Both numerator variables ≥ 8, both denominator variables ≤ 3

## Pricing Implications

> "People pay for certainty."

The Value Equation explains why:
- A **surgeon** charges $50K for a 2-hour operation (Dream Outcome: high, Likelihood: high, Time: minutes, Effort: zero for patient)
- A **YouTube video** about the same surgery is free (Likelihood: unknown, Effort: you do it yourself)

### Price-to-Value Discrepancy Rule
> The offer should be **so valuable** that the prospect feels stupid saying no. The price should represent a 10:1 or 100:1 value-to-cost ratio in the prospect's mind.

## Hormozi Laws (from this chapter)

1. **"The pain is the pitch."** — Start with their pain, not your solution.
2. **"Perception is reality."** — It doesn't matter what the value IS; it matters what the prospect BELIEVES the value is.
3. **"Increase value, don't decrease price."** — When struggling to close, never drop price. Instead, increase perceived value via any of the four variables.

## Common Failure Modes

| Failure | Which Variable | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| "Sounds cool but I'll think about it" | Low Perceived Likelihood | Add proof, guarantees, risk reversal |
| "I don't have time for that" | High Effort & Sacrifice | Do more for them, simplify, add tools |
| "How long does this take?" | High Time Delay | Create fast wins, front-load results |
| "I can get that cheaper elsewhere" | Low Dream Outcome | Reframe the outcome bigger, add exclusivity |
