# Market Selection — Agent Execution Reference

> Source: $100M Offers, Ch. 4 — "Finding the Right Market"

## The Three Market Levers

A Grand Slam Offer in the wrong market still fails. Market selection is **prerequisite** to offer design.

### Starving Crowd Hierarchy
> "A starving crowd is the single greatest advantage a business can have."

You don't need a better offer — you need a market that is **already desperate** for a solution.

---

## The Four Market Indicators

Score each 1–5. Total ≥ 16 = green light. Total ≤ 12 = pivot.

### 1. Massive Pain (Weight: Critical)
- Is the problem **urgent**? (not "nice to have")
- Does the pain keep them up at night?
- Are they actively searching for solutions right now?
- Will it get worse if they don't act?

**Test**: Would they describe this problem to a stranger unprompted?

### 2. Purchasing Power (Weight: Critical)
- Can they afford to pay for a solution?
- Do they have discretionary income or budget authority?
- Are they already spending money on inferior solutions?

**Test**: Are they spending $500+/yr on adjacent products? If yes = purchasing power confirmed.

### 3. Easy to Target (Weight: Important)
- Can you find concentrations of these people? (FB groups, associations, conferences, directories)
- Do they self-identify? (job titles, certifications, memberships)
- Can you reach 10,000 of them with a single campaign?

**Test**: Can you name 3 places where 1,000+ of them gather?

### 4. Growing Market (Weight: Important)
- Is the market expanding or contracting?
- Are new people entering this market regularly?
- Is the total addressable market (TAM) increasing?

**Test**: Google Trends — is search volume for their core problem flat, rising, or falling?

## Niche Selection Formula

### Three Dimensions of Niching Down

```
NICHE = [Target Market] × [Problem] × [Delivery Vehicle]
```

Example progression:
1. **Broad**: "I help businesses grow" ❌
2. **Better**: "I help restaurants get more customers" ⚠️
3. **Niche**: "I help Mexican restaurants in Austin get 50+ Google reviews in 30 days" ✅

### Hormozi Law: Niche Until It Hurts
> "Don't pick a niche, solve a problem for a specific type of person."

The riches are in the niches because:
- You understand the prospect's language, fears, and objections intimately
- You can create hyper-specific proof and case studies
- Competition drops to near-zero
- Price sensitivity disappears (you're the ONLY option)

## Agent Decision Tree: Market Qualification

```
START
├── Does the market have massive pain?
│   ├── NO → STOP. Find a painful problem.
│   └── YES ↓
├── Can they pay for a solution?
│   ├── NO → STOP. Serve a wealthier segment of this market.
│   └── YES ↓
├── Can you find and reach them?
│   ├── NO → STOP. Pick a more accessible sub-market.
│   └── YES ↓
├── Is the market growing?
│   ├── NO → PROCEED WITH CAUTION (can still work but harder)
│   └── YES → GREEN LIGHT ✅
```

## Commodity Trap Diagnostics

> Source: Ch. 3 — "The Commodity Problem"

### Symptoms of Commoditization
- Prospects ask "how much?" before understanding value
- You compete primarily on price
- Your service feels interchangeable with competitors
- Leads say "I found something cheaper"
- Profit margins are shrinking

### Escape Routes (in order of leverage)
1. **Differentiate the OFFER** (not the product) — bundle differently, deliver differently
2. **Differentiate the MARKET** — go narrower, more specific
3. **Differentiate the VEHICLE** — change how it's delivered (1-on-1, cohort, digital, hybrid)
4. **Change the CATEGORY** — create a new category where you're the only player

### The Pricing Continuum

```
Commoditized ←————————————————————————→ Grand Slam Offer

Low Price                                    Premium Price
Many Competitors                             No Comparison
Price-Driven Decision                        Value-Driven Decision
Race to Bottom                               Race to Top
```

> **Hormozi Law**: "If you're competing on price, you don't have an offer — you have a commodity."
