## Golden Rules: The Grand Slam Offer Framework  ### Critical Rules  These MUST be followed. Violations cause significant problems.  #### 1. Pay Commissions Before Receiving Funds  **Rule**: Pay your team their commissions even when the money hasn't arrived yet.  **Why**: Paying early builds trust, loyalty, and momentum. It signals confidence in your offer and prevents talent from leaving during lean periods.  **Do**: ``` Salesman earns $22,000 commission on $120,000 in sales. You wire the $22,000 even though the $120,000 hasn't arrived. Balance drops to $1,036. ```  **Don't**: ``` Wait for the $120,000 to clear before paying the $22,000 commission. Salesman leaves for a competitor. ```  **Test**: Verify commission payments are made within 48 hours of sale, regardless of cash flow.  ---  #### 2. Go All-In on Your Offer  **Rule**: When you believe in your offer, commit fullyΓÇöfinancially and emotionally.  **Why**: Half-measures signal doubt. Full commitment creates the momentum needed to overcome early resistance and build proof.  **Do**: ``` Launch 6 gyms simultaneously. Spend $3,300/day on a credit card with no cash in the bank. Go into debt at $412/hour. ```  **Don't**: ``` Launch one gym at a time to "test the waters." Wait until you have cash reserves before spending. ```  **Test**: Track the ratio of committed resources to available resources during launch.  ---  #### 3. Collect Cash Upfront  **Rule**: Structure offers so customers pay before receiving the full value.  **Why**: Upfront cash solves the working capital problem. It funds growth without external financing and creates a cash flow advantage over competitors.  **Do**: ``` Sell a "challenge offer" at a gym. Collect all cash upfront as your fee. Use that cash to fund operations. ```  **Don't**: ``` Bill after delivery. Wait 60-90 days for payment. ```  **Test**: Calculate the cash conversion cycle for each offer.  ---  #### 4. Build Offers, Not Just Products  **Rule**: The offer is the primary driver of business success, not the product itself.  **Why**: A great product with a weak offer fails. A mediocre product with a Grand Slam Offer succeeds. The offer determines whether customers buy.  **Do**: ``` Craft an offer so compelling customers feel stupid saying no. Include guarantee, urgency, scarcity, and value stacking. ```  **Don't**: ``` Focus on product features before crafting the offer. Compete on price alone. ```  **Test**: Measure offer conversion rate independently from product quality.  ---  ### Important Rules  Should be followed. Violations cause friction or technical debt.  #### 1. Launch Multiple Things Simultaneously  **Rule**: When launching, do multiple launches at once rather than sequentially.  **Why**: Simultaneous launches create compounding effectsΓÇömore data, more proof, more momentum. Sequential launches waste time and delay learning.  **Do**: ``` Launch 6 gyms on the same day. Run all ads simultaneously. Capture all revenue in one period. ```  **Don't**: ``` Launch one gym, wait for results, then launch the next. ```  **Test**: Count the number of concurrent launches per quarter.  ---  #### 2. Use Debt Strategically, Not Fearfully  **Rule**: Use credit and debt as tools for growth, not as threats to avoid.  **Why**: Fear of debt causes missed opportunities. Strategic debt accelerates growth when the underlying offer is sound.  **Do**: ``` Use a $100,000 credit card to fund a $3,300/day burn rate. Go from $1,036 to $100,000+ in one month. ```  **Don't**: ``` Avoid debt at all costs. Wait until you have cash reserves. ```  **Test**: Track the return on invested debt (revenue generated per dollar of debt).  ---  #### 3. Document Everything  **Rule**: Record your numbers, decisions, and stories as they happen.  **Why**: Documentation creates proof, enables storytelling, and provides data for future decisions. It also builds credibility with customers and partners.  **Do**: ``` Screenshot your bank account when it hits $1,036. Keep processing records for every month. Document the journey from rags to riches. ```  **Don't**: ``` Forget to record key moments. Rely on memory for important data. ```  **Test**: Verify that key milestones have documented evidence.  ---  #### 4. Build Trust Through Transparency  **Rule**: Be honest about your struggles and share your journey openly.  **Why**: Vulnerability builds connection. People buy from those they trust, and trust comes from authenticity.  **Do**: ``` Tell the story of going from $1,036 to $100,000. Admit mistakes and shortcomings. Share the real numbers, not just the highlights. ```  **Don't**: ``` Pretend everything is perfect. Hide failures and struggles. ```  **Test**: Measure audience engagement with behind-the-scenes content.  ---  ### Guidelines  Best practices. Violations are acceptable with justification.  #### 1. Focus on One Thing Until It Works  **Rule**: Master one skill or offer before expanding to others.  **Why**: Dilution of focus leads to mediocre results across many areas. Mastery in one area creates a foundation for expansion.  **Do**: ``` Perfect the Grand Slam Offer before scaling. Build one successful gym before launching six. ```  **Don't**: ``` Expand into new industries before mastering the current one. ```  **Test**: Evaluate whether new ventures leverage existing mastery.  ---  #### 2. Give Back Early  **Rule**: Donate and help others even when you're still building.  **Why**: Generosity builds reputation, creates goodwill, and attracts opportunities. It also reinforces the belief that success is not zero-sum.  **Do**: ``` Donate $2,000,000 to fund equal opportunity. Help fund gyms in low-income areas. ```  **Don't**: ``` Wait until you're "rich enough" to give back. ```  **Test**: Track the percentage of revenue given back.  ---  #### 3. Build a Portfolio, Not a Single Business  **Rule**: Diversify across industries and business types over time.  **Why**: A single business is vulnerable to market changes. A portfolio creates stability and compound growth.  **Do**: ``` Build companies in photography, publishing, fitness, consulting, beauty. Mix brick & mortar, software, service, e-commerce, and education. ```  **Don't**: ``` Put all eggs in one basket. ```  **Test**: Count the number of distinct businesses and industries in the portfolio.  ---  ### Quick Reference  | Category | Rule | Priority | |----------|------|----------| | Cash Flow | Pay commissions before receiving funds | Critical | | Strategy | Go all-in on your offer | Critical | | Cash Flow | Collect cash upfront | Critical | | Strategy | Build offers, not just products | Critical | | Launch | Launch multiple things simultaneously | Important | | Finance | Use debt strategically, not fearfully | Important | | Documentation | Document everything as it happens | Important | | Trust | Be transparent about struggles | Important | | Focus | Master one thing before expanding | Guideline | | Generosity | Give back early | Guideline | | Growth | Build a portfolio, not a single business | Guideline |  ---  ### Checklist  Pre-launch/deploy checklist derived from rules:  - [ ] Commissions are paid within 48 hours of sale, regardless of cash flow - [ ] The offer is compelling enough that customers feel stupid saying no - [ ] Cash is collected upfront, not billed after delivery - [ ] Multiple launches are running simultaneously, not sequentially - [ ] Debt is being used strategically with a clear return on invested capital - [ ] Key milestones and numbers are documented with evidence - [ ] The story of the journey is being told openly and honestly - [ ] One core skill or offer has been mastered before expansion - [ ] A portion of revenue is being given back to the community - [ ] The business model is diversified across industries and types  ---  *These rules emerged from the pattern of Alex Hormozi's journey: from $1,036 in the bank to $120M in sales, built on the foundation of a Grand Slam Offer, a credit card, and a girl who believed in him.*
