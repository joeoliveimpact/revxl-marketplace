# Hormozi $100M Offers — Plugin Reference Cheat Sheet

> One-page framework summary for offer-architect skills. Source: Alex Hormozi, *$100M Offers*. Workspace book reference: `Hormozi - 100M - Offers - Book.md`.

---

## The Grand Slam Offer construction (4 layers)

1. **Maximize the Value Equation** (4 drivers)
2. **Trim & Stack** (inventory all problems → solutions, anchor at retail)
3. **Enhance** (Scarcity, Urgency, Bonuses, Guarantees, Naming)
4. **Communicate** (price-to-value gap so wide that not buying feels stupid)

---

## The Value Equation

```
            Dream Outcome   ×   Perceived Likelihood of Achievement
Value =   ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
            Time Delay      ×   Effort & Sacrifice
```

- **Dream Outcome (↑):** Frame benefits in terms of status gained, viewed from others
- **Perceived Likelihood of Achievement (↑):** Proof, social proof, expert positioning, guarantees
- **Time Delay (↓):** Engineer short-term wins — emotional buy-in in first 7-14 days is critical
- **Effort & Sacrifice (↓):** Done-for-you > do-it-yourself; decisions-made-for-you > options menus

If you can drive the denominator toward zero, value approaches infinity. Most marketers focus on the top; the discipline is the bottom.

**Pro tip:** Logical solutions have usually been tried. Use psychological solutions. (Dotted maps at train stations vs. faster trains.)

## Trim & Stack

For each significant problem on the client's path to the dream outcome:
- The problem (in the client's words)
- Your matched solution
- Perceived retail value (anchor to comparable retail or retainer)
- Source of the anchor

Target: 10-15 line items, total perceived value 4-7× actual price.

---

## Bonuses (demand multipliers)

Each bonus:
- Solves a specific objection or fear
- Has anchored perceived value
- Costs you little to deliver
- Has a punchy name

Common high-impact patterns:
- Welcome / starter kit (logistical momentum)
- Spouse / partner brief (kills partner-veto risk)
- Annual re-test voucher (retention hook for Year 2)
- Resource library (asynchronous self-serve)
- Retreat / community priority access (scarcity + status)

---

## Guarantees — stack 3 narrow, never 1 unconditional

At premium price points, unconditional money-back guarantees are refund-risk magnets. Replace with:

1. **Performance / outcome guarantee** (adherence-gated, conditional, defensible)
2. **Format / fit guarantee** (program adjusts to client in early window)
3. **Decision-maker veto guarantee** (spouse / doctor objection → paused deposit)

Stacking 3 narrow > 1 broad. Hormozi: "stack guarantees."

## Scarcity & Urgency

**Scarcity (real):**
- Cohort size cap (tied to coach's actual capacity)
- Application-only sales process
- Retreat / event seat caps

**Urgency (honest only):**
- Founding-cohort pricing with documented trigger to raise (close rate target, active client count, time elapsed)

**What kills trust at premium price points:**
- Fake countdowns
- Limited-time bonus expiration
- "Ends Friday" pressure

## Naming — MAGIC framework

- **M**ake a magnetic reason why
- **A**nnounce your avatar
- **G**ive them a goal
- **I**ndicate a time interval
- **C**omplete with a container word

Example pattern:
> *"The [magnetic-reason] [avatar] [container]: [time interval] to [goal]"*

The container word matters. "Protocol," "Stack," "System," "Method," "Reset" all carry different weights. Match the container to the avatar's worldview.

## Commitment-pricing ladder

| Cadence | Avg churn | LTV multiple |
|---------|----------:|-------------:|
| Monthly | 10.7% | 1× |
| Quarterly | ~5% | ~2.5× |
| Annual | ~2% | 5×+ |

Rule of thumb: 1-month per-month price is 30-50% higher than 12-month equivalent.

Discount tactics for annual commitment:
- 15-25% off equivalent monthly rate
- OR: waived setup fee (only revoked if client cancels early)

---

## The two non-negotiables

1. **Always charge upfront for the first 30 days.** Hormozi: collect money within first 30 days to cover delivery cost + acquisition cost.
2. **Anchor everything to a dollar.** Every perceived-value claim has a retail source. Every price has a derivation.
