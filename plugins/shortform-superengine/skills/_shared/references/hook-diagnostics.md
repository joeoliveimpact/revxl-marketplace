# Competitor Cross-Reference — Hook Diagnostics Reference

## SCOPE NOTE

This skill uses the 4 Hook Killers as a **diagnostic lens** to explain WHY a competitor hook wins or a client hook under-reaches. The goal is analysis, not generation. The generative hook-writing frameworks (BUT/THEREFORE story structure, specificity ladder, hook variant stacking) belong to a future content-creation skill — not this analysis skill. Use what's here to label and explain reach gaps; do not use it to write new hooks.

---

## The 4 Hook Killers

Sourced from: `research/Content Skills Harvest - artemnovitckii - 06.21.26.md` (viral-hooks skill, artemnovitckii/content-skills).

When a client hook under-reaches relative to a comparable competitor hook, assign one (or more) of the four killers as the root cause. Name the killer first; the fix follows.

---

### 1. DELAY
**What it is:** The topic arrives too late. The viewer doesn't know what the video is about until after their attention window has closed.

**Fix:** Delete everything before the topic. The first noun phrase in the hook should carry the subject. If the hook starts with setup, context, or a preamble ("So I've been thinking about this for a while…"), the delay killer applies.

**In competitor analysis:** compare the first 3–5 words of a high-reach competitor hook to the client's hook on the same topic. If the competitor leads with the subject and the client buries it, DELAY is the gap.

---

### 2. CONFUSION
**What it is:** The hook requires a re-read. Dense phrasing, passive voice, or multiple ideas in one sentence forces cognitive effort — the viewer scrolls instead.

**Fix:** Fewer words, simpler words, active voice, one idea per sentence. If reading the hook aloud takes a breath-and-a-half, it's probably CONFUSION.

**In competitor analysis:** a competitor hook that outperforms on the same topic often has measurably fewer words and a simpler sentence structure. Flag the client's hook word count vs the winning competitor's.

---

### 3. IRRELEVANCE
**What it is:** The hook is clear and arrives on time, but the viewer doesn't feel it's for them. The #1 cause: creator-POV "I" framing ("I discovered…", "I was struggling with…") without a fast pivot to the viewer's pain.

**Fix:** Swap me/my → you/your. Frame around a pain the viewer already feels, not an experience the creator had. The viewer is asking "is this for me?" in the first 1–2 seconds — the hook must answer yes.

**In competitor analysis:** check whether high-reach competitor hooks use "you/your" or a named audience pain in the opening phrase. If the client's hooks consistently open with "I" and competitors open with "you" or the pain label, IRRELEVANCE is the gap.

---

### 4. DISINTEREST
**What it is:** The hook is clear, on time, and relevant — but there's no open question. Nothing makes the viewer curious about what comes next. The hook lands flat.

**Fix:** Create an A-vs-B contrast — name the common belief (A) and put your take (B) against it. The contrast can be stated ("Most people do X. Here's why that's wrong.") or implied ("The one thing your doctor isn't checking for thyroid problems."). Either way, a tension must be present.

**In competitor analysis:** high-reach hooks almost always contain an open question or a tension. If a competitor's hook has explicit contrast and the client's does not, DISINTEREST is the gap — even if everything else is technically correct.

## Per-Platform Attention Deadline

The "DELAY" window is not the same on every platform. When diagnosing hooks, use the right deadline for the format being analyzed:

| Platform / Format | Attention deadline | What earns the view |
|---|---|---|
| Instagram / TikTok Reel | First ~2 seconds (spoken + visual) | First phrase + visual stimulus together |
| YouTube | Title + thumbnail earn the click; first 15–30s confirms | Hook confirms the title's promise |
| LinkedIn | 1–2 lines before "…see more" fold | First sentence must be standalone compelling |
| X (Twitter) | First line of the post | No fold, but feed is dense — first line is everything |
| Newsletter | Subject line earns the open | Body hook then confirms within first sentence |
| Carousel (IG / LinkedIn) | Slide 1, ≤9 words | Visual + text together in one glance |

For this skill, the primary format is Reels — the ~2s cliff is the default. Note the platform when analyzing non-Reel hooks.

## Validation note (07.13.26)

The 4 Killers were validated against a **120-reel hand-mapped corpus** as close to a
**pass/fail separator**: killers fired on **9/12 measured losers and 0/12 winners**, and
**23/24 breakout reels ran clean** (no killer present). Treat a firing killer as a strong
disqualifier, not a style note — a hook that trips one is a rewrite, not a tweak. (Corpus
figures are the measured aggregate; per-niche numbers stay in each client's own analysis.)
