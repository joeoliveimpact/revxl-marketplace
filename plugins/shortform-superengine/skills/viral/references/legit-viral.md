# Viral

Take an Instagram Reel script the creator already has and make it travel further, without losing its message or their voice.

## What you return

1. The finished rewrite, laid out as spoken lines with the on-screen text marked beside the line it sits under.
2. A change list. One entry per change: what you changed, why you changed it, and what it should do for the viewer.
3. The share type you aimed at, named once: a DM to a friend or a repost to a story, plus, for a DM, who the viewer would send it to.
4. Three scores out of 10, one each from the target viewer, a skeptic and an editor, with a sentence on each.
5. The one thing to check before recording.

If the creator calls the script final, or asks for suggestions rather than a rewrite, you return proposals only. Pair every proposal with the exact line it would replace and stop there. Never quietly rewrite a script the creator has frozen.

## When NOT to use this

- There is no script yet, only a topic. That is work for `angles`.
- Only the opening is weak. That is work for `hook`.
- The opening is fine and viewers leave in the middle. That is work for `rehooks`.
- The creator wants a soft take made sharper. That is work for `polarize`.
- The request is a written post, a carousel, LinkedIn or X copy. Say this skill is built for Instagram Reels, and adapt only if the creator confirms they want that.
- The request is about a code hook, a webhook or an automation hook. This skill has nothing to do with those. Do not respond as if it does.

Reel captions are out of scope as well. You work on the spoken script and the on-screen text, nothing else.

## Ask the creator for

- The script as it stands.
- The core message in one sentence.
- The goal of the Reel: what a viewer should do or feel.
- Samples of how the audience actually talks, if the creator has any.
- Which share type matters most to them right now.
- Whether the script is final.
- Any claim they want kept, together with the qualifier they attach to it.

Missing pieces are worth asking for before you rewrite. If the creator would rather you proceed, say which assumption you made in place of each answer.

## Method

1. **Lock the brief.** Write down the core message, the voice and the goal, and confirm them with the creator before you touch a line. Everything later is judged against these three.
2. **Diagnose line by line.** Label what each line is doing: opening, setup, proof, turn, payoff, call to action, filler. Make no edits in this step. You are looking for lines that do no job and jobs no line does.
3. **One quality per pass.** A pass is a full read of the script looking for a single thing, in this order: clarity, voice, the "so what", proof, specificity, felt emotion, friction at the call to action. At the end of each pass, re-check the qualities you already passed, since a fix for one often breaks another.
4. **Re-time for speech.** After each pass, read the script at recording pace. Every change lands either in the voiceover or in the on-screen text. Say which, for each change. On-screen hook text is complete and readable at the first frame: two grabbers on screen at 0.0 seconds, not animating in. Later pieces may animate in.
5. **Name the share type.** A Reel sent by DM and a Reel reposted to a story are two different jobs. A save is not a share: it sends the Reel to nobody. Pick one, say it out loud in the change list, and let it settle the close calls. A story repost plays only the first 10 seconds or so, so if that is the target, the reason to repost has to land inside them.
6. **Add share triggers, with one main emotion leading.** A share trigger is the reason a viewer passes the Reel on: a genuinely new insight, an emotional story, something they send to look smart or to help a friend, honest vulnerability, or a feeling they have had but never named. There is no cap on share reasons, but one main emotion leads and the rest support it. Then run the send-to test: name who the viewer would send this to. If you cannot name that person, the trigger is not landing yet. An optional share line can say it out loud, such as "send this to the friend who...", where it fits the creator's voice.
7. **Run the relatability test.** The script has to mirror the viewer's exact problem, in the terms they use, with one human imperfection left in it. This test can fail. If it fails, fix that before anything else, because no amount of polish rescues a script the viewer does not see themselves in. Then a light useful-today check: does the viewer walk away with one thing they can use today? If not, note it in the change list. This check is light: it never fails the script on its own.
8. **Score from three seats.** Read the rewrite as the target viewer, then as a skeptic, then as an editor, and score each out of 10. Keep working until every score is 7 or better and the average is 8 or better. Report the scores. The creator decides whether it ships.
9. **Name the one pre-record check.** One line, the single thing most likely to sink this specific Reel on camera.

## Guardrails

- Never promise something the video does not deliver. A hook the body cannot pay costs trust and follows.
- No manipulation. No fake urgency, no invented scarcity, no bait.
- Do not buy saves at the cost of shares. If a change lifts one and kills the other, say so and let the creator choose.
- Do not copy what went viral for another creator. Their result came with their account, their face and their timing.
- Never invent evidence. No statistics, client results, studies or quotes the creator did not supply. If a line needs support that is missing, ask for it or mark the claim unsupported. Do not fill the gap.
- Earnings, client-result and transformation claims appear exactly as the creator supplied them, with the qualifier they supplied. Nothing supplied means you ask.
- Do not sanitize the creator's word choices. Blunt, funny and crude lines are theirs to keep. Your guardrails limit targets and factual claims, not vocabulary.
- Audience language is sampled for tone and vocabulary only. Never paste a customer's sentences into the script.
- Do not borrow structure from a template built for a written post or a paid ad. Their metrics, their shapes and their calls to action do not carry over to a spoken Reel.
- No em dashes and no en dashes in anything you write. Use a comma, a period or a colon.

## Tests before you deliver

- Does the rewrite still say the core message from step 1, in the creator's voice, aimed at the stated goal?
- Did the relatability test pass, on its own terms, and not by argument?
- Is every claim either supplied by the creator or marked unsupported?
- Can you name the share triggers, the one main emotion that leads, and who the viewer would send it to?
- Does the change list explain every change, with a viewer effect for each?
- Are all three scores 7 or better, with an average of 8 or better?
- Read aloud: does it fit the length the creator is recording to?
- Frozen script: are you returning proposals mapped to exact lines, and no rewrite?

## Close

End every response by naming the one thing the creator should check before recording. One line, specific to this script, never generic advice.
