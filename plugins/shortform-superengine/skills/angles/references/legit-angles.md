# Angles

Turn one Instagram Reel topic, or one concept seen working elsewhere, into several concepts that are genuinely different from each other.

## What you return

A numbered set of concepts. For each one:

1. Who it is for: the audience slice, at the moment they would see it.
2. Why they care: the motivation it hooks into.
3. The approach: the angle in one sentence, plus the awareness level it speaks to.
4. The format: how it is shot and structured as a Reel.
5. The evidence level it rests on, named from the ranking in step 4 of the method.

Then: any pairs you merged, and why. Any concepts that belong together as a series, grouped, offered as a series only once one Reel in it has worked. And the one thing to check before recording.

## When NOT to use this

- A script already exists and needs polishing. That is work for `viral`.
- A script exists and only the opening is weak. That is work for `hook`.
- A script exists and viewers leave mid-Reel. That is work for `rehooks`.
- The creator holds a stance and wants it stated with conviction. That is work for `polarize`.
- The request is a written post, a carousel, LinkedIn or X copy. Say this skill is built for Instagram Reels, and adapt only if the creator confirms.
- The request is about a code hook, a webhook or an automation hook. Nothing here applies.

Reel captions are out of scope. You work on the spoken script and the on-screen text only.

## Ask the creator for

- The topic, or the concept they want to adapt.
- Their audience slices: who they serve, split into groups that want different things.
- Their own winners: Reels of theirs that worked, with whatever numbers they have.
- Words their audience actually uses, from calls, DMs or comments.
- Competitor outliers: Reels with far more views than that creator's own average. That outlier score, views against that creator's own average, is the tell that it works.
- Their back catalogue, so you do not hand back something they already posted.
- How many concepts they want.

## Method

1. **Define an angle properly.** An angle is a different reason to care, tied to a different motivation. It is never a different sentence about the same reason. If two concepts would be shot the same way and land on the same person for the same reason, you have one angle written twice.
2. **Pull from the seed categories.** Use them as prompts, not as a checklist: pain, outcome, proof, curiosity, comparison, urgency, identity, contrarian, story arc, format-as-angle, naming the unnamed, data, case study, behind the scenes.
3. **Build each concept as four parts plus evidence.** Slice, motivation, angle, format, and the evidence it rests on. Pick the awareness level it addresses: unaware, problem aware, solution aware, product aware. A concept aimed at an unaware viewer and one aimed at a solution-aware viewer are different concepts even on the same angle.
4. **Rank the evidence and say where each concept sits.** Strongest first: the creator's own winners, then words their audience used, then a competitor's outlier, a Reel far above that creator's own average views, then patterns across the niche's organic content, then patterns from an adjacent niche, then a hunch. A hunch is allowed. Calling a hunch anything else is not.
5. **Work in waves.** First a core set across the slices. Then extend the best two into variants worth shooting. Then a few wild cards. Dedupe inside the set, and against the back catalogue.
6. **Run the distinctness test on every pair.** Two concepts are different if their premise or take differs, or if their execution differs. Different wording alone never counts. Answer yes or no for each pair. Merge or replace every failure, and report which ones you merged.
7. **Adapting something viral, borrow the structure, never the script.** The format and the order of its beats can travel. Change the angle and write every word fresh. Copying the script gives the creator someone else's video with their face in it.
8. **Judge concepts, not single videos.** Ask whether the idea could carry five Reels, not whether one clip would do well. Where a topic supports a run, offer it as a series only after one Reel in it has worked, and say what the order does. If none has been posted yet, deliver each as a standalone Reel and name the one to post first.
9. **Be honest about thin evidence.** On a small account, the creator's own winners are not a reliable signal. Say the evidence is thin, say what you leaned on instead, and do not dress a hunch up as a pattern.
10. **Name the one pre-record check.**

## Guardrails

- Never invent data, case studies, client results or quotes. If a concept needs proof the creator has not supplied, ask for it or mark the concept as unsupported and let them decide.
- Earnings, client-result and transformation claims appear only as the creator supplied them, with the supplied qualifier. Nothing supplied means you ask.
- Audience words shape tone and word choice by default. When a phrase from the creator or their audience is gold, strong enough to work as it is, keep it word for word.
- Do not copy another creator's script. Borrow the structure, never the script.
- Do not pad the set to hit a number. Four real concepts beat ten with six rewordings in them.
- Do not sanitize the creator's word choices. Blunt, funny and crude framing is theirs to keep.
- Do not carry over structures from templates built for written posts or paid ads.
- No em dashes and no en dashes. Use a comma, a period or a colon.

## Tests before you deliver

- Every pair passes the distinctness test: a different premise or take, or a different execution, not different wording.
- Every concept carries all five tags, including its evidence level.
- Nothing in the set repeats something already in the back catalogue.
- Every concept is shootable as a talking-head Reel by one person with a phone.
- Every piece of evidence named is something the creator supplied or something you can point at, not something you produced.
- Where the account is small, the thin-evidence note is present and plain.
- Series are grouped, with a reason for the order, and offered only once one Reel in them has worked.
- The count matches what the creator asked for, or you say why it is lower.

## Close

End every response by naming the one thing the creator should check before recording. One line, specific to the concept they are most likely to shoot first.
