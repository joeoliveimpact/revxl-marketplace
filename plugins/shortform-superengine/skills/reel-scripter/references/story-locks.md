# Story Locks — the word-level craft-scoring rubric

The **6 Story Locks + 7 Swaps**: a line-level toolkit for tightening any reel script
*without changing the topic*. Each lock is a small, mechanical edit that closes an "exit
door" — a place the viewer's attention can leak out. Niche-agnostic. Placeholders:
`[pain]`, `[outcome]`, `[mechanism]`, `[topic]`, `[belief]`, `[audience]`, `[number]`.

> **Scoring lens.** This file is the rubric *behind* the Hook / Body / CTA craft scores. The
> reel-scripter scores **craft quality, not view prediction** — it cannot know if a reel will
> go viral, but it CAN measure whether the writing lands these proven moves. A script scores
> higher the more locks it hits and the more swaps it applies; a flat, hedge-filled, contrast-free
> script scores low. Think of it as "how many exit doors did this script close." Cross-reference
> `_shared/references/hook-diagnostics.md` (the 4 Hook Killers) — every lock fixes at least one.

---

## The two non-negotiables (if you only do two things)
1. **Contrast** — split your big claims A→B with a contrast word ("but" is king). Contrast is
   the engine under every other lock; conflict holds attention.
2. **Kill the hedges** — every "might / I think / kind of / maybe" is a door of doubt. Delete or
   harden it. Certainty earns the next second.

---

## The 6 Story Locks

### 1. Term-Branding
**What it is:** Give your best framework, idea, or mistake a *name*. A named concept triggers the
labeling effect — anticipation becomes "mental quicksand," the viewer can't leave until you
explain what the term means.
**Swap tactic:** Find the one idea worth keeping and brand it. Generic noun → proprietary label.
**Before → After:**
- ❌ "Here's a way to think about [topic]."
- ✅ "I call this the **[Memorable Name] [Method/Loop/Trap]** — and once you see it, you can't unsee it."
**Fixes:** DISINTEREST (a named thing opens a loop the unnamed version doesn't).

### 2. Embedded-Truths (kill the hedges)
**What it is:** Hedging language ("if," "maybe," "might," "I think," "hopefully") signals doubt —
each one is an exit door. State the claim as a fact the viewer can lean on.
**Swap tactic:** `if → when` · `might work → the reason this works` · `I think → I found` ·
delete `just / kind of / sort of / hopefully` on sight.
**Before → After:**
- ❌ "This *might* help you [outcome], I think, if you stick with it."
- ✅ "This is *why* you'll finally [outcome] — once you do [mechanism]."
**Fixes:** DISINTEREST (flat, low-stakes) and CONFUSION (hedges add noise around the point).

### 3. Thought-Narration
**What it is:** Say the viewer's inner monologue out loud. Naming the objection they're already
thinking buys trust and feels personal — at scale.
**Swap tactic:** Right after a big claim, voice the doubt, then answer it.
**Before → After:**
- ❌ "[Claim]. Anyway, moving on…"
- ✅ "[Claim]. Right now you're thinking *'that won't work for [my situation]'* — here's why it does."
**Fixes:** IRRELEVANCE (it pivots straight to the viewer's head) and DISINTEREST (re-opens tension).

### 4. Negative-Frames
**What it is:** Loss framing pulls roughly twice as hard as gain framing. "Don't / stop / avoid /
worst / costing you" outperform their positive twins.
**Swap tactic:** *Negative-flip* any positive tip — take the advice and state the cost of NOT doing it.
**Before → After:**
- ❌ "Do [right action] to get [outcome]."
- ✅ "The **worst** thing you can do for [outcome] is [common action] — and you're probably doing it."
**Fixes:** DISINTEREST (loss aversion injects stakes a neutral tip lacks).

### 5. Loop-Openers (re-hook cadence)
**What it is:** A reel isn't one hook — it's a chain of them. Re-flip the attention "hourglass"
before it empties by opening a fresh micro-loop, led by a contrast word.
**Swap tactic:** Between beats, insert a re-hook: "but here's the part nobody talks about," "this is
where it gets weird," "and that's not even the [mechanism]." **Cadence: roughly every 20–30s for
short-form.** A 4–5 beat reel wants 2–3 loop openers.
**Before → After:**
- ❌ "[Point 1]. [Point 2]. [Point 3]." (flat list, no re-hooks)
- ✅ "[Point 1]. **But** here's what surprised me — [Point 2]. And **that's** the part that changes [outcome]."
**Fixes:** DELAY in reverse (re-earns the *next* window) and DISINTEREST (keeps a loop open at all times).

### 6. Contrast-Words ("but" is king)
**What it is:** Curiosity = the gap between expectation and claim. Contrast IS that gap. The single
highest-leverage word in short-form is **but**; close behind: *actually, instead, turns out, except, yet.*
**Swap tactic:** Take a big sentence, lean it hard into A, then flip to B with a contrast word.
**Before → After:**
- ❌ "Most people [do X] and that's fine and it sort of works."
- ✅ "Everyone [does X]. **But** the opposite is what actually [drives outcome]."
**Fixes:** DISINTEREST (manufactures the open question) and CONFUSION (a clean A→B is easier to follow than a hedged middle).

## The 7 Swaps (the quick-edit pass)
Run this as a final sweep over any drafted script. Each maps to a lock above.

| # | Swap | Do this | Lock |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Name it | Brand your framework / mistake / idea | Term-Branding |
| 2 | Kill weak language | `I think→I found`, `might→will`, delete `just/kind of/sort of/maybe/hopefully` | Embedded-Truths |
| 3 | Use THEIR words | Swap insider jargon for the audience's own phrasing (beats the curse of knowledge) | (clarity) |
| 4 | Go negative | `don't / stop / avoid / lose / costing you` over the positive version | Negative-Frames |
| 5 | Hooks throughout | Drop a re-hook every 20–30s ("here's what everyone misses") | Loop-Openers |
| 6 | More contrast | Add `but / instead / turns out` to introduce conflict | Contrast-Words |
| 7 | Narrate the doubt | Voice their objection, then answer it | Thought-Narration |

## How this scores (craft, not virality)
- **Hook score** rewards: a contrast/open-loop in the first line, zero hedges, subject-first
  (no DELAY), "you"-framing (no IRRELEVANCE). A bare, hedged, contrast-free hook scores low.
- **Body score** rewards: ≥4 of the 6 locks present, a loop-opener at the right cadence, near-zero
  hedge words, and most big claims split A→B.
- **CTA score** rewards: one clear ask stated without hedging (see `cta-scaffolds.md`) — "comment X
  *to get*…" beats "maybe let me know your thoughts."

> **The one rule to remember: contrast.** "But" introduces conflict; conflict holds attention.
> If a script lands nothing else, make sure its haymakers are split A→B.
