# Story Locks — the word-level craft-scoring rubric

The **4 Story Locks + 6 Swaps**: a line-level toolkit for tightening any reel script
*without changing the topic*. Each lock is a small, mechanical edit that closes an "exit
door" — a place the viewer's attention can leak out. Niche-agnostic. Placeholders:
`[pain]`, `[outcome]`, `[mechanism]`, `[topic]`, `[belief]`, `[audience]`, `[number]`.

> **Scoring lens.** This file is the rubric *behind* the Hook / Body / CTA craft scores. The
> reel-scripter scores **craft quality, not view prediction** — it cannot know if a reel will
> go viral, but it CAN measure whether the writing lands these proven moves. A script scores
> higher the more locks it hits and the more swaps it applies; a flat, hedge-filled, contrast-free
> script scores low. Think of it as "how many exit doors did this script close." Cross-reference
> `_shared/references/hook-diagnostics.md` (the 4 Hook Killers) — every lock fixes at least one.

---

## The two non-negotiables (if you only do two things)
1. **Contrast** (a scoring name here; the contrast-word method lives in `viral`).
2. **Kill the hedges** — every "might / I think / kind of / maybe" is a door of doubt. Delete or
   harden it. Certainty earns the next second. Harden the stance, never the facts: no number,
   result or finding the creator did not give.

---

## The 4 Story Locks

### 1. Embedded-Truths (kill the hedges)
**What it is:** Hedging language ("if," "maybe," "might," "I think," "hopefully") signals doubt —
each one is an exit door. State the claim as a fact the viewer can lean on.
**Swap tactic:** `if → when` · `might work → the reason this works` · `I think X → X` (`I found` only when found) ·
delete `just / kind of / sort of / hopefully` on sight.
**Before → After:**
- ❌ "This *might* help you [outcome], I think, if you stick with it."
- ✅ "This is *why* you'll finally [outcome] — once you do [mechanism]."
**Fixes:** DISINTEREST (flat, low-stakes) and CONFUSION (hedges add noise around the point).

### 2. Negative-Frames
**What it is:** Loss framing: "don't / stop / avoid / worst / costing you". It adds stakes when
the content is a warning or a cost. Use it by content, never by count.
**Swap tactic:** Where the content is a warning or a cost, *negative-flip* the tip: state the
cost of NOT doing it. No quota: a tip that is not a warning stays positive.
**Before → After:**
- ❌ "Do [right action] to get [outcome]."
- ✅ "The **worst** thing you can do for [outcome] is [common action] — and you're probably doing it."
**Fixes:** DISINTEREST (loss aversion injects stakes a neutral tip lacks).

### 3. Loop-Openers (re-hook cadence)
**What it is:** A reel isn't one hook — it's a chain of them. Re-flip the attention "hourglass"
before it empties by opening a fresh micro-loop, led by a contrast word.
**Swap tactic:** Score whether a re-hook sits at each slot `rehooks` set at Step 2. Its lines are
rehooks' own (Step 3): flag a missing or weak one, never write one here.
**Before → After:**
- ❌ "[Point 1]. [Point 2]. [Point 3]." (flat list, no re-hooks)
- ✅ "[Point 1]. **But** here's what surprised me — [Point 2]. And **that's** the part that changes [outcome]."
**Fixes:** DELAY in reverse (re-earns the *next* window) and DISINTEREST (keeps a loop open at all times).

### 4. Contrast-Words
Scoring name only: are the big claims split A→B with a contrast word? The method lives in `viral`.

## The 6 Swaps (the quick-edit pass)
Run this as a final sweep over the drafted script. Each maps to a lock above. A line another
skill wrote (hook, rehooks, polarize, viral) is flagged for the user, never rewritten here
(the rule for every pass after the picks: `SKILL.md` Step 3).

| # | Swap | Do this | Lock |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Kill weak language | `I think X→X` (`I found` only when found), `might→will`, delete `just/kind of/sort of/maybe/hopefully` | Embedded-Truths |
| 2 | Use THEIR words | Swap insider jargon for the audience's own phrasing (beats the curse of knowledge) | (clarity) |
| 3 | Go negative | `don't / stop / avoid / lose / costing you` where the content is a warning or a cost; no quota | Negative-Frames |
| 4 | Hooks throughout | A re-hook at each slot `rehooks` set at Step 2; flag a missing one | Loop-Openers |
| 5 | More contrast | Scoring name only; the method lives in `viral` | Contrast-Words |
| 6 | Second person | Scoring name only; the method lives in `viral` | Viewer-as-Subject |

## How this scores (craft, not virality)
- **Hook score** rewards: a contrast/open-loop in the first line, zero hedges, subject-first
  (no DELAY), "you"-framing (no IRRELEVANCE). A bare, hedged, contrast-free hook scores low.
- **Body score** rewards: the locks that fit this content present, a re-hook at each slot
  rehooks set at Step 2, near-zero hedge words, and most big claims split A→B.
- **CTA score** rewards: one clear ask stated without hedging (see `cta-scaffolds.md`) — "comment X
  *to get*…" beats "maybe let me know your thoughts."

**Two instruments, no conversion.** Step 3 scores each *option* 1 to 10 at the gate; Step 4d
scores the assembled *draft* 0 to 100 (Hook / Body / CTA / overall). Both read this rubric, but
they are separate instruments with no conversion between them: a 7 is not a 70, a 70 is not a 7,
and no option score carries into the draft score.

**Option score anchors (Step 3, 1 to 10; the gate defaults to 7).** Score against the section's
own dimensions (`step3-options.md` pass 3) and these anchors:
- **9** ... every dimension lands in full and there is nothing to fix. A 9 hook: one subject and
  one question a stranger names after a single read, contrast or an open loop in the first line,
  spoken to "you", zero hedges, no Hook Killer.
- **7** ... clears the bar. Nothing is missing and no Hook Killer fires, but one dimension is soft
  and a single swap from the 6 Swaps lifts it. A 7 hook: clear subject and question, but the
  contrast is only implied, or one hedge word is left to cut.
- **6 or below** ... withheld. Any one of these: a Hook Killer fires (DELAY, CONFUSION,
  IRRELEVANCE, DISINTEREST); a dimension is missing outright (two subjects, no question planted,
  two asks); or it needs two or more swaps. A firing Hook Killer caps the option at 6 however
  strong the rest is; if the redo still trips a killer, it scores 6 or below.
- **8** sits between a 7 and a 9; keep **10** for an option you would not change a word of.

> **The one rule to remember: contrast.** "But" introduces conflict; conflict holds attention.
> If a script lands nothing else, make sure its haymakers are split A→B.
