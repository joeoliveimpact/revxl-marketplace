# Retention Psychology — the content *inside* the beats

Beats are the skeleton (`body-structures.md`). This file is the **psychology that lives inside
them** — the loops, payoffs, and connective tissue that decide whether a viewer rides one beat
into the next or bails. `story-locks.md` is the *line-level* pass (contrast, hedges, one re-hook
swap); this is the *system* those line-edits serve. Distilled from the Secondary-Hooks,
After-the-Hook, and Under-the-Radar sources + the measured corpus behind this engine's
reference library.

> The reel is not one hook. It is a **chain of curiosity**: pop a question in the viewer's head,
> answer it, pop a new one — all the way down. Break the chain anywhere and they scroll.

---

## 1. The Spine — the connective tissue between beats

Every beat must **inherit the question the last beat opened, resolve it, and open the next.** The
throughline *is* the retention mechanism. Churn happens when a beat doesn't connect to the one
before it — the viewer can't follow the story and leaves (same root cause as a fragmented hook:
confusion = churn).

- **Transition/bridge move:** each beat's opening job is to carry the viewer from the emotional
  state the previous beat left them in, into the next hit of value. The bridge "confirms" the
  promise the prior beat made.
- **Test:** read the script beat-to-beat and ask at each seam — *does this line answer the question
  the last line planted, and plant the next?* A seam with no open question is a scroll point.

---

## 2. Curiosity = Contrast → Open Loop

Dopamine (the thing that overrides the scroll) is released by two triggers: **emotional response**
and **curiosity loops** — open questions that become "a maze in the brain."

**How to open a loop:** contrast against a belief the viewer already holds. Baseline reality vs. a
proposed new reality. *All curiosity is rooted in contrast — the bigger the contrast, the more
curious they get.* (This is why "but / actually / turns out / the opposite" are the highest-leverage
words — see `story-locks.md` §Contrast.)

**The leash (sustain → close):** once curious, you have a limited window to frame the argument
before the loop goes stale. **The more outlandish the claim, the shorter the leash** — pay off a
big-contrast loop fast. Every open loop must eventually **close with a real payoff** (raw curiosity
in the hook, genuine resolution by the end). The mechanism is the **Zeigarnik effect** — the brain
holds an open loop as an unfinished task and won't let go until it resolves.

---

## 3. The Nested Loop System + Partial Payoffs

Loops run at three scales, resolving at different points:

| Loop | Scope | Resolves |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | The reel's main promise/question (set by the hook) | At the end — the payoff the whole reel earns |
| **Secondary** | A supporting point or sub-question | Mid-reel, one beat later |
| **Micro** | Sentence-level curiosity ("but here's the part nobody mentions…") | Next sentence |

**Partial payoff = progressive revelation.** Reveal information in stages: give a real piece of the
answer, then use it to open the next loop. The pattern is **Teaser → knowledge gap → progressive
reveal → satisfaction *with the next step already opened*.** The viewer always just learned
something real AND is now curious about the next thing.

**Anticipation is the strongest form of the partial payoff:** reveal details or tease conditions
that let the viewer *predict something valuable is coming* — the more concretely they can
anticipate it, the longer they hold. Anticipation paid off with a **non-obvious, contrasting
reveal** is the single most powerful retention move in the corpus.

> **The one rule that keeps this honest:** never stack teases back-to-back with no payoff between
> them. Each secondary hook must sit on top of value already delivered — the viewer should have
> *learned something* by that point, not just collected another promise. Empty tease → churn.

**Hold the primary payoff to the END — revealing it early is the #1 retention killer.** The
primary loop (the answer the hook promised) must close at the *finish*, not the middle. Reveal the
punchline halfway and the viewer feels they already got what they came for — curiosity collapses,
watch-time dies, and the algorithm stops pushing it. So in the hook, **tease the payoff you'll pay
off LAST**, never one you'll resolve early. For a numbered/countdown reel this means the strongest
item goes **last** and the hook teases *that* one (`body-structures.md` numbered/list Skeleton B:
"save the best… #N strongest, slow down") — never tease #1 and cash it in at second 10. Caveat:
if early retention is bleeding, front-load one surprising item to survive the open, but keep a
distinct final payoff so the end still closes a loop.

## 4. Secondary Hooks — mechanics, placement, dosage

Secondary hooks are **scripted re-engagement moments** that close one gap while opening the next,
placed at natural retention drop-off points. Think of them as "the beads that keep the momentum" —
something meaningful lands every second so the viewer never hits a dead spot.

**The hook is a sequence, not a moment.** Top performers layer 4–5 micro re-hooks inside the first
~7 seconds — the primary hook buys the first 2–3 seconds, then small meaningful shifts keep buying
the next one. The placement table below governs the *big* re-engagement points; this early density
governs the open.

**Placement (the 12–15s window is the most critical re-engagement point at every length — put your
strongest secondary hook there):**

| Reel length | Secondary hooks |
|---|---|
| 30s | 1 (~12–15s mark) |
| 45s | 1–2 |
| 60s | 2–3 |
| 90s | 3–4 |

**Dosage warning — more is not better.** A million cuts, zooms, effects, and text spam is *noise*,
not pacing. Layer a *few* deliberate secondary hooks over a consistent rhythm the viewer can
intuitively follow. Build a "pacing identity," not chaos.

> **Critical distinction:** a secondary hook is a **scripted content moment** (a verbal or on-screen
> line that shifts attention / reveals partial info / opens a new gap) — NOT a visual pattern
> interrupt (camera cut, zoom, text animation). Those are editing decisions. The script owns the
> content hooks; the edit owns the visual interrupts. Don't confuse the two.

## 5. The Dopamine Ladder (the retention model)

Retention is a staircase of increasing dopamine hits — a six-level ladder; ascend it and the viewer
watches to the end *and* comes back.

- **Rung 1 (visual):** raw stimulation — color, motion, brightness — earns the split-second that lets
  the hook play. (This is why scroll-stopping visuals matter, `hook-mastery.md` §Mistake 3.)
- **Rungs up:** each beat must pay a *bigger* hit than the last — escalating contrast, stakes, or
  reveal. A flat middle stalls the climb and the viewer drops.
- **Endgame — the Pavlovian effect:** enough satisfying climbs and the viewer feels the dopamine
  *just from seeing your name or face*, before they've watched anything. That's a following.

## 6. After the Hook — the 3-part bridge

The stretch right after hook + problem-identification is where most reels lose the viewer (the
~15s cliff). Bridge it in three moves:

1. **Transformation tease** — show a brief glimpse of the *outcome* before explaining the process
   (opens a dopamine-driven anticipation loop).
2. **Hope injection** — a micro-win or perspective shift that gives immediate relief ("this is
   simpler than you think").
3. **Pattern interrupt every ~15–20s** — a scripted shift that resets attention and forces the brain
   from autopilot (System 1) back to deliberate focus (System 2). Must feel organic, not arbitrary.

Place an **insight moment at the 12–15s mark** — the point where viewers decide to keep watching or
share. Insight moments that respectfully challenge an industry assumption drive the most shares.

## 7. Three proven post-hook structures

Body skeletons in `body-structures.md` are organized by hook *bucket*; these three are organized by
*intent* and carry validated beat-timings. Pick by the reel's goal, then fill with the loop/payoff
mechanics above.

| Structure | Best for | Beat timing |
|---|---|---|
| **Transformation Arc** | Conversion (highest) | Shared-struggle hook (0–5s) → personal journey (5–15s) → breakthrough (15–20s) → application framework (20–35s) → community CTA (35–45s). Stories are remembered far better than facts. |
| **Myth-Buster** | Shares | Controversial claim (0–3s) → why the common belief is wrong (3–12s) → proof/insight moment (12–15s) → alternative approach (15–25s) → steps (25–35s) → engagement prompt. Manufactures the cognitive dissonance that drives sharing. |
| **Authority Solution** | Trust / high-ticket | Problem (0–3s) → brief credibility (3–8s) → live demo / case study (8–20s) → 3-step framework (20–30s) → results preview (30–35s) → next-step CTA. Lower conversion, builds long-term authority. |

## 8. Loop design (the completion trick)

A reel that **ends where it begins** — same visual/line as the open, now recontextualized — sees far
higher completion (a "127% higher completion" corpus figure), which the algorithm reads as quality
and pushes wider. Close the primary loop by returning to the opening image with the payoff attached.

---

## 9. Repeaters + register (light layer)

- **Repeaters:** say your most valuable point **twice** — once tight (with the term if needed), then
  again "stupid-simple" with a metaphor or example. This is a *comprehension* device (repeat the
  concept two ways), not a filler catchphrase.
- **Register:** 6th-grade-or-below word choice; active voice; mostly staccato (short) sentences with
  a varied "trance rhythm"; emotionally-weighted trigger words (fear/shock drive the most shares).
  Line-level execution lives in `say-this-not-that.md` and `story-locks.md`.

---

## Guardrails
- **Deliver on every loop.** A curiosity gap you open and never pay off reads as clickbait and burns
  trust. Open only loops the body can close.
- **Value before the next tease** — §3's honesty rule. No empty back-to-back teases.
- **Secondary hooks are script, not edit** — §4. Don't score editing decisions as content.
- **Craft, not promises** — these mechanics raise craft quality; they don't predict views. The
  reel-scripter never forecasts virality (consistent with the rest of the product).
