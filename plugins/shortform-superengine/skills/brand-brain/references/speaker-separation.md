# Speaker Separation — who said it decides where it goes

Every call yields THREE extractions, split by speaker role:

| Turns | Feed | Always useful? |
|-------|------|----------------|
| Prospect/client | VoC (pains, desires, objections, verbatim language) | YES — regardless of who ran the call |
| Brand owner | Voice guide (cadence, vocabulary, signature phrases, stance) | only if the brand owner actually ran the call |
| Brand owner | Story + humor seeds (stories, analogies, bits they already say) | same condition |

## Staff/setter-run calls
Mine the prospect side for VoC as normal. **Discard the staff side for voice** — it is not the brand owner's voice. Tag each VoC insight's source so staff-call data feeds ideal-client copy without polluting the voice guide.

## Unreliable diarization (Fathom mashes turns)
Transcript speaker labels are NOT trustworthy — Fathom and similar tools sometimes mash both speakers into one labeled turn. Before extracting, sanity-check labels against content (who is coaching whom, who owns the business being discussed). Where a labeled turn plainly contains both voices, reassign by content and CORROBORATE (the reassigned line must fit that speaker's established context elsewhere in the call). Flag every reassignment inline in the artifact's evidence ref. Uncorroborated ambiguous lines: discard for voice, keep for VoC only if speaker-irrelevant.

## Auto-detect brand owner vs staff
Compare the brand-owner-side speaker against the known voice baseline + offer context. If sales calls aren't in the brand owner's voice, flag it:
> "These look like your setter's calls, not yours. I'll use them for prospect data, not your voice. Want to make sure we're capturing your own calls too?"

Offer to wire that source in.

## Attribution guard (hard rule)
Every extracted line is tagged with WHO said it, and only ever feeds THAT person's artifacts. A brand owner's signature bit must never land in a client's brain; a client's phrasing never merges into another brand. When mining a call where the skill's operator (e.g. the agency) talks to the client whose brain is being built: the CLIENT is the brand-owner side here — the operator's turns are staff-side, discard for voice.

## Bucket mapping (why buckets matter downstream)
- **sales/strategy calls** → richest PRE-purchase pains + objections + desired outcomes → feed precall, launch, warm-nurture, post-call, no-show campaigns. DM export lands here.
- **client/1:1 calls** → retention language, wins, what they value → feed onboarding, winback.
- **group calls** → recurring themes + community language → feed warm-nurture, launch proof.
Tag every insight with its bucket so consumers pull the right evidence per campaign.
