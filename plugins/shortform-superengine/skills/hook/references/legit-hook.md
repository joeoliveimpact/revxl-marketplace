# Hook

Write the first two seconds of an Instagram Reel as a set of genuinely different openings, each one complete enough to record.

## What you return

Three to five openings that differ in approach, not in wording. For each one:

1. The spoken line, as it will be said on camera.
2. The on-screen text: two grabbers, each short enough to read in about a second, complete at the first frame.
3. The visual: what the camera sees at 0.0 seconds.
4. One line of rationale: who this stops, and why.

Then mark one option as the primary and one as the alternative, built on a different framework from the primary. If you diagnosed an existing opening, say which basis the diagnosis rested on: the creator's own Instagram insights, or a read-aloud against the rules in this file. Close by naming the one thing to check before recording.

**Frozen script.** If the creator says the script is final, or asks for suggestions rather than a rewrite, return proposals only, each anchored to the exact existing line it would replace or follow, quoted. Never rewrite the opening or the body lines. This overrides every rewrite, move and swap instruction in this file.

## When NOT to use this

- Viewers watch the opening and leave in the middle. The opening is not the problem. That is work for `rehooks`.
- The whole script needs work, not just the front. That is work for `viral`.
- The topic itself is undecided. That is work for `angles`.
- The request is a written post, a carousel, LinkedIn or X copy. Say this skill is built for Instagram Reels, and adapt only if the creator confirms.
- The request is about a code hook, a webhook, a lifecycle hook or an automation hook. Those have nothing to do with this skill. Do not respond as if they do.

Reel captions are out of scope. You work on the spoken script and the on-screen text only.

## Ask the creator for

- The topic of the Reel.
- The audience slice: not "coaches", but which coaches, at which moment.
- The goal metric: stop the scroll, hold attention, or earn shares.
- The current opening, if there is one.
- Their own Instagram insights for that Reel, if they have any: plays, 3-second views, average watch time, the retention graph.
- What is saturating their niche right now.
- The script body, if it exists, so the opening and the body can be built to fit.

## Method

0. **Check whether the script is frozen.** If the creator says the script is final, or asks for suggestions rather than a rewrite, return proposals only, each anchored to the exact existing line it would replace or follow, quoted. Never rewrite the opening or the body lines. This overrides every rewrite, move and swap instruction below.
1. **Three parts, three jobs.** An opening is a visual, a spoken line and a line of on-screen text. The on-screen text carries clarity and the spoken line can carry the curiosity. The text may repeat the spoken line word for word only as Written hook below allows.
2. **Work top-down.** Pick the audience slice, then one pain, desire or objection, then the format, then write the three parts. Openings written the other way round tend to be clever about nothing in particular.
3. **Match the family to the goal.** An opening built to stop the scroll is not the one built to hold attention, and neither is the one built to earn a share. Pick the family that matches the creator's stated metric and say which you picked.
4. **Spread the options on a grid.** The grid is audience slice by motivation by format. Put one idea in each cell you use. Five rewordings of one idea is one option, not five.
5. **Tension inside about two seconds.** Something must be unresolved almost immediately. Check the width of that gap: too small and the opening gives the answer away, too wide and it turns vague. Run the clarity test: a viewer can tell who this is for, and it raises one question, not several. The surest way to pass it: ask out loud the exact question you want the viewer to hold, so the subject and the question land in one line. Every Reel stands alone: write for a viewer who has never seen the creator's other Reels, so the opening needs no earlier context. No brand mention in the opening. On-screen text is complete and readable at the first frame: two grabbers on screen at 0.0 seconds, not animating in. Secondary pieces may animate in later, such as the supporting line in Written hook.
6. **Diagnose an existing opening as a stop problem or a hold problem.** A stop problem means people scroll past. A hold problem means they stop, then leave. Then, only if the script is not frozen, rewrite the on-ramp with the opening, meaning the first few lines of the body too, not just the first sentence. If it is frozen, propose instead, per step 0. Diagnose on organic numbers only, never on paid-ad metrics, which measure a different thing in a different feed.
   - With the creator's Instagram insights in hand: read plays against 3-second views for the stop rate, then average watch time and the retention graph for the hold.
   - With no data: read the opening aloud and judge it against the two-second tension rule and the frame-zero text rule.
   - Say which of the two bases you used. Do not present a read-aloud judgement as if it were data.
7. **Check coach-niche fatigue.** If the pattern is everywhere in this niche this month, the viewer has already learned to scroll past it. Say so plainly and offer an opening from another family. The same goes for the visual. Work out what Reels in this niche nearly always look like, then open on something that looks different: a shot, a setting or a layout the viewer does not expect here, or a face or figure the audience already knows, in frame. Looking different is what makes people stop; extra movement or busy editing does not.
8. **Deliver primary plus alternative.** The primary is your recommendation. The alternative sits on a different framework, so the creator has a real choice rather than a second draft. Give the reasoning for each.
9. **Name the one pre-record check.**

## Written hook

The on-screen text: the words on screen in the first frames, separate from the captions. Viewers' eyes hunt for text first, so this is often what decides the stop.

1. Its job is clarity: who this is for and what it is about, in the audience's own trigger word ("make your rental look expensive"). The spoken line can carry the curiosity.
2. Substance: the subject, plus one of a pain point, a dream outcome, or a change in their future. Specific numbers beat round ones.
3. It may repeat the spoken line word for word, as long as it does not look like captions. Plain-font text stays separate from the captions; only styled, premium text can be the word-for-word line.
4. Two lines at most in plain font, broken where the phrase reads naturally. Three lines only when one is big and the others are small accents.
5. Readable from the first frame and on screen for about three seconds. The first line lands at frame one; a supporting line may build in after it.
6. Place it top or middle, wherever it does not collide with the face or the visual. Readability beats style.

## Adapting a proven hook

When the creator brings a hook that already worked, from a competitor or a swipe list, plus their own topic, adapt it rather than starting from the grid. The result still comes back as the options in What you return.

1. **Check the proof.** Confirm the hook actually worked, on numbers the creator can point at. With no proof, say so and treat it as a hunch.
2. **Name the skeleton and the trigger.** The skeleton is the shape of the line, what it does word by word. The trigger is the reason it stops people. Write both down.
3. **Keep the skeleton and the trigger, swap the rest.** Swap in the creator's subject, their audience's words and their own numbers. Numbers come only from the creator.
4. **Refuse a bad fit.** If the skeleton cannot carry the creator's topic without bending the truth or losing the trigger, say so and write from the grid instead.
5. **Never keep the original wording.** If a phrase from the source survives, rewrite it.
6. **Rebuild the visual and the on-screen text.** Do not reuse the source's shot or text. Build both for the creator's topic, per Written hook.

## Guardrails

- No invented numbers in any opening. No statistic, client result, study or quote the creator did not supply. If an opening wants a number the creator does not have, ask for it or drop the number.
- Earnings, client-result and transformation claims appear only as supplied, with the supplied qualifier. Nothing supplied means you ask.
- No brand or handle mention inside the opening.
- No first-frame text that animates in. If it is not readable at 0.0 seconds, it is not doing the job.
- No single idea reworded into five options. Options differ in approach.
- Do not sanitize the creator's word choices. Blunt, funny and crude lines stay. Your guardrails limit targets and factual claims, not vocabulary.
- Audience language shapes tone and word choice by default. When a phrase from the creator or their audience is gold, strong enough to work as it is, keep it word for word.
- Do not lift an opening from a template built for a written post or a paid ad. Their structures and their calls to action do not carry over to a spoken Reel.
- No em dashes and no en dashes. Use a comma, a period or a colon.

## Tests before you deliver

- Do the options differ in approach, or only in wording? Name the difference for each pair. Merge anything that fails.
- Can the on-screen text be read in about a second, and is it complete at the first frame?
- Is there tension inside two seconds, said out loud at recording pace?
- Is the gap the right width: not so small it gives the answer away, not so wide it turns vague?
- Clarity test: can a viewer tell who this is for, and name the one question it raises?
- Does each of the three parts do its job, and if the text repeats the spoken line, is it styled so it does not look like captions?
- Does each opening lead into a body the creator can actually deliver, rather than promising something they cannot pay off?
- Is every number in the set one the creator supplied?
- Is the primary marked, and is the alternative on a different framework?
- On a diagnosis, did you name the basis you used?

## Close

End every response by naming the one thing the creator should check before recording. One line, specific to this opening, never generic advice.
